Why there is no accent hue

Official paperwork lives in a copy chain: a monochrome laser printer, the copier, a scanner, a filing cabinet. Colour that carries meaning falls out somewhere along it. The one colour a formal document usually carries, a letterhead mark or seal, belongs to the institution, and this system does not imitate it.

What the accent token does

It holds the ink and is used reversed: white on ink marks the one thing the reader must do (return by a date) or decide (decision needed). One per page.

A serif to read, a sans to find

Letters and memos are read straight through, so the body is a serif. Headings, table heads, labels and the signature block are sans. On a projected deck the body switches to sans, because thin serif strokes wash out.

Ruled tables and status

Heavy rules bound a table, hairlines separate rows, and a light grey head column survives a photocopy. Status is symbol and word first: ✓ Done, ▲ Scheduled, ✕ Not started.
