## What they say

- **b2 itself carried no record**, so its 96.95 ms is not named from b2; its wall (106.6 against 51 to 57) moved with its loop max, so the extra was inside the call's longest gap.
- **The search mirror's background extraction cannot land in the first batch after a read on this tree.** A read links the artifact `viewed`, which the mirror counts as the host being busy, so no background extraction starts for MIRROR_QUIET_MS (2 s) after any read (the search test "holds a due extraction off for MIRROR_QUIET_MS after a session's read links an artifact" fails when a link does not count); and both of the gate's documents are stored over MIRROR_BACKGROUND_MAX_BYTES (Scale 200's head 197,759 bytes after the named version, Scale 20's log 136,585 bytes, read from the replay's store), so background work never takes them.
- **The host's timed background work was not due in b2's batch** (from the step timeline): maintenance 60 s after the host's start, about 23:58:08.8Z; the auto version 2 min after the seed's human write, about 23:59:09.4Z, and it writes nothing until a revision passes the named version; both documents' idle unloads inside the 66 s wait, Scale 20's next one about 23:59:16Z; the idle replica sweep at about 23:58:09.4Z and 23:59:09.4Z.
- **What is left is the call's own window**: a V8 collection, the commit's fsync, the thread off the CPU, or the batch's own work. The record tells them apart: `cpuMs` near the gap's length means the thread ran (a `gc` entry names a collection); well under it, the thread waited (a long `commit` names the fsync; nothing named, the scheduler).
- **The replays**: the first batch's CPU inside its longest gap stayed between 41.2 and 72.2 ms (median 49.5) while the gap reached 380.2; every stray was time off the CPU: the commit's F_FULLFSYNC in 3 of 40 (100.8, 166.8 and 217.8 ms at under 1 ms of CPU), the scheduler or a collection's helper threads in the rest. The host's own CPU work in that batch does not double.
- **The app**: the 40-round repeat (load 11 to 14) read p50 38.1, p95 59.8 and one stray, round 34 at 126.9 ms (wall 139.1) over a **63.1 ms major collection** inside the batch's longest gap (cpu 81.0); the 12-round run's slowest round was also over a major collection (13.0 ms in 70.7), and quiet run 2's row 5 itself held a 13.2 ms one (83.53). Quiet run 4's `5 batch max` (87.95) was a commit's fsync, 87.4 ms at 0.7 ms of CPU.
- **A collection after a first read is systematic**: a session's first read builds two whole copies of the 200-page document on the host, its host replica and its agent replica's port-synced doc (the record's `replica` entries, 28 to 44 ms of each read), about 15 MB promoted soon after; a full collection forced right after the read took 41.5 and 73.1 ms. The leading candidate for b2's +52 ms is that collection's pause landing in the next batch; the next red row 5 says so with a `gc` entry `major`, or names another cause.
