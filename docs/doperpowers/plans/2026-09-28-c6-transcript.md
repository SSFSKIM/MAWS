## Purpose

After C6, a session's whole history reads like a conversation rather than a tool log. A user message sits in its bubble; below it the assistant's answer comes first, and the work that produced it (reads, searches, edits, commands, subagents, thinking) folds under one line, "Worked for 2m 14s · 12 tool calls · 1 denied", that opens into semantic groups ("Edited 3 files, ran 2 commands"), each group into cells, each cell into its full detail (the command with its cwd and output and exit status, the edit with its inline diff, the read with its path chips, the MCP call with a raw escape hatch). A denied action is never buried: it stands outside its group, the divider counts it, and clicking the count lands on it. A turn that changed files ends with a receipt naming the first three files with line counts, a route into Review, and Undo bound to the engine's file rewind. Subagents appear as one sentence per lifecycle step with a glyph each, updating as their messages arrive, and open the child's own transcript rendered by the same code. Answered questions and approved plans stay in history as what was asked and what was chosen; a pending one leaves a slot where C7's request card docks. Compaction, reset, retraction and rewind draw a line where the model's context changed, so what the reader sees matches what the model has. `!` commands show as replayed rows keyed on the engine's bash tags. Markdown streams with math, diagrams and highlighted code as tokens arrive, and a five-hundred-turn transcript with nested child activity scrolls at sixty frames per second while expanding old work never drags the reader back to the stream.

To see it working before C3 and C4 exist: `pnpm dev:harness` opens the transcript harness, a development page that plays any probe fixture or a synthetic five-hundred-turn transcript into the real renderer at adjustable speed, through a replay stub that turns journal rows into X1 events. C7 docks its cards into the slot this renderer leaves; C8 mounts this renderer over a child agent's events for its drill-in.

What C6 does not do: talk to an engine (C3), read `~/.claude` (C4), own the composer, the request card or the docked approval (C7), the right panel and its drill-in navigation (C8), or the tab strip and window (C5). C6 renders X1 events it is given and exposes a component, a store and callbacks.

## Terms

- **Event** (X1): one `SessionEvent` from `src/shared/graph/events.ts` (C1), a discriminated union on `kind`; C3 produces them from the wire, C4 from disk, the replay stub below from fixtures. C6 consumes events and nothing rawer.
- **Presentation model** (X3): the tree of **rows** and **cells** derived from events, defined in `src/renderer/src/transcript/model/rows.ts`. A **row** is a unit of the virtualised list (one entry in `getVirtualItems()`); a **cell** is one tool call's line inside an activity group (or, when standalone, a row of its own); **cell detail** is the expanded body of a cell.
- **Turn**: the events between a `turn.started` and its `turn.ended`, or opened implicitly by the first top-level `user.message` after a closed turn. A turn has an **origin**: `human` when the owner sent the message, `engine` when the engine started it after a workflow or background completion. Origin is resolved by the rule *Turn origin* under Derivation, never read raw: `engine` needs positive evidence, and a null origin (disk history) is human.
- **Work area**: the rows of a turn between its user message and its final answer (thinking, groups, cells, subagent rows, request slots). The **divider** is the row that summarises the work area and folds it.
- **Activity group**: consecutive **groupable** tool cells rendered as one row with a summary phrase; a **standalone** cell breaks the run and is a row of its own.
- **Category**: one of the six summary buckets of X3 plus the catch-all `other` (Interfaces, classification), in the fixed order MCP sources, loaded skills, file changes, exploration, commands, web.
- **Answer**: the assistant text blocks after the last tool use of a turn. **Commentary**: assistant text blocks between tool uses (the model narrating). Both are `assistant_text` rows; only the answer stays outside the work area.
- **Follow mode**: the list is pinned to its end and moves with new content. Broken by any upward scroll beyond the threshold; restored by "Jump to latest" or by scrolling back to the end.
- **Replay stub**: `src/renderer/src/transcript/dev/replay-stub.ts`, a development-only normaliser from X8 journal rows to X1 events, implementing the derivation rules pinned in C1's Interfaces for the frames the transcript renders. It stands in for C3's normaliser and is retired when C3 lands.
- **Harness**: `src/renderer/harness.html`, a second renderer entry built only in development or when `MAWS_HARNESS=1`, that mounts the real transcript over a chosen fixture through the replay stub, with playback speed, an "expand all old work" control and a metrics overlay Playwright reads.
- **Spill**: a tool result too large for the wire; the engine writes it under `tool-results/` and the event carries `spillPath` (X1 `tool.result`). C4 reads it on request.
- **Safeguard verdict**: X1's `tool.result.safeguard` (`not_evaluated`, `not_flagged`, `flagged`), the auto-mode server lane's review of a tool call.
- **Interruption**: a `user-rejected` denial inside a turn whose `turn.ended.terminalReason` is an aborted reason; rendered as "stopped", never as "denied" (pass-2 item 7).
- **Disposition**: how a tool result carrying a `denial` renders and counts: `denied` (a standalone denied cell, counted in the divider's denied count), `auto_denied` (the same cell, counted as auto-denied) or `stopped` (rendered as stopped, counted in neither). The denial table under Derivation assigns one to every `DENIAL_KINDS` member.
- **Block identity**: the key `<apiMessageId>:<blockIndex>` under which an assistant text or thinking block is known from its first `assistant.block.delta` through its `assistant.block.complete`; text and thinking row ids are built from it, so a row keeps its key when its block completes (*Streaming* under Derivation).
- **Hydration**: how a mounted transcript catches up with a session that produced events before it mounted: subscribe and buffer, apply the snapshot, drain the buffer with id deduplication, then deliver live (Public surface).

## Interfaces and Dependencies

### X3 — identifiers and classification (`src/renderer/src/transcript/model/rows.ts`, `keys.ts`)

```ts
import type { SessionId, MessageUuid, ApiMessageId, EventId, ToolUseId, TaskId, AgentId, RequestId } from '@shared/graph/ids';
import type { DenialKind, SafeguardVerdict, PermissionMode } from '@shared/graph/enums';
import type { ContentBlock, TokenUsage, Question } from '@shared/graph/events';

export type TurnId = string & z.$brand<'TurnId'>;   // `turn:<userMessageUuid>`; `turn:e<eventId>` for engine-initiated turns or a user message without uuid
export type RowId  = string & z.$brand<'RowId'>;    // formats in keys.ts, listed below
export type CellId = string & z.$brand<'CellId'>;   // `cell:<toolUseId>`; `cell:think:<apiMessageId>:<blockIndex>`; `cell:hook:<hookId>`; `cell:reads:<firstToolUseId>` for a merged read cell

export const TOOL_CATEGORIES = ['mcp', 'skills', 'files', 'exploration', 'commands', 'web', 'other'] as const;  // fixed summary order (X3); `other` is phrased only when it is the sole category
export type ToolCategory = (typeof TOOL_CATEGORIES)[number];

// Attached to every tool event at derivation time (X3's five fields, plus the two X1 facts the renderer shows in detail).
export interface ToolClassification {
  groupable: boolean;                 // false ⇒ standalone: the cell is a row of its own and breaks the group run
  hidden: boolean;                    // true ⇒ the cell exists in the expanded group but contributes no summary phrase (bookkeeping tools)
  category: ToolCategory;
  denial: { kind: DenialKind; disposition: 'denied' | 'auto_denied' | 'stopped'; interruption: boolean; reason: string | null } | null;   // disposition from the denial table under Derivation; interruption: user-rejected inside an aborted turn
  subagentLifecycle: 'start' | 'progress' | 'finish' | 'message' | null;
  startsGroup: boolean;               // a new group begins here even though the previous cell was groupable
  safeguard: SafeguardVerdict;
  humanDecision: boolean;             // a permission.requested for this tool use was resolved by the human ('user')
}
```

Row id formats (`keys.ts`), all deterministic from X1 identifiers so a re-derivation of a turn yields the same ids and the virtualiser keeps its anchor: `time:<turnId>`, `user:<messageUuid>` (or `user:<eventId>`), `text:<apiMessageId>:<blockIndex>`, `divider:<turnId>`, `group:<firstCellId>`, standalone cells use their `CellId`, `receipt:<turnId>`, `subagent:<taskId>` (a grouped sentence uses the first task id), `agentmsg:<eventId>`, `think:<apiMessageId>:<blockIndex>` (text and thinking rows key on the block identity, which a live delta already carries, so the key is the same before and after the block completes; *Streaming*), `plan:<toolUseId>`, `question:<toolUseId>`, `slot:<requestId>`, `error:<eventId>` (`retry:<turnId>` for the in-place retry row, `reconnect:<eventId of the episode's first 'attempting' event>` with the row updated in place through its episode, so two reconnect episodes never share a key), `boundary:<eventId>`, `bash:<inputMessageUuid>`, `local:<messageUuid>` (or `local:<eventId>`).

Rules the reducer implements (the parts that are decisions; the executor derives the rest from the row types):

- **Turn boundaries.** `turn.started` opens a turn (id from `userMessageUuid`, else `turn:e<eventId>`) unless its `userMessageUuid` is in `echoUuids`, in which case it is dropped: the `!` echo has a lifecycle of its own with a `system/init` (`pass2/bash-context-echoed` seq 9 is the echo, seq 12 its `system/init`, seq 13 its empty result), and C3's `system/init` rule can open a turn for it. If no turn is open, a top-level `user.message` that is not `isSynthetic`, has `bash: null` and is not a `bash_echo`, or an `assistant.block.*` or `tool.use`, opens one implicitly (C4's backfill may not synthesise `turn.started`); bash-tagged, echo, synthetic, `local-command` and `interrupt-marker` messages never open a turn. `turn.ended` with `isEchoReceipt: true` closes nothing. A `command.lifecycle` event never closes a turn (a dropped idle prompt arrives as a `turn.ended` with `terminalReason: 'cancelled'` from C3 or the stub; Decision Log). Two implicit closures (whole-branch review, 2026-09-28): a session state's `process.exited` closes every accepted open turn as `stopped` (C3 synthesises no `turn.ended` for a killed or stopped process; pending prompts stay pending), and a `turn.started` for a prompt with no pending turn closes the previous accepted open turn and becomes current, as `stopped` when that turn carries interrupt evidence (a retained interrupt marker or a `user-rejected` denial) and as `completed` otherwise (C4's disk backfill emits `turn.ended` only from `system/turn_duration`, which SDK-entrypoint sessions, MAWS's own included, never write: 117 of the owner's 150 most recent sessions have none, so `stopped` by default would label every disk turn "You stopped"; re-review of the whole-branch fix wave); the `next` fold applies only to a pending prompt whose message was already written. Any other `turn.ended` closes the turn, stores it, and re-derives the turn once, which is when the origin is re-resolved (next rule), interruptions are paired (denial `user-rejected` + aborted `terminalReason`) and the divider state settles (`completed`, `stopped` when the terminal reason is an abort, `failed` when `isError`).
- **Turn origin.** A turn is `engine` only on positive evidence: its `turn.started.origin` is non-null and not `'human'` (C3 emits `'engine'` from a `system/init` that no prompt of ours precedes; the replay stub does the same), or its closing `turn.ended.origin` is non-null and not `'human'` (`task-notification` today: `pass1/subagent` seq 424 and 486, `pass2/workflow` seq 108; any future kind reads the same way). A turn whose `turn.started.origin` or `turn.ended.origin` is `'peer'` (C3, 2026-09-28: a subagent- or peer-driven turn) is `peer`, its own value, never folded into `engine`. Otherwise the turn is `human`: null on both events is what C4's backfill emits for every disk turn (its table maps a `user` record to `turn.started { origin: null }` and `system/turn_duration` to `turn.ended { origin: null }`), and such a turn draws its user row from its message. The absence of a user message is not evidence: a human turn without one renders as a human turn without a user row, never as an engine turn. An `engine` turn has no user row; when the `task.notification` or `subagent.finished` that triggered it is inside the turn, that row comes first, and the divider reads `Continued for …`.
- **Child traffic.** An event with `parentToolUseId !== null` is appended to `children[toolUseOf(parentToolUseId)]` and updates the matching subagent row's last-activity line (`tool.use.toolName`, latest text) and nothing else in the parent. `variant: 'child'` transcripts are built from a child's own event slice, where those events are top-level.
- **Streaming.** The first `assistant.block.delta` for a block identity `<apiMessageId>:<blockIndex>` not yet in `live` allocates a `LiveBlock` whose `rowId` is the `text:` or `think:` id of that identity and bumps `version` (the turn gains a row: structure changed, and `rowsOf` must show it before the block completes); every later delta for that identity appends to its buffer without bumping `version` (the live row subscribes to the buffer through the store, so no turn re-derives on a token). A text block's row shows the buffer with `live: true` and `messageUuid: null`. `assistant.block.complete` pairs with the `LiveBlock` of the same identity only when that entry is compatible and uncompleted (same block type, `completed: false`); otherwise, as when the complete's `blockIndex` is not the stream index, it pairs with the earliest uncompleted `LiveBlock` of the same `apiMessageId` and block type. The selected entry's identity, not the complete's own `blockIndex`, stays the row's canonical identity: the complete replaces that entry's buffer with the block's content, fills `messageUuid`, marks it completed and bumps `version`; the row id does not change, so the virtualiser's key and the reader's expansion state survive completion. Gate, the split-envelope case of `pass1/permission-roundtrip`: seq 85 completes the thinking block streamed at index 0 and seq 89 the text block streamed at index 1 of `msg_011CfVbJVKzQBxgf5S8iR6fF`, each `assistant` envelope holding a single locally indexed block, so a text complete carrying local index 0 must skip the completed thinking entry at `:0` and complete `text:msg_011CfVbJVKzQBxgf5S8iR6fF:1`, leaving the thinking row intact. A delta whose `apiMessageId` is null takes the turn's most recent non-null one (from an earlier delta or complete); a block that never streamed (disk, C4) takes its identity from the complete event. A `tool_use` block's delta accumulates `inputJsonDelta`; the cell is created at `tool.use`, not from partial JSON. Live deltas carry `apiMessageId` and no `messageUuid` (X1), which is why the block identity, not the message uuid, is the key.
- **Groups and rows** are derived per turn from `events`, in order: user row; then, in event order, thinking rows or cells, tool cells folded into groups by the classification, subagent rows (one per `subagent.started`; consecutive starts from the same assistant message merge into one grouped sentence), agent-message rows, plan and question rows, request slots for pending requests, commentary text rows; then the answer rows (text blocks after the last tool use of the turn); then the receipt when any `files` cell exists. The divider row is emitted right after the user row when the turn has any work-area row; a turn with no work area has no divider, except an `engine` turn, which always gets its divider (M6: `pass2/workflow`'s second turn is a bare answer the engine continued, and without the divider nothing read `Continued for …`, so it looked like more of the previous turn). Work-area rows are present in `rowsOf` only when the turn is expanded.
- **Expansion state** lives in `ui`, keyed by ids, so a re-derived turn keeps what the reader opened. Default for a closed turn is collapsed; an open (working) turn is expanded with completed groups collapsed and the active group's header live. At `turn.ended` the turn collapses only if the reader is following (the view tells the store through `setFollowing`); otherwise it stays expanded until the reader collapses it (residue *Virtualisation and anchoring*).
- **Revisable log.** `boundary.retraction` removes every row derived from `retractedMessageUuids` from its turn and inserts a boundary row at that position; `supersedes` on a completed block replaces the superseded message's rows. `boundary.compaction` and `boundary.reset` become boundary rows between turns (or inside the turn if they arrive mid-turn). `boundary.rewind` hides every row after `precedingAssistantUuid` (turns are kept in `turns`, removed from `order`, counted in `hidden`) and inserts a boundary row at the end offering to reveal them; a revealed branch renders muted with its rows marked not in context.
- **Requests.** `permission.requested`, `question.requested`, `plan.requested`, `dialog.requested`, `elicitation.requested` add to `pending` and emit a `request_slot` row at their position; the matching `*.resolved`/`*.answered` removes the slot and, for a question or a plan, emits the historical row with the answer or the outcome. A permission resolved by the human marks the tool's `humanDecision`. A `permission.requested` whose `resolvedBy` is `'abort'` or `'exit'` leaves no historical row (the process exit row says what happened).
- **Receipts** aggregate `files` cells of the turn including child-agent edits attributed to it; `added`/`removed` count `+`/`-` lines of `structured.structuredPatch` hunks, falling back to line counts of `input.new_string`/`input.old_string` when `structured` is null (disk backfill without `toolUseResult`).
- **Bash rows.** A `user.message` with `bash.part === 'input'` opens a bash row keyed on its uuid, loose when no turn is open (`pass1/queue-and-interrupt` seq 235 arrives after the last result) and in the open turn's work area otherwise; the next `bash.part === 'output'` completes it. A `user.message` with `origin === 'bash_echo'` renders nothing, opens nothing, and adds its uuid to `echoUuids` (Turn boundaries; overlap with C3, below).
- **Local-command and interrupt-marker messages** (C3's M2 findings, relayed 2026-09-28; additive `user.message.origin` values). `origin === 'local-command'` is the engine's `<local-command-stdout>…</local-command-stdout>` echo after a slash command (`set_model` in `pass2/auto-routed` seq 153, 162, 173, 186 and `pass2/auto-unrouted`, all `isReplay: true`): it renders as a `local_command` row (tags stripped, loose when no turn is open, in the work area otherwise), never a user bubble, and opens no turn. `origin === 'interrupt-marker'` is the engine's `[Request interrupted by user]` user frame (`pass1/queue-and-interrupt` seq 197; `pass2/per-task-stop-on` seq 65 and the three other `per-task-stop-*` journals, one each; the engine's tool-use variant `[Request interrupted by user for tool use]` in `per-task-stop-off` and `per-task-stop-workflow-on` is the same marker, M6, and C3's normaliser should tag it `interrupt-marker` too). On disk the marker is an ordinary `user` record (array text content in 138 of 144 recent cases on the owner's Mac) that C4's backfill gives its own `turn.started` before its `user.message`; the reducer therefore folds a turn whose only message is an interrupt marker back onto the turn before it (that turn's closure becomes `stopped`, the marker's turn leaves `order` and `turns`), and derive never renders a marker message as a user row whatever turn holds it (whole-branch re-review round 2). Ask of C4: tag the record `interrupt-marker` and emit no `turn.started` for it, as C3 does: it yields no row of its own and opens no turn; its turn's `stopped` disposition already carries the interruption. The replay stub tags both from the wire (a string content wrapped in `<local-command-stdout>` tags; a single `text` block equal to `[Request interrupted by user]`).
- **Steers and dropped prompts** (SUPERSEDED 2026-09-28 by C3's live acceptance, see *Turn boundaries on this engine* below; the text is kept for the record; `steer: true` now means a prompt whose `started` arrived inside an open turn, `priority: 'next'`). A human prompt written while a turn is running is a *steer*: C3 (and the stub) emit its `user.message` and no `turn.started`; the reducer attaches it to the open turn (its uuid joins the turn's `userMessageUuids`) and emits its user row with `steer: true` in event order among the turn's rows, outside the work area (it stays in `rowsOf` when the divider is collapsed: user, divider, steers and answer in order, receipt). The turn closes at its own `result` as usual (`pass1/queue-and-interrupt`: 99662785 at seq 146 is a steer of the turn opened at seq 4, closed by the result at seq 198 naming `a5bf8381`). A `command.lifecycle` `cancelled|discarded|refused` whose `commandUuid` names a steer marks that steer's row `dropped: true` (copy: a quiet `Not sent` marker on the bubble; seq 191) and changes nothing else, whether it arrives before or after the turn's `result`, unless the closing `turn.ended.userMessageUuids` named that steer (it was consumed; a late lifecycle cannot un-send it); one naming the turn's opening prompt after the turn's `result` (seq 200; `pass2/auto-unrouted` 14, 22, 30; the `per-task-stop-*` cases) is a no-op. A prompt written at idle and dropped before its `system/init` (no fixture shows it; a synthetic journal `transcript/idle-drop` pins it on both sides) arrives as `turn.ended { terminalReason: 'cancelled', result: 'cancelled', isError: false, numTurns: 0, costUsd: 0, usage: zeros, userMessageUuids: [<uuid>], origin: 'human', resultText: null, resultIndex: null, isEchoReceipt: false, durationMs: 0 }` derived by C3's normaliser and the stub from the lifecycle row (its `seq` and `at`); the reducer closes the turn and marks its opening user row `dropped: true`; no divider (no work area). The reducer itself never closes a turn on a lifecycle event.
- **Turn boundaries on this engine** (binding, from C3's live acceptance and fix wave, 2026-09-28). `priority: 'now'` preempts the running turn (its `result` carries `terminal_reason: aborted_streaming`) and runs the prompt as its own next turn and never folds; `priority: 'next'` folds at its `started` (below). C3's normaliser emits `user.message` at the write (so the bubble shows on send) and opens `turn.started { userMessageUuid, origin: 'human' }` at the engine's `command_lifecycle { state: 'started' }` for that uuid; a prompt written while a turn runs gets its own turn when its `started` arrives with no turn open (`priority: 'now'`: the running turn is aborted first), or folds into the running turn when its `started` arrives inside an open turn (`priority: 'next'`: the engine takes it at the next tool boundary and one `result` names both prompts; C3's normaliser adds the uuid to the open turn and opens nothing). Refinement from C3's live run, 2026-09-28. The reducer therefore treats a top-level human `user.message` with no matching `turn.started` yet as a **pending turn**: a turn of its own (id `turn:<uuid>`), placed after the current one in `order`, with its user row and no divider, `steer: false`; the `turn.started` naming that uuid opens it when no turn is open (no second turn, no second user row) in place, unless another turn opened after the prompt was written and has since run, in which case the accepted turn moves to the end of `order` so the transcript keeps run order (what the model saw, §5.3) over write position, or, when a turn is open, folds the pending prompt into that turn: the pending turn is removed, its uuid joins the open turn's `userMessageUuids`, and its user row renders inside the open turn in event order as a bubble with `steer: true` (outside the work area, present when the divider is collapsed); a `command.lifecycle` `cancelled|discarded|refused` naming a pending turn's prompt marks its user row `dropped: true` and closes it without work (`pass1/queue-and-interrupt` seq 146 pending, 191 dropped, no `started` ever comes); after-result lifecycles naming a closed turn are no-ops. A pending bubble is never a loose event. The replay stub mirrors C3: `user.message` at the `out` querying frame, `turn.started` at the `command_lifecycle started` for that uuid (every prompt in all 40 journals has one except the cancelled queued prompt; verified 2026-09-28), so the equivalence gate compares like with like. The idle-drop synthetic journal still pins the drop; the `terminalReason: 'cancelled'` synthetic `turn.ended` of the superseded rule is no longer emitted.

`useTranscriptStore` (`src/renderer/src/stores/transcript.ts`) keeps one `TranscriptState` per key (`appSessionId`, or `appSessionId:agentId` for a child), attaches a source through the hydration protocol below, applies batches through `applyEvents`, exposes `rows(key)`, `lastAssistantLine(key)` (for C5's sidebar and Overview secondary line), `jumpTo(key, rowId)` (expands the owning turn and group, then scrolls), and `setFollowing(key, boolean)`.

**Hydration protocol** (`useTranscriptStore.attach(key, source)`, shared by both adapters and the harness; the one order, so a batch that arrives before, during or after the snapshot lands the same): (1) `source.subscribe` first; while the key is `hydrating`, every batch is buffered, not applied; (2) `await source.snapshot()` and apply it through `applyEvents`, which records each id in `seen`; (3) drain the buffer in arrival order, dropping every event whose `id` is in `seen` and applying the rest, one `applyEvents` call per batch; (4) switch the key to `live`: later batches apply directly, still dropping ids in `seen` (a live batch can overlap the snapshot's tail, and disk-derived events carry `seq: null`, ruling 5 of the wave-2 ledger). Events reach the reducer as snapshot, then buffered batches, then live batches, none twice and none reordered within its batch; `seen` doubles as the retraction index. `detach(key)` unsubscribes and clears the buffer; an `attach` that starts a key over (no remaining holder) hydrates into a fresh reducer state and installs it once the snapshot has applied, keeping the old rows visible until then (no empty flash on a tab switch; a failed snapshot leaves the old rows beside the failure banner; a `jumpTo` requested during the round trip is carried across the install and re-resolved by row id), because the fresh snapshot is the whole history and may carry the other id form (live versus disk) after an unobserved process exit or child finish (whole-branch review and its re-review, 2026-09-28). `replaySource.snapshot()` resolves `[]` (the player releases everything through `subscribe`); `ipcSource.subscribe` is `session.eventBatch` filtered by `appSessionId` (and `agentId` for a child), `ipcSource.snapshot` is one `session.events` call (`fs.agentEvents` for a child).

```ts
const SCHEME = /^([a-zA-Z][a-zA-Z0-9+.-]*):/;
export const remarkTranscriptSources: Plugin = () => (tree) => visit(tree, (node, index, parent) => {
  if (!parent || typeof index !== 'number') return;
  if (node.type === 'html') { parent.children.splice(index, 1); return index; }               // raw HTML, block and inline, is removed before remark-rehype
  if (node.type === 'link' && !SCHEME.test(node.url)) node.url = `maws-path:${node.url}`;    // a scheme-less href is a path chip, never a navigable link

});

  const scheme = SCHEME.exec(url)?.[1].toLowerCase() ?? null;
  if (key === 'src') return /^data:image\//i.test(url) ? url : null;                         // images: inline data only
  return scheme !== null && ['https', 'http', 'mailto', 'maws-path', 'maws-image'].includes(scheme) ? url : null;   // links: https, http, mailto and the chips; javascript:, file:, data:, vbscript: and anything else lose the href
};
```

## Residue decisions (C6, 2026-09-28)

Paste-ready for the charter's §12 C6 section, in the C1 format: what the design left to the child, settled here; the execution document `docs/doperpowers/plans/2026-09-28-c6-transcript.md` carries the milestones and the concrete X3 definitions. Grades: binding where the design binds the outcome, advisory where a later child may overturn with a dated line here.

- *Derivation architecture*: an incremental reducer (`applyEvents`) that keeps one `TurnState` per turn and re-derives rows per turn on change, with rows memoised per turn version, the first delta of a new block bumping the version once (a live row appears) and every later delta written to a live buffer that only the live row subscribes to; the flat row list for the virtualiser is the concatenation of per-turn row arrays. Reason: a five-hundred-turn transcript receives up to sixty delta batches a second, and only the open turn may pay for them; retraction, supersession and rewind need "unapply", which a per-turn rebuild from retained events gives for free. Alternative: memoised selectors over the whole event array, rejected because every selector is O(events) per invalidation and the memo keys would have to reinvent turn boundaries. Binding on stable ids and per-turn re-derivation (C7 and C8 key on X3 ids); advisory on the store mechanism (Zustand, per C1's *State wiring*).
- *Row identity*: ids are deterministic functions of X1 identifiers (`user:<messageUuid>`, `text:<apiMessageId>:<blockIndex>`, `cell:<toolUseId>`, `group:<firstCellId>`, `divider:<turnId>`, `subagent:<taskId>`, `boundary:<eventId>`, …; the full table is in the execution document), never positions; text and thinking rows key on the block identity `<apiMessageId>:<blockIndex>`, which a live delta already carries, so a row's key survives its block's completion. Reason: TanStack's end anchoring and prepend stability require stable keys, and a re-derived turn must produce the same ids so expansion state and scroll anchors survive. Alternative: index keys, rejected (every prepend and every collapse renumbers). Binding (X3).
- *Tool classification*: the table in the execution document maps tool names to the six categories of X3 plus a catch-all `other` that is phrased only when it is the sole category, with three presentations (grouped, grouped-but-hidden for bookkeeping tools such as `TodoWrite` and `Task*`, standalone), overrides in order (denial, human decision, image result), and a group-start rule (first groupable cell after the turn start, a standalone row, a commentary text block, a subagent row or a boundary; thinking cells never start a group). `Agent`, `Task` and `Workflow` calls are not cells: their lifecycle events are the subagent row. Hooks from settings and plugins are command cells inside a turn and are not rendered outside one. Reason: the function must be total over MCP, plugin and future tools, and a category for the unknown keeps the six-bucket order intact. Alternative: dropping unknown tools from the summary, rejected because it hides work. Binding (X3).
- *Denials, interruptions and the safeguard verdict*: every `DENIAL_KINDS` member has a disposition: `denied` (`user-rejected` outside an aborted turn, `permission-rule`), `auto_denied` (`automode-blocked`, `automode-parsing-error`) or `stopped` (`user-rejected` inside a turn whose terminal reason is an abort, the interruption of pass-2 item 7; `interrupted`; `cancelled`; `automode-unavailable`, which is X1's Needs-you kind and never a denied cell). A `denied` or `auto_denied` cell is standalone whatever its group; the divider counts `denied` and `auto-denied` separately, each count linking to the first such cell, expanding the work area if collapsed and moving focus; a `stopped` cell renders "stopped" and is counted in neither. The safeguard verdict appears as one line in cell detail and as one segment of the turn summary popover, never on the cell line. Reason: §19, §13 X1 (`automode-blocked` informational, `automode-unavailable` Needs-you) and pass-2 item 7. Binding.
- *Copy*: the divider states (`Working`, `Working for {t}`, `Worked for {t} · {n} tool calls`, `Continued for {t} …` for engine-initiated turns, `You stopped after {t} …`, `Failed after {t} …`, ` · {d} denied`, ` · {a} auto-denied`), the group phrases per category, the cell lines, the subagent sentences, the boundary and error lines are fixed in the execution document's copy table.
