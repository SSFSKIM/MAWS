### 1.2 The memory-budgeted process policy (§4.4, S-5)

Charter line 152, verbatim:

> Session process policy is memory-budgeted, never a fixed timer. A process stays live while it has a running turn, background tasks or workflows, session-scoped permission grants, connected MCP servers per `mcp_status`, or a held peer message. Idle processes without those are candidates for exit only under budget pressure, least recently used first. The budget counts the whole process tree under the engine pid (stdio MCP servers included) and is visible in Session info; measured under the owner's settings, an idle session is 270 to 420 MB and a tool call peaks near 550 MB. When a process with session grants must exit, the app snapshots the grants it issued (it sees every `updatedPermissions` it sent) and re-applies them on resume through `apply_flag_settings` (verified: the restored grant is not re-prompted). Exit never silently discards background work: the user is asked. Until the budget policy ships (C12), engine processes never exit on their own: only archive, quit, or an explicit stop ends one. (S-5)

Grade: §11 (line 277) lists "the memory policy shape and interim rule (§4.4)" among the binding items.

**Numbers.** From `spikes/protocol-probe/findings-pass2.md` §2 (lines 30 to 42) and the fixtures:

| Case | Tree RSS | Parts |
|---|---|---|
| One idle session under the owner's settings (`real-settings.jsonl`, idle after initialize) | 418,688 KiB | engine 257,744, `python -m ptc.mcp` 51,792, the advisor's node MCP server 109,152 |
| Peak during a tool call | 466,096 KiB snapshot, 555,360 KiB sampled peak | |
| Three idle sessions at once (`real-settings-idle-1.jsonl`) | 272,464 KiB | engine about 160 MB of it |
| Same (`real-settings-idle-2.jsonl`) | 280,736 KiB | |

The engine's own RSS drops from about 258 MB alone to about 160 MB when three run together. Why is unverified; the likely cause is shared text pages counted differently. So a sum of per-process RSS may overstate the budget, and C12 should choose its measure with that in mind. Findings: "Stdio MCP servers are about 40 percent of an idle tree."

**This machine.** `hw.memsize` is 34,359,738,368 (32 GiB) on an Apple M1 Max with 10 cores. `memory_pressure` read 52 % free during this pass, with many agent worktrees running. That this is the owner's daily machine is inferred from the fixtures being recorded here. At §10's scale, nine idle sessions are about 3.8 GB (9 × 420 MB) and about 5.0 GB at simultaneous peak (9 × 550 MB), so on 32 GB budget pressure at nine sessions is unlikely. The policy matters when Scene A grows past roughly twenty sessions, or when other load competes (inference).

**What exists in code:**

- *Process table* (`src/engine-host/process-table.ts`). Reservations keyed by app id (lines 79 to 93). `keepAliveReasons` computes §4.4's list in order: turn, background tasks, grants, a connected MCP server, a held peer message (lines 19 to 29). The header says it is "computed … and never acted on: under the interim rule nothing exits a process but stop, archive and shutdown (C12 decides otherwise later)" (lines 5 to 8). One `ps -axo pid=,ppid=,rss=` every 10 s samples own and whole-tree RSS for all live processes (lines 14, 31 to 61, 116 to 144). Sampling starts in `src/engine-host/index.ts:34`.
- *Status* (`src/shared/engine/status.ts`). `backgroundTasks`, `grants`, `mcpServers`, `heldPeerMessages` (lines 35 to 38), `pendingRequests` and `queue` (39), `keepAlive` (40, commented "informational under the never-exit rule") and `memory { rssKiB, treeRssKiB, sampledAt }` (41). They are assembled in `src/engine-host/session.ts:336-339`. Held peer messages expire after five minutes (`session-status.ts:198-217`).
- *Grant snapshots.* Recorded from every `updatedPermissions` sent (`session.ts:153-156`), persisted through the state store, re-applied on a resume or an inheriting fork (`session.ts:440-444`) and loaded or inherited at start (`session.ts:462-480`). Archive keeps the state file (`src/engine-host/ipc.ts:189-211`, `forgetJournal` only). C10's live item 9 passed: "grants re-applied from the kept state file" (`2026-09-28-c10-sessions-worktrees-trust.md:819`).
- *Termination ladder* (`src/engine-host/session-ladder.ts:8`). `end_session` with 3 s grace, then SIGTERM and 3 s, then SIGKILL and 1 s.
- *Exit reasons* are `quit | stop | archive | crash | unknown` in `session-ladder.ts:10` and X1 `process.exited.reason` (`src/shared/graph/events.ts:206`). `session.stop` takes only `stop | archive` (`src/shared/ipc/schema/session.ts:83`).
- *Reconnect triggers.* `process_exit | host_restart | transport_error | user` (`events.ts:201`); only `user` fires in P0.
- *Resume with a message.* C10's `runResume` sends the message at `ready` (`src/main/sessions/resume.ts:19-60`). The disclosure of background work before a stop is C10's `BackgroundWorkDisclosure` (`src/renderer/src/sessions/BackgroundWorkDisclosure.tsx:28`), used by archive and by C11's route switch.
- *Session info* (C11, on its branch) shows `status.memory.treeRssKiB`. C11's document, line 249: "the 9 × 420 MB budget line arrives with C12 and is not shown."

**What is missing:**

- any budget figure, setting or pressure signal;
- any eviction;
- an exit reason for it (X1 and X2 additive);
- a state that shows a process was released for memory rather than failed or stopped;
- Send resuming an exited session (`submit-state.ts:73-75`);
- a cap on the per-session event ring an exited row keeps. `RING_SIZE = 5_000` (`src/engine-host/session-stream.ts:24`); the tracker row says "~6,000" and is stale. A range the ring has dropped can be rebuilt from the launch's journal (`session-stream.ts:4-8`), so the ring is a cache.

**The contradiction, restated.** For the owner, `mcp_connected` holds every idle process, and the grants reason holds every process that has ever received an "always allow" in this launch. Read literally, the policy therefore never exits anything, and §10 item 6 would pass trivially. Whether MCP servers carry state worth keeping is real but unverified: the ptc MCP server is the owner's persistent-kernel tool, and whether its kernel dies with the server process was not checked. This is why the keep-alive list is a decision for the owner, not a mechanical fix.

## 3. Open product decisions for the owner

Each decision lists the facts, the alternatives, my recommendation, and the condition under which another alternative wins.

### D1. May idle engine processes exit under memory pressure, and what does the owner see?

**Facts:** §1.2. Every owner session holds `mcp_connected`. Grants are re-applied without a re-prompt (verified). Cold initialize with the owner's settings takes 0.8 to 1.1 s. The queue persists in the state file. Send is disabled on an exited session today.

**Alternatives:**

- (a) Keep never-exit in P0 and drop the policy: show each tree's memory in Session info and a total somewhere, and let the owner stop sessions by hand. §4.4 and §10 item 6 are amended to make never-exit the P0 rule.
- (b) Implement §4.4 literally. For this owner it never exits anything.
- (c) Implement with an amended keep-alive list:
  - **Hard reasons:** an open turn, background tasks or workflows, pending requests, queued messages, a held peer message.
  - **Grants:** not a blocker, since the snapshot re-applies.
  - **Connected MCP servers:** not a blocker, except servers the owner marks as keeping a session alive (a setting listing server names, for stateful ones).
  - **Eviction:** least recently used among idle eligible sessions, once the sum of trees exceeds the budget.
  - **What the owner sees:** the row and Overview card stay idle with a quiet "released" mark (not the failed glyph, no notification, no Needs-you). Session info says when and why ("released at 14:02 to stay under 8 GB"). Typing and pressing Send resumes the session transparently: a `session.reconnect { trigger: 'process_exit' }` row, about 1 to 2 s before the turn starts, the queue and grants intact.
  - **Over budget with no candidates:** one app-wide banner ("Memory budget exceeded: N sessions hold background work") and nothing is stopped, since §4.4 says "the user is asked" and a banner that offers the per-session stop with C10's disclosure asks without deciding for them.

**Recommendation: (c).** Scene A is the point of the product. On 32 GB the budget rarely binds, but when it does it has to work, and transparent resume makes an exit cost about one second. The budget default is the owner's number. My proposal is 25 % of physical memory, 8 GB here, about nineteen idle sessions at 420 MB. Ship it as a setting, with the budget line in Session info.

**Needs:** a dated §19 entry amending §4.4's list (grants, MCP) and §10 item 6's wording, and Send-to-resume in C7's submit state.

**When (a) wins:** the owner never runs more than about fifteen sessions at once, or the ptc kernel (or another MCP server's state) turns out to die with its server and the owner would rather not mark servers one by one.

### D2. Are spares worth their cost on this machine?

**Facts:** §1.3.

- 255 MB was measured without settings or MCP; an owner-settings spare would be about 420 MB (inference).
- It saves the 0.8 to 1.1 s cold initialize on a new session's first send only.
- One spare fits one route and environment, serves no resume or fork, and needs a probe of claim-time project loading.
- §3 already places it in P1.

**Alternatives:**

- (a) No spare in P0, as §3 P1 says.
- (b) One pooled spare kept warm, as §12 C12 says: about 420 MB always resident, the ptc and advisor servers running idle, and a probe gate.
- (c) A spare on intent: spawn `--await-claim` when an unstarted tab opens (or on its first keystroke), claim it at first send, end it when the tab closes. Nothing stays resident when no new tab is open, and most of the second is hidden while the owner types. It still needs the claim-cwd probe and the trust shape.

**Recommendation: (a).** Amend §12 C12's Purpose to drop it.

**When (c) wins:** the owner, after the sitting, finds the new-session latency noticeable. (b) is dominated by (c) on this owner's usage.

### D3. What does `--max-budget-usd` mean on a resume, and should MAWS expose a spend cap at all?

**Alternatives:**

**Recommendation: (a).** The 5h and 7d windows already bound the mainline.

## 4. Proposed cut

### Gate verdict

C12 as written fails **well-scoped** on four counts:

- **State owners.** The process policy spans the engine host's table and ladder, main's read model and settings, and the composer. Release engineering is build configuration, the keychain, a feed and Apple's notary service. Recomposition is the parent's act.
- **Verification.** Multi-session memory runs, notarization with a clean-machine install, and the owner's sitting have nothing in common.
- **Dependency timing.** Engine custody and restart coordination can start now. The budget needs C11's settings v3 and Session info on master. The sweep needs C8b, C11 and wave 4. Recomposition is last.
- **Acceptance** is chained by "and".

It also fails **well-defined** until D1, D2 and D7 are ruled. It decomposes into three children plus the closing act.

### C12a: Process budget (plan)

**Purpose.** MAWS keeps as many sessions live as memory allows and releases idle ones without the owner losing anything: a released session looks idle, and the next message resumes it.

**Acceptance (observable):**

1. With the budget set just below the sum of N idle trees, starting session N+1 releases the least-recently-used eligible session within one sample period (10 s). Its row and card read released, and Session info names the budget and the total.
2. A session holding any hard keep-alive reason is never released. Over budget with no candidate, the banner appears and nothing stops.
3. Typing in a released session and pressing Send resumes it (`session.reconnect { trigger: 'process_exit' }`) and delivers the message exactly once. Queued messages survive, and an issued grant is honoured without a re-prompt (pass-2 `grant-reapply` fixtures as the pin).
4. Exited rows keep no event ring beyond the cap, and a late `session.events` read of a dropped range still answers from the journal.
5. Nine idle sessions under the owner's settings, with the policy on, measured and recorded as §10 item 6's evidence.

**Edges:** blocked-by D1, C11's merge (settings v3, Session info, `engine.configure`) and C7's Send-to-resume (built here as a C7-marked seam if C7's executor is closed). Blocks the sitting.

**Contracts:** X1 and X2 additive (an exit reason for a budget release, `session.stop` reason, `engine.sessions` or a new budget read); X6 unchanged.

**Grain hint:** plan, four milestones:

Whole-branch review at reviewer-high, the process lifecycle being C3-grade.

**Acceptance:**
