## Purpose

After this child, every session MAWS runs has a dependable channel to a human who is not watching. The agent marks what it sends: `<to-human>` messages, an `<essential>` the human must not miss, an `<insight>` that teaches, and a `<need-input>` question it can wait on while it keeps working. MAWS shows a session as that report, with everything between the prompt and the answer folded into one working record. A question becomes a card the owner answers in place: a click on a choice sends the answer as the next prompt, through the queue while the agent is busy, and Reply sends free text from the card. The card reads answered only when the engine has the prompt. An unanswered question and an unread essential appear in the sidebar and in Needs you, and as an OS notification when the window is not in front. These states survive a restart and a resume. None of this needs the owner's doperpowers plugin: a clean install gets MAWS's own contract. On the owner's machine, the doperpowers mod hands its to-human drawing to MAWS, so one view, one toggle and one question handler remain.

How to see it working: in a scratch settings home with no doperpowers, start a session from the app and ask the agent to report a plan, flag a risk and ask one question with two choices. The report view shows a message card, a must-read card and a question card above one "Working record" row. Switch to another session and the sidebar row shows the question. Click the recommended choice and the card reads "Queued" while the agent works, then "Answered" once the prompt is delivered. Restart MAWS, and the card still reads "Answered" and the essential still reads unread until Mark read.

## Terms

- **Mark**: one of the four spans the grammar recognises in assistant text: `to-human`, `essential`, `insight`, `need-input` (with flat `<choice>` lines, one `<choice recommended>`). A *question* is a `need-input` mark; an *essential* is an `essential` mark.
- **The lane**: MAWS's to-human behaviour for a session (contract, advertisement, presentation, answers, attention). *On* or *off* per app (settings) with a per-session override (`inherit | on | off`). Its effective value is the override when set, else the app's.
- **The contract**: MAWS's own versioned instruction text (Design §1), passed to the engine with `--append-system-prompt`.
- **Compatible style**: an output style whose front matter declares `maws-to-human-contract: <n>` with `n` in MAWS's compatible set. When the launch's effective settings select one, MAWS appends nothing.
- **Prompt snapshot**: the engine's record of the system prompt it rendered, written into the transcript as an `attachment` of type `prompt_snapshot` at a launch's first turn and reused by later launches (U2, Design §2).
- **Recorded state**: what the session's last prompt snapshot after its last compact boundary carries: `unrecorded` (no snapshot), `contract:<n>` (MAWS's contract n), or `none`. An output style is never in a snapshot (U2 run 6).
- **Desired state**: what the lane asks for this launch: `maws-contract`, `external-compatible-style` or `off`.
- **Watermark**: the per-session time from which marks can raise attention items (Design §5).
- **Report view / full transcript**: the two views of a session tab while the lane is on (Design §7).
- **The seam**: `sendHumanPrompt` (X22, P2-0's), the one way a human-origin prompt leaves the renderer outside the composer's box.

## Design (the residue, with reasons)

### 1. The contract (X18)

The text below is contract **1**, verbatim, as the module constant `TO_HUMAN_CONTRACT` in `src/shared/owner/contract.ts`. Its first line is the header `CONTRACT_HEADER(1)`, which the prompt-state reader (§2) recognises.

```
# Messages to the human (MAWS to-human contract 1)

The human who runs this session reads a report of it, not the transcript. Your prose, your tool calls and their results stay in the session as your working record, which they open only when they go looking. Only what you mark reaches them, so anything you want them to read, your answers included, goes inside a mark:

- `<to-human>…</to-human>`: a message delivered to them, wherever you write it, written as you would write to someone away from the screen.
- `<essential>…</essential>`: what they must not miss. It stays in front of them until they mark it read.
- `<need-input>…</need-input>`: a question you can wait on while you go on with other work, one question per mark, the question on the mark's first line. Its options, when it has any, go inside the mark under the question, one `<choice>…</choice>` line each, with `<choice recommended>…</choice>` on the one you would pick. Their answer arrives later as their next message, beginning `Answering "<the question's first line>":`. Input you cannot continue without is not a mark: ask for it with AskUserQuestion.
- `<insight>…</insight>`: before and after you write code, a brief explanation of two or three implementation choices specific to this codebase. It is shown beside your messages.

For example, a question with two options:

<need-input>Which backend should the board use?
<choice recommended>Postgres: already running for the board</choice>
<choice>SQLite: one file, no daemon</choice>
</need-input>

Write marks as plain text in your reply, never inside code blocks or inline code, where they are shown as code. Say what you mean directly and literally, without metaphor or flourish.
```

Why this text: it keeps every semantic of the owner's style (`output-styles/to-human.md`). Marked spans are the delivered messages; essential is must-not-miss; one question per need-input mark with flat choices and a recommendation; AskUserQuestion for anything blocking; insights before and after code. It adds three things the style leaves to its plugin: the answer's form, the code-span rule of grammar 2 (§4), and the sentence that unmarked text is not delivered. U2 showed that sentence was needed. The draft without it left the model's main answer unmarked (run 2), and with it every part of the reply was marked (run 5 S13). The coding instructions stay because the text is appended to the engine's prompt and replaces nothing.

The answer form `Answering "<head>": <answer>` is the doperpowers mod's, character for character (`answerText` and `questionHead`). With one form, legacy recognition (§6) reads answers given from a terminal, and a compatible style needs no new wording. The contract version changes only when the semantics change. A wording change that keeps semantics keeps the number, and `COMPATIBLE_CONTRACTS` lists every version MAWS can present (initially `[1]`).

### 2. Delivering the contract: the decision per launch (X6; G1)

**What U2 found.** The installed 2.1.295 records each rendered system prompt in the transcript as a `prompt_snapshot` attachment, with the appended text as its last segment. A later launch reuses the last snapshot after the last compact boundary instead of rendering. A resume or a fork passed a different `--append-system-prompt` keeps the old one, and a launch that omits the flag keeps the old one too. A compaction's next turn renders live and records anew. `--system-prompt-snapshot off`, which the binary declares, makes that one launch render live (its own prompt or none) and records nothing, so the next plain launch reverts. The snapshot is the engine's way of keeping the system prompt byte-identical across resumes for the prompt cache. An output style is not part of it: the engine carries the selected style as conversation attachments (`output_style_instructions`, `output_style`) that follow the settings in force (run 6 S15). So the snapshot only ever holds MAWS's own appended text, as its last segment, starting with the contract's header (run 6 S14). §4.5's "appended exactly once per MAWS-owned launch, resume and fork … set or omit the prompt at the next owned launch" is therefore true for a new session only. The fixtures and the run-by-run results are in `spikes/protocol-probe/findings-pass3-p2-to-human.txt`. Its reading of 2.1.292 (`cl`, `Swe`, `DB`, `GXe`) matches the observations.

**The decision.** One function, `ownerChannelLaunch` in `src/main/owner/launch.ts`, runs in main immediately before `session.launch` is dispatched from `setup.ts`'s intent launch (new and fork, including side threads) and from `resume.ts`. It never runs for the trust bootstrap. It computes three things:

1. **Desired state.** If the lane is off for the session: `off`. Else, the resolver (below) reads the effective style for the launch's settings context: a compatible style gives `external-compatible-style`, anything else (none, the default, a built-in, an incompatible or unresolvable style) gives `maws-contract`. When the style cannot be established, MAWS appends. Doubled instructions say the same thing twice, but missing ones silence the lane, so appending is the safer error.
2. **Recorded state.** `src/main/owner/prompt-state.ts` reads the transcript of the session being resumed (for a fork, the parent's; for a new session, none) at `transcriptPathOf(s.env, cwd, engineSessionId)` (`src/main/sessions/context.ts`), the path the session service already uses. It finds the last `attachment` record of type `prompt_snapshot` that follows the last `system` `compact_boundary` record, in file order. It classifies the snapshot's `systemPrompt` segments: `contract:<n>` when a segment starts with `CONTRACT_HEADER(n)`, else `none`. No snapshot, or no readable file, is `unrecorded`. The read scans from the end in 1 MiB chunks for the two record types and caches the answer per (path, size). A later launch then scans only the bytes appended since, so a long session costs one full scan.
3. **The flags.** The snapshot should carry the current contract exactly when the desired state is `maws-contract`, and no contract otherwise. A compatible style arrives through the conversation's style attachments, never through the snapshot. The rule:
   - "append" (`--append-system-prompt <TO_HUMAN_CONTRACT>`) is passed iff the desired state is `maws-contract`, on every such launch, even when the snapshot makes it a no-op, so that it takes effect at the session's next compaction;
   - "off" (`--system-prompt-snapshot off`) is passed iff the recorded state is not `unrecorded` and differs from the wanted one: wanted `maws-contract` and recorded is not `contract:<current>`, or wanted anything else and recorded is some `contract:<n>`;
   - `applied` (the instructions in effect after this launch) is the desired state when "off" was passed or the recorded state is `unrecorded`. Otherwise it is what the snapshot keeps: `maws-contract` for `contract:<current>`, and for `none`, the desired state unless that is `maws-contract` (then `off`, pending the next compaction).

| desired ↓ / recorded → | unrecorded | contract:n (current) | contract:m (other) | none |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `maws-contract` | append; applied maws-contract | append; applied maws-contract | append + off; applied maws-contract | append + off; applied maws-contract |
| `external-compatible-style` | —; applied external | off; applied external | off; applied external | —; applied external |
| `off` | —; applied off | off; applied off | off; applied off | —; applied off |

"Off" is the only way to honour a change at once. It is passed on every launch while the states differ, because it records nothing, until a compaction lets a plain launch record the desired prompt (U2 S6 and S10). Its cost is the snapshot's purpose: a launch under it re-renders the system prompt and may miss the prompt cache, so MAWS passes it only while the states differ. The cell `contract:n` under `external-compatible-style` takes the cost to keep §2 item 3's "no doubled instructions": a session that started under MAWS's contract and now has a declared style would otherwise carry both. Until the doperpowers PR (§11) adds the key, the owner's style is not compatible, so the owner's sessions get the contract beside the style and carry both. The PR removes the cause, and this cell then clears the contract from each such session at its next launch.

**The flag's availability.** The flag is not in the CLI's help. `engine.binaryInfo` gains `declaresSystemPromptSnapshot: boolean`, read once per binary (the same path, size and mtime cache as `--version`) by scanning the binary's bytes for the literal `--system-prompt-snapshot <on|off>`. Main asks for "off" only when it is true. `buildLaunch` also omits the flag when the plan's binary does not declare it, so a launch is never broken by it. When "off" is wanted but unavailable, `applied` stays the recorded state and the session's row says the change applies "after this session's next compaction".

**What is recorded.** Each decided launch writes `owner_channel_sessions` (§10): the launch id, desired, recorded, the flags passed, applied, the resolved style name, and the watermark rule's effect (§5). X20's `source` for a session is `applied` mapped to `maws-contract | external-compatible-style | off`. The launch spec gains two optional keys (Interfaces, X6). The host's state file never stores them: `saveLaunchSpec` writes the spec without them. That copy is read by no production code, and a key in it would move an older build's file aside (§4.2's hazard). Because nothing new is stored, C does not wait on P2-0's tolerant reader to be in the owner's running build. The full argument list stays in the journal's spawn row as it does today.

**The resolver** (`src/main/owner/resolve-style.ts`) reads, never writes. Its config home is the engine's: `claudeConfigDir(s.env, s.env.HOME || home)` over the session service's `s.env`, the captured login-shell environment that main's `index.ts` passes to the session service and the engine is launched with. It is never computed from main's `process.env`, which a Dock-launched app does not share with the shell (a `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` exported only in the shell would otherwise send the resolver and the prompt-state reader to the wrong home). M2 tests the two environments differing. It takes `outputStyle` from the highest-precedence source that sets it: managed settings (`/Library/Application Support/ClaudeCode/managed-settings.json`), then MAWS's own `--settings` (which never sets it), then local `<cwd>/.claude/settings.local.json`, then project `<cwd>/.claude/settings.json`, then user `<configHome>/settings.json`. A missing or malformed file sets nothing. It then finds the style named:
- `default`, `Explanatory` or `Learning` is a built-in and never compatible.
- `<plugin>:<style>` is looked up among installed plugins (`<configHome>/plugins/installed_plugins.json`, version 2) that are enabled in the merged settings' `enabledPlugins` and whose installation applies to the cwd (user scope, or project or local scope with a matching `projectPath`). In `<installPath>/output-styles/*.md` it takes the file whose front-matter `name`, or file stem, equals `<style>`.
- Any other name is looked up in `<cwd>/.claude/output-styles/` and then `<configHome>/output-styles/`.

A style is compatible when its front matter carries `maws-to-human-contract` with an integer value in `COMPATIBLE_CONTRACTS`. The engine's loader ignores unknown front-matter keys, and `claude plugin validate` accepts the key (U2, run 2 style).

**The mismatch check** (§4.5). On the first `session.init` of a decided launch, main compares `outputStyle` (null reads as `default`) with the resolver's style name. If they differ, the resolver misread the engine's settings. Main records `observed_style` and `style_mismatch`, and the session's state carries `mismatch: {resolved, observed}`, shown as a notice at the top of the report view and on the mods row. The next decision for that session resolves by the observed name (looking that style up as above) until a launch observes agreement. Because the engine injects the style it names there through attachments that follow the settings (run 6), `output_style` is the style in effect. The snapshot, read from the transcript, is the truth for the appended contract only.

**Why this shape.** The two alternatives lose. Passing "off" on every launch would make every resume re-render and forgo the cache for every session. Never passing it would leave a lane switched on missing from every existing session until its next compaction. The contract would also stay in force after the owner turned the lane off, which is G1's "off at the app or the session stops future injection" broken in the session the owner just switched. A SessionStart hook injecting the text as context was rejected: it would add a copy to the history at every launch and requires hooks MAWS does not own.

### 3. The advertisement (X18; G2)

`MAWS_NATIVE_TO_HUMAN=1`, where the value is `TO_HUMAN_CONTRACT_VERSION`, travels in the launch's `extraSettings.env`, merged beside `CUA_BROWSER_BACKENDS` by `withOwnerChannel` in `launch-mapping.ts`. It is set on every decided launch whose lane is on, whatever the desired state, and omitted when the lane is off. It says only that MAWS draws the channel. The value is per launch: the engine applies settings env to its process environment at startup and snapshots nothing of it, so toggling takes effect at the next launch. U2 verified that `$.env.get('MAWS_NATIVE_TO_HUMAN')` in a function-hook worker reads `1` through `--settings` env alone (run 2 new, style) and through the process environment alone (run 2 resume), and `<unset>` with neither (run 2 off, fork). A SessionStart command hook reads the same. The order of §4.5 therefore resolves at its first entry, the `extraSettings.env` seam, and needs no host change. The constructed environment (an allowlist entry plus a spec field) stays unused, as does the client id `maws-main` that a `session.attach` hook reads (`clientId=maws-main`, run 2). If an engine release ever stops exposing settings env to the worker, the same conformance case fails and the second transport takes over: a host-side `buildEnvironment` entry fed by the same decision, still never touching `extraSettings`. That is a change confined to `launch.ts` and the decision's output.

### 4. The grammar, version 2, and the marks' identity (X18; §4.4's revisit)

`to-human-parser.ts` gains `TO_HUMAN_GRAMMAR_VERSION = 2` and one change. Before tokenising, it computes the code regions of the whole text and ignores every tag token inside one. A code region is a fenced block (a line opening with three or more backticks or tildes, through the matching closing fence or the end of the text) or an inline code span (a run of N backticks through the next run of exactly N backticks in the same paragraph). A mark that contains a code block keeps it, because its own tags lie outside the fence. Everything else stays as S0: exact lowercase tags, flat choices, unbalanced marks literal, nested marks as separate runs. `preview.ts` reads through the same `parse`, so a preview never picks a mark quoted in code. Why: the contract tells the model to keep marks out of code, and an agent that explains the vocabulary quotes it in code. Version 1 would turn every such example into a question card and an attention item, which is §8's "false items" risk. The doperpowers mod still counts quoted tags, but that matters only in a terminal once it defers on MAWS. Stored history that quoted a tag in code now draws it as code, the intended change, and the version constant marks it.

A display-only leniency applies while a block streams. `parseLive(text)` treats a final unclosed mark as open, so a to-human card fills in as it arrives. It is never used for durable state.

**Identity.** `src/shared/owner/marks.ts` derives a block's marks from `parse`. A question is `{appSessionId, messageUuid, blockIndex, span}`, where `span` is the ordinal among the block's `need-input` parts. An essential uses the ordinal among its `essential` parts. `markIdOf` renders `${appSessionId}:${messageUuid}:${blockIndex}:${'q'|'e'}${span}`. The two kind-scoped ordinals keep a question's id stable when an essential is added to or removed from the same block. `questionHead(text)` is the doperpowers function, character for character (§1). The message uuid and block index come from `assistant.block.complete`, which the live stream and the disk backfill carry identically for a record. M4 pins that with a live journal against its transcript.

### 5. Main's owner channel (X18, X1; G6)

`src/main/owner/service.ts` starts after the index and beside the attention service, and listens to the same events: `session.eventBatch`, `session.queue`, `session.status`, `session.forgotten`.

**Derivation.** For each `assistant.block.complete` it considers only blocks that are text, from the main thread (`parentToolUseId === null`), not `aborted`, and not `isApiErrorMessage`. It parses the block's text with grammar 2 and upserts a row for each question and each essential into `owner_channel_marks`, idempotent on the mark id. A replay, a resync burst and a backfill re-deliver the same ids and change nothing. `supersedes` retires the marks of the replaced uuids. A `boundary.rewind` or `boundary.retraction` retires the marks of the messages it takes off the session's chain; M4 reads the events' fields and pins one case each. To-human and insight marks need no state and are not stored. Only finalized text counts: user prompts, tool output and subagent text never do.

**The watermark.** `owner_channel_sessions.enabled_at` is set at a decided launch whose lane is on when it is null, and cleared at a decided launch whose lane is off. A mark is *item-eligible* (`item = 1`) when its message time (the event's `at`) is at or after `enabled_at` at derivation. History written before the session's first lane-on launch in MAWS never raises an item. That covers the upgrade, a session first indexed later, and the part of a fork or side thread copied from its parent. History is still presented and answerable (§6, §8). A mark is *live* (`live = 1`) when its event came from a MAWS process (`seq !== null`, which includes E10's resync burst for what ran while the app was closed) while the lane was on and the session was not foreign-held. Only live marks may notify, once.

**The projection.** While the lane is on for a session, its item-eligible marks project into the attention service as a third list beside broker and derived. The attention service gains `setOwnerItems(appSessionId, items, silent: AttentionId[])`; silent ids join `restoredIds`, which the notifier already skips.

| mark state | item | notified when |
|---|---|---|
| question `unanswered`, session not foreign-held | `async_input`, pending | the mark is live and the item is new in this run |
| question `queued` or `delivered` | none (the answer is on its way or in) | — |
| essential `unread` | `essential_unread`, pending | the mark is live, the item is new, and the session is not foreign-held |
| essential `read` | none | — |

The item id is `${appSessionId}:${kind}:${markId}`. `createdAt` is the mark's time, and `anchor` is `{messageUuid, blockIndex, span}`. The summary is the kind's label and the mark's first line, so the OS notification's body reads "Question — Which backend should the board use?". A next turn retires neither kind (they stay out of `RETIRED_BY_NEXT_TURN`), and a running engine does not either. An archive retires the session's items as it does today; the mark rows stay. At start, the attention service skips persisted items of the two kinds, as it does broker items, and the owner service re-projects every session from its table with all ids silent. The table is the one truth, and `attention_items` holds a copy that is overwritten. "Foreign-held" today means `record.holder.kind` is `terminal` or `background_job`. When A lands, it is `availability === 'foreign_held'`. A contested session (`self` with `contestedBy`) is MAWS's and counts as held by MAWS. A change of holder re-projects that session, silently.

**Notifier and badge.** `GROUP` maps `async_input` to `asyncInput` and `essential_unread` to `essential`, the two new groups (§9). `ACTIONABLE_KINDS` gains both, so they count in the dock badge and stand in Needs you's tier 1, as the owner's §2 item 3 asks. The memory budget is untouched: its "waiting for you" hard reason comes from the host's pending requests, not attention, so a waiting question never keeps an engine alive.

### 6. Answering (X22 consumed; G4)

**The card's send.** A choice click sends `answerText(head, choice.text)` through `sendHumanPrompt(appSessionId, text, {origin: 'answer'})`. Reply opens a text field on the card and sends `answerText(head, text)` with `{origin: 'reply'}`. A multi-line text keeps its newlines after the header. After a Reply send, focus moves to the session's composer, whose draft was never touched. The renderer then reports the seam's result to main with `ownerChannel.answered {appSessionId, markId, text, origin, disposition, messageUuid?, queueId?, reason?}`. Main owns the state from there, because only main persists and sees every event.

**Correlation: the host says what happened to a prompt.** Inferring an answer's fate in main from queue snapshots and matching text loses identity. A hold claims a steer before it appends the held entry, two holds can finish in either order, a promotion removes an entry before its steer starts, a reconnect publishes the current queue before the missed lifecycle, a failed write or an exit's recovery replaces a submission's uuid, and two answers can share their text. The host's queue already journals `meta queue` rows for `release {queueId, messageUuid}` and `hold {queueId, messageUuid}`. C completes that record with one row per transition (M3) and has the normaliser emit each row as an X1 event, `queue.transition {action, queueId, messageUuid, reason?}`:

| action | ids | written by (`src/engine-host/queue.ts`) | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `release` | Q, U | `submit` (exists) | entry Q submitted as uuid U |
| `revert` | Q, U | `submit`'s failed persist or write | U never reached the engine; Q is queued again |
| `uncertain` | Q, U | `settleAfterExit` | U's submission was cut by the exit; Q is queued again, uncertain |
| `retire` | Q, U | `settleAfterExit` (released entries) | Q and U ended with the process |
| `drop` | Q, U, reason | the dropped-before-start path | Q and U ended: `cancelled`, `discarded` or `refused` |
| `withdraw` | U | `claim` (hold, the plain stop's hold-all, `cancelSteer`) | the host is withdrawing steer U; the engine's `cancelled` for U that follows is the host's own, not a drop |
| `giveback` | U | `giveBack` | the withdrawal did not happen; U is a pending steer again |
| `hold` | Q′, U | `appendHeld` (exists) | steer U withdrawn into a new entry Q′ |
| `cancel` | U | `cancelSteer` (new, below) | steer U withdrawn and dropped |
| `abandon` | U | `settleAfterExit`, before clearing the steers; and the queue's recovery of a state file whose pending steers belonged to a process that is gone | pending steer U ended with the process |
| `promote` | Q, U | `promote`, journaled before the steer's write | entry Q leaves the queue as steer U |
| `remove` | Q | `remove` | the human removed entry Q |

**An answer's members.** An answer in flight is tracked by its *members*, the entry ids and message uuids that have carried it, each `alive`, `withdrawing`, `dead` or `unknown`. The first member comes from the seam's result: the uuid for `sent` and `steered`, the entry id for `queued`. Every later member comes only from a `queue.transition` row, and text never establishes one. The association is two-way: when a row names any member of an answer, every id the row names becomes a member, with the life the row gives it. So a `release`, `revert` or `uncertain` naming a known U enrols its entry Q, just as one naming a known Q enrols its U. The lives each row gives:

| row | entry id | uuid |
|---|---|---|
| `release` | alive | alive |
| `revert` | alive | dead |
| `uncertain` | alive | `unknown` |
| `retire`, `drop` | dead | dead |
| `withdraw` | — | `withdrawing` |
| `giveback` | — | alive |
| `hold` | Q′ alive | dead |
| `cancel`, `abandon` | — | dead |
| `promote` | dead | alive |
| `remove` | dead | — |

A `command.lifecycle` of `started` or `completed` for a member uuid **delivers** the answer, and a delivered answer stays delivered. A lifecycle of `discarded` or `refused`, or of `cancelled` for a uuid that is not `withdrawing`, kills that uuid; `refused` also sets an in-memory `failed{refused}` note. A `cancelled` for a `withdrawing` uuid changes nothing. The host's next row for it decides the outcome: `hold` transfers it, `cancel` ends it, `giveback` returns it. A member is never killed on an event that only implies death. The host states every death as a row or a lifecycle, a pending steer's death at an exit included (`abandon`), so the live stream and the recovery read carry the same facts.

**Members added late.** Lifecycle evidence can precede the row that makes a uuid a member. A promotion's write can drain U's `started` before the queue returns (`queue.test.ts`, the promotion test), and `promote` is journaled before the write for that reason. Even so, every member added, live or during a recovery fold, is reconciled at once against the lifecycle evidence already seen for it in the session's log or in the folded history. A member added after its `started` therefore still delivers the answer.

The answer is **unanswered** again once no member is alive, withdrawing or unknown and none was delivered. An `unknown` member is settled by the transcript's evidence (below): found, delivered; not found, dead.

| state | persisted as | the card |
|---|---|---|
| `unanswered` | state, plus the in-memory `failed{reason}` of the last refused send | the choices and Reply; the failure's reason with Retry, Resume or Take over |
| `queued` | state, answer text, origin, members | "Queued — sends when the agent reaches a pause", with Cancel |
| `unconfirmed` | the same | "Sent — not confirmed", with Answer again (below) |
| `delivered` | state, answer text, origin, time | "Answered: <answer>"; Reply stays |
| `retired` | state | the message is gone from the chain (§5) |

**What "delivered" is.** It is the engine taking the prompt, not MAWS writing it. The host's normaliser emits `user.message` (`isReplay: false`, origin `human`) the moment the host writes the frame, and suppresses the engine's `--replay-user-messages` echo (`src/engine-host/normalizer.ts`, `userOut` and the `pendingHumanUuids` check). A `next` steer the engine later cancels has that event and no other (`pass3/steer-next-cancelled.jsonl`: out, `queued`, `cancelled`). So delivery is the engine's lifecycle, `started` or `completed`, which both the idle send and the folded steer have (`pass3/steer-next.jsonl`). The transcript's evidence is the engine's own record: a `user` record with that uuid. M4 opens by recording a cancelled steer with the transcript kept, to confirm that a withdrawn prompt leaves no `user` record. If it does leave one, the evidence narrows to a `user` record that a later record names as its `parentUuid`. The same evidence serves legacy recognition. §4.3 and X22 call the user message's appearance "delivered", and P2-0's `waitForHumanPromptDelivery` awaits the `user.message` event, which fires at the write. Hand-back item 1 routes the correction to the architect for P2-0 and E1. C reads the lifecycle itself and does not wait on it.

**Late reports.** The renderer's report reaches main after the seam answers. By then the engine may have started, cancelled or refused the prompt, and the host may have promoted or held it (`queue.test.ts` covers a cancel drained before `steer()` resolves). Main therefore keeps, per session for the last ten minutes, every `queue.transition` and `command.lifecycle` event it received, up to 2 048. When a report arrives, its first member is replayed through that log in event order, so the answer lands in its true current state, whichever of these came first.

**Restarts and reconnects: the recovery read.** Main does not see what happened while it was down. That covers an app closed while E10's supervisor kept the session, a crash, or a session that exited meanwhile. E10's resync folds only the digest's events, and the queue's current snapshot arrives before the missed lifecycle (`src/engine-host/session-stream.ts`), so neither is a barrier for C. C adds a host read over the journals the host already retains (20 launches or 30 days): `session.promptHistory {appSessionId, since}` → `{events: (queue.transition | command.lifecycle)[], complete: boolean}`. It is read through the normaliser in launch order and is the same journal reader P2-A builds for X17's recovery, shared. At start and at every host reconnect, for each session holding a `queued` or `unconfirmed` answer, the owner service reads the history since its oldest such answer and folds it through the member rules. Until that read has been folded, it draws no negative conclusion for that session's answers. Only then does it apply the current `session.queue.list` snapshot, positively: an entry it shows is alive. When `complete` is false (the journals rotated), every member the history could not settle is checked against the transcript's evidence. A member still unresolved leaves the answer `unconfirmed`, never unanswered and never delivered by a guess. "Answer again" on an `unconfirmed` card sends a new answer, and the old record is kept for the transcript's evidence to settle later.

**Seam results without an identifier.** P2-0's seam returns `started` with neither a uuid nor an entry id when it resumes a released session and sends (`send-human-prompt.ts` at a3379850). C cannot correlate that answer, so it is recorded `unconfirmed` at once. Legacy recognition (a unique head) may still settle it. Hand-back item 1 asks that `started` carry the uuid or entry id of the prompt it sent, which A's acquisition must preserve. With it, the answer is correlated like any other.

**Legacy recognition** is the conservative fallback the section asks for. It reads answers given in a terminal through the doperpowers mod, and answers whose report never arrived (a renderer that died between send and report). A human prompt the engine took (by the evidence above) settles a question as `delivered`, origin `legacy`, when it carries a line `Answering "<head>": …` and exactly one question of the session has that head, was asked before it, and is `unanswered` or `unconfirmed`. A prompt whose uuid is a member of any answer is never read this way. Two questions with one head are never resolved by it, so "two identical questions stay distinct" rests on members alone.

**Cancel.** `ownerChannel.cancel {appSessionId, markId}` is main's. It acts on the answer's live members:
- For an alive entry Q still `queued` in the host, it calls `session.queue.remove {queueId: Q}`; an entry already `submitting` or `released` answers `already_delivering`.
- For an alive steer U, it calls a new host command, `session.steer.cancel {messageUuid: U}` → `{outcome: 'cancelled' | 'already_started'}`. The command uses the hold's claim and `cancel_async_message`, then drops the steer without appending a held entry or releasing the queue's head, and journals `cancel`.

`hold` followed by `remove` would not be safe: `hold` may release the held entry into a turn that ended during the cancel, and `remove` then refuses it (`queue.test.ts`, the hold whose turn ended mid-cancel), so the answer would be sent by its own cancellation. Each command's success is also the row (`remove` or `cancel`) that kills the member. The answer becomes unanswered by the member rules, which apply to the same answer only. `already_started` and `already_delivering` leave it queued for the lifecycle to deliver, and a failed command leaves it unchanged. The claim keeps U in `withdrawn`, so the transcript hides the dropped bubble as it does for a held steer. Cancelling from the composer's queue tail goes through the composer's own actions and reaches the same rows. A delivered question keeps Reply for a follow-up answer, which replaces the stored answer, and drops its choice buttons.

**An exited or foreign-held session.** Until A lands, the seam refuses on an exited session as the composer does. The card shows the refusal with the existing Resume action, and after A it shows "Starting…" and the answer follows (`started`). In a foreign-held session the card shows no choice buttons and no Reply, only "This session is open in a terminal" with the existing Take over.

### 7. The report view and the two-level fold (§4.6; G3)

The view is a per-tab flag in the transcript store's UI state, `view: 'report' | 'transcript'`, kept in memory. A persisted preference is §9's deferral. It defaults to `report` when the lane is on and `transcript` otherwise. A two-state control in the session pane's header switches it (open question 2). ### 10. The index migration (X15 slot): why `attention_items` does not suffice

`attention_items` holds whole lists per session, replaced at every change, of items that wait on someone. It cannot hold what C must keep after an item is gone: a delivered question's answer and its correlation (uuid or queue id), an essential's read time, the per-session override, the watermark and the launch record. Its rows also vanish when an item retires. Encoding these in its columns would overload `request_id` and `tool_use_id`, and an archive's retire would delete them. One migration therefore adds two tables. The attention items stay a projection of them.

```sql
CREATE TABLE owner_channel_sessions (app_session_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES sessions(app_id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  override TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'inherit' CHECK (override IN ('inherit','on','off')),
  enabled_at TEXT,                      -- the watermark (Design §5); null while off or never launched with the lane on
  launch_id TEXT, launched_at TEXT,     -- the last decided launch
  desired TEXT CHECK (desired IN ('maws-contract','external-compatible-style','off')),
  recorded TEXT,                        -- 'unrecorded' | 'contract:<n>' | 'none' at that launch
  appended INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, snapshot_off INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  off_unavailable INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, -- "off" was wanted and the binary lacks it (§2; added at M2's review, 2026-10-09)
  applied TEXT CHECK (applied IN ('maws-contract','external-compatible-style','off')),
  style TEXT, observed_style TEXT, style_mismatch INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0);
CREATE TABLE owner_channel_marks (id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  app_session_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES sessions(app_id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  kind TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('need-input','essential')),
  message_uuid TEXT NOT NULL, block_index INTEGER NOT NULL, span INTEGER NOT NULL, grammar INTEGER NOT NULL,
  head TEXT NOT NULL, at TEXT NOT NULL, item INTEGER NOT NULL, live INTEGER NOT NULL,
  state TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (state IN ('unanswered','queued','unconfirmed','delivered','unread','read','retired')),
  answer_text TEXT, answer_origin TEXT CHECK (answer_origin IN ('answer','reply','legacy')),
  answer_members TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]',   -- JSON: [{kind: 'entry'|'uuid', id, life: 'alive'|'withdrawing'|'dead'|'unknown'}] (§6)
  answered_at TEXT, read_at TEXT, state_changed_at TEXT NOT NULL);
CREATE INDEX owner_channel_marks_session ON owner_channel_marks(app_session_id, state);
```

An older build that meets the new version opens the index read-only and unavailable, the existing rule for every migration (`openDb`). Nothing older reads these tables.

### 11. The doperpowers change (a proposal; the architect makes it a PR in the owner's repository)

The smallest diff that keeps the terminal UI and the other mods, and hands the to-human UI to MAWS when it runs under MAWS. Base: the owner's repository at e96479ec (7.141.4).

1. `output-styles/to-human.md`: one front-matter line, `maws-to-human-contract: 1`. The body already carries contract 1's semantics, and its answer form is the mod's. U2 shows a plugin style with the key loads, is reported by `system/init`, and passes `claude plugin validate`.
2. `hooks/mods/to-human.tsx`: the variable is read once per worker and every hook the view registers passes through on a non-terminal surface when it is set. A sketch, which the PR writes against the file:
   ```tsx
   // MAWS draws this channel itself when it says so (MAWS_NATIVE_TO_HUMAN, the contract version it presents); the
   // terminal keeps this view. Read once per worker; every hook awaits the same answer.
   let maws: Promise<boolean> | undefined
   const native = async ($: EngineInterface, e: { surface?: string }) =>
     (await (maws ??= $.env.get('MAWS_NATIVE_TO_HUMAN').then((value) => value !== undefined && value !== ''))) && e.surface !== 'terminal'
   ```
   Inside `registerToHuman(on)`, each `ui.render` hook (UserMessage, TurnDuration, AssistantMessage, ToolUse, ToolGroup, ToolResult, AbovePrompt), each `ui.press` hook, and the `prompt.submit` hook start with `if (await native($, e)) return next(e)`. The hooks that are synchronous today become `async`, as AssistantMessage already is. Where an event carries no surface, the variable alone decides, which is safe because only MAWS ever sets it. The literal `'MAWS_NATIVE_TO_HUMAN'` keeps the read declared (validate lists it under the module's env reads). A `session.start` hook may prime the read so the first render already knows. The PR also bumps the plugin to 7.142.0 in `plugin.json` and `marketplace.json`, the repository's convention.
3. `hooks/to-human-stream.sh`: after `set -uo pipefail`, `[[ -n ${MAWS_NATIVE_TO_HUMAN:-} ]] && exit 0`. An exit with no output leaves the batch as the engine drew it, as the script already does for unmarked text. Command hooks see the variable (U2), and under MAWS the hook would otherwise spawn bash and jq per flush for a drawing MAWS no longer shows.
4. `hooks/mods/register.tsx`: no change. Registration runs before any `$` exists, so the decision must live in the view's hooks. Gating `registerToHuman` there would need `process.env`, which the sandboxed worker does not offer.

### 12. Failure semantics and edge cases

| case | behaviour |
|---|---|
| the style cannot be resolved (unreadable settings, a missing plugin, an unknown name) | desired `maws-contract` (§2), the reason in the launch record; the init check catches the misreading |
| the transcript to classify is missing or unreadable | `unrecorded`; the launch's own flags decide |
| the binary does not declare `--system-prompt-snapshot` | no "off"; applied stays recorded; "after this session's next compaction" |
| the lane switched while a process runs | presentation at once; instructions and variable at the next launch; the running process is never changed (§4.5) |
| a mark inside a code region | literal (grammar 2) |
| an aborted or refused-and-superseded block | no mark, or its marks retired |
| a rewind or retraction | the marks of the messages it removes retired |
| compaction | marks keep their rows; the transcript keeps the messages; nothing changes |
| a fork or side thread | marks copied from the parent fall before its watermark: presented, answerable, no items |
| a renderer crash between send and report | no member exists; legacy recognition settles a unique head once the engine takes the prompt; otherwise the question reads unanswered and its answer is visible in the transcript |
| a host write fails, or an exit leaves an entry uncertain | `revert` or `uncertain` rows; the entry stays an alive member and its next `release` adds the new uuid (§6) |
| the app closed or crashed while an answer moved | the recovery read (`session.promptHistory`) folds what happened before any negative conclusion; rotated journals and unsettled members leave it `unconfirmed` |
| Cancel races the engine taking the steer | `session.steer.cancel` answers `already_started`; the card reads "Already on its way" and the lifecycle delivers it |
| the seam answers `started` without an identifier | `unconfirmed`; a unique head settles it; hand-back item 1 asks the seam for the id |
| the same answer clicked twice | the buttons are disabled from Sending until unanswered again |
| a foreign-held session | presentation; no `async_input`; essential items silent; Take over on the card |
| the window focused when an item arrives | no notification (the notifier's rule); the badge counts it |
| notifications unsupported (an unsigned build) | the notifier's fallback (dock bounce and `attention.notified {delivery: 'fallback'}`) |
| the lane off | P1's behaviour exactly; mark rows kept and items withdrawn; re-enabling re-projects silently |

## Risks

1. **The engine's snapshot rule changes, or the flag disappears.** It is undocumented and new. Mitigations: the binary-declaration gate, which never breaks a launch; the decision table is safe if a future engine honours a new append on resume (an extra "off" only costs one render); M2's conformance tests on the U2 shapes; the re-probe step at every engine bump. Residual: a silently changed segment shape would read as `none` and cost renders. The classifier's tests say which shape they pin.
2. **Two views on the owner's machine until the doperpowers PR merges.** Before it merges, the lane-on variable is ignored by the mod, which keeps folding user and tool rows through the surface and drawing its own band. Mitigation: the PR merges and the plugin is updated before C lands (hand-back item 4). If it cannot, C lands with the lane's default on, and the mods row says "a plugin may also draw its own to-human view" when `system/init`'s `output_style` names a plugin style that does not declare the key. That is a label, never a precedence rule (Decision Log 4).
3. **Doubled instructions** before the PR. The owner's style carries the semantics without declaring the key, so the owner's sessions get MAWS's contract beside the style. The semantics agree, so the harm is tokens. The PR's key removes the cause, and §2's table then clears the contract.
4. **Prompt-cache cost** of "off" launches for sessions whose recorded state differs, until their next compaction. This is bounded to state changes: the lane switched for a session, old sessions resumed with the lane on (recorded `none`), and, once the PR lands, sessions of the owner's style that recorded MAWS's contract meanwhile. The owner's style sessions created before C recorded `none`. With the PR their desired state is `external-compatible-style`, which needs no "off" for `none`, so they pay nothing.
5. **Delivery read wrongly.** Reading the host's write as delivery would mark answers delivered that the engine later cancels. §6 reads the engine's `started` instead, and M4 tests through the real normaliser. The disk rule waits on M4's recorded cancelled-steer check.
6. **Model compliance.** An agent that marks nothing draws as the full transcript, so nothing is hidden. An agent that marks some of a turn and leaves the rest plain has the plain part under the record row, whose label counts it. The contract's wording was tested (U2 S13). A regression shows in the live run's transcript.
7. **Attention noise.** Two kinds only, the watermark, live-only notification, silent hydration, foreign-held suppression, and grammar 2.
8. **Lost correlation.** Members come only from the host's own rows, and the recovery read covers what main missed. What still cannot be settled reads `unconfirmed`, never a guess. Legacy recognition settles only a unique head.
9. **Version slots.** C, B and #90 all migrate. Slots are renumbered at rebase (§4.9).
10. **Reading large transcripts at launch.** The tail scan with the per-path cache. A first full scan of a 50 MB transcript is a few hundred milliseconds inside a launch that already waits on the shell capture.
