## 1. Purpose

**Higher purpose.** MAWS is a general workstation for supervising Claude Code agents, built first to the baseline of three reference apps and then past it, the owner's own workflows arriving as plugins on a strong base (charter §1, D-5, D-20). P0 reached the baseline on daily use; P1 moved everything the agent workflow needed out of the terminal and into the app (P1 §15, closed 2026-10-08).

**What this phase is for.** The owner started living in MAWS at P1's close, and the first days of use found the surfaces where Claude Desktop is still better: a discovered session answers "not running" to every control until a manual Resume; `/plugins` and `/skills` are terminal menus in a dialog where Desktop has a library; `/resume` is a flat list where Desktop has a picker with search, groups and a preview; the to-human lane is a plugin's drawing that MAWS cannot answer, fold or notify from; a comment on a deliverable opens a whole session view where Desktop opens a small comment thread beside the element. P2 fixes the daily driver on those surfaces. Each child builds Desktop's composition where Desktop's is better (D-20) and MAWS's own where the owner's notes ask for it.

**The measure (Decision Log 1).** On the surfaces the owner uses every day, MAWS is at least as good as Claude Desktop, and nothing in daily use sends the owner to Desktop or to a terminal. The owner named what still did on 2026-10-05 and 2026-10-08: sessions that say they are not running, the plugins and skills interface, the resume list, to-human as a plugin's view, comments opening as a new tab.

**What it must not be.** Not Desktop's services: no claude.ai Docs, Artifacts or Cowork (P1 P-4 stands). Not a plugin platform: one built-in, disableable communication mod is application behaviour, not an API beside the engine's `ui_surface_v1` (D-9). Not a redesign: the shell, the transcript and the composer stay as P0 and P1 built them. Not the co-work layer: the docs, slides and design editors, the design system, a Spaces-like organiser and stored comment threads with persistent pins are P3 (charter §3 as amended today). Not the browser's top chrome: the bookmarks bar, the extensions bar, the avatar and the profile popover are the cua_repl session's work on the cua repository's board #90, landing on this master beside P2.

## 4. Design (thin; the shared surface, graded)

Everything below is advisory inheritance unless marked **[binding]** with its joint-view reason. Children expand their own sections in place; a child that overturns advisory content records a dated line in §12 under its id.

**4.1 Availability, apart from process status** **[binding — A, B, C and D all read it, and the owner's decision 4 fixes the policy].** Main derives one `availability` per session record from the record, the holder registry and the host's process status: `usable | starting | resumable | foreign_held | archived | missing_directory | needs_trust | failed`. Process absence is not an error. One acquisition entry in main (X16; `session.acquire`, or `session.start` extended to join) is what every engine-needing action calls: it joins a launch already starting instead of refusing it, runs the existing guarded resume with E13's `browserBackend`, and answers the same outcome shape to every waiter so each executes its intended operation once. Opening a transcript and reading cached facts acquire nothing; a Send, a chip change, a fresh read, `/btw`, Send to main, a peer reply and a resubmit acquire. The memory budget and keep-alive rules of charter §4.4 are unchanged; no fixed idle timer while an app is connected; no engine per sidebar row; no wake from mounted widgets or polls. A terminal-held session stays read-only with the existing Take over (owner decision 4). The C10 restart rule "never auto-resumes" means no blanket replay of indexed history, not a ban on owner-action-driven acquisition (A records the charter line).

**4.2 Observed facts** **[binding on the owner and the exposure; advisory on fields].** The host sidecar owns a last-observed engine-facts snapshot per session (X17): launch id, engine version, observed-at, effective model, effort and mode, `output_style`, the loaded plugin list, the last context reading, each field known or unknown. It is written from `system/init`, `reload_plugins` and the context sample; recovered once from the raw journal for sessions MAWS ran before; absent for terminal-only sessions. Main exposes it as a snapshot read beside `session.digests`. Surfaces label freshness ("Loaded last time, <when>" against "Loaded now"); unknown is never shown as empty; a record's defaults are never labelled observed; an old effective mode never confirms a new requested one.

**4.3 The send path** **[binding — C's answers and E1's card send through it, and A makes it resumptive].** Every human-origin send (composer Send, a choice click, the question card's Reply, the comment card's Reply) goes through the composer's submit path to `session.send` with the queue's semantics (`priority: 'next'` while a turn runs, as C7 landed them). Answers are human prompts, never plugin-origin submissions. A's acquisition makes that path continue any eligible record; until A lands, a send on an exited session behaves as today.

**4.4 The owner channel** **[binding — one grammar feeds presentation, previews and attention across C, D and E1].** The four marks (`to-human`, `essential`, `insight`, `need-input` with flat `choice` lines, one `recommended`) have one grammar, `src/shared/owner/to-human-parser.ts`, versioned; only finalized assistant text produces durable items, never user prompts or tool output. A question's identity is `(appSessionId, source message uuid, block index, span ordinal)`, not its first line. Question states: unanswered, queued, delivered (answered), with a cancel returning to unanswered; an essential is unread until Mark read (owner decision 3). Two attention kinds, `async_input` and `essential_unread`, each with a source anchor; the engine may still be running while a question waits; an unrelated next turn retires neither. OS notifications for those two kinds only; hydration and replay are silent; nothing is notified for history on upgrade. The grammar question "tags inside code count" is revisited by C before default notifications, versioned.

**4.5 The contract and its advertisement** **[binding on the mechanism; advisory on wording].** MAWS carries a short versioned contract text of its own preserving the style's semantics (to-human spans are the delivered messages; essential is must-not-miss; need-input is one question per mark with choices; insight teaches; keep the coding instructions). It is appended exactly once per MAWS-owned launch, resume and fork through `--append-system-prompt`, resolved centrally in the launch mapping, unless the launch's effective settings select a compatible style (`doperpowers:to-human`, recognised by the installed plugin and style name, the plugin's manifest version as the compatibility signal), in which case nothing is appended and the launch records `source: 'external-compatible-style'`; the observed `output_style` in `system/init` is compared and a mismatch surfaced. Whenever the native lane is on, MAWS sets `MAWS_NATIVE_TO_HUMAN=<contract version>` in the launch environment through the existing `extraSettings.env` seam; the doperpowers mod reads it once and passes its to-human drawing, its toggle and its question presses through, keeping its terminal UI and its other mods (a small change in the owner's repository, prepared by the architect as a PR for the owner's review; owner decision 4 of this cut). A per-app default on and a per-session override set or omit the variable and the prompt at the next owned launch; nothing is written under `~/.claude`; a foreign-live session is never changed. Delegated unknown U2 verifies the mechanism before C binds its details.

**4.6 The report view** **[binding on ownership; advisory on layout].** MAWS owns the owner-channel presentation on every surface, live or detached, attached or not: `Rows.tsx:47`'s exclusivity ends when the native lane is on. Message cards for to-human; the essential as a prominent must-read item with unread state; the insight adjacent to the message on wide layouts and an inline aside on narrow ones; the question as one card with the recommended choice marked, the choice buttons, a Reply action opening a field on the card, and the answered state. The fold is two-level: everything between the prompt and the answer folds into one working record; clicking it shows the ordinary collapsed divider ("Worked for 10s · 2 tool calls"); clicking that expands the tool rows as the full transcript draws them. Report is the default view for an enabled session, with the full transcript one toggle away per tab; untagged imported history is not hidden before the contract was in effect.

**4.7 The comment card** **[binding on the seam, the shared component and the artifact scope; advisory on dimensions].** One `CommentCard` behind X10's seam, chosen by an additive `placement` hint ({kind: 'card', host: 'deliverable' | 'browser', rect?} or {kind: 'sheet'}, the sheet staying for the code gutter). The card holds the composer (anchor label, screenshot thumbnail, the Side thread or Send to main control) and, once a thread exists, the thread: its rows from the anchored prompt on, drawn through MAWS's transcript rows and the owner channel's presentation, a Reply field sending through §4.3, a needs-you state when the thread's agent raises a permission, a question or a trust card (one click to the Side pane or a tab), Close and Escape; "Open as tab" stays. The Side pane keeps its listing and re-docks the card. The fork and both sends are unchanged (X11; the owner's "no change in backend"). In the deliverable viewer the card is positioned from the pick's `rect` inside the viewer body (renderer DOM, no cover protocol) and follows the viewer's scale when the viewer gains fit and zoom. In the browser pane the card is docked in the pane's own box, never over the native view (the cover protocol is not used for a card); the pin's position arrives as an additive field on `browser.comment.picked`. A side thread sees its parent's artifacts: `artifact_update` on the parent's id creates the parent's next version and `artifact_list` lists them (the A-39 tab-sharing rule applied to artifacts; amends P1's per-session artifact scoping; Decision Log 9, flagged for the owner).

**4.8 Library dialogs** **[binding on the rule; advisory on sizes].** Widgets gain a second size, library, for `/plugins` and `/skills` (about `min(960px, 100vw-48px)` by `85vh`), still centred over the window, still one open widget with focus returning to the composer (charter §5.4 stands); B records the Decision Log line under D-20 and §5.4. **4.9 Versions.** Settings and index numbers are allocated in the P1 ledger's §4 table, the repository's single allocation table (X15), in landing order and after cua board #90's reservations (settings 12, index 7, `profiles.json` 2).
