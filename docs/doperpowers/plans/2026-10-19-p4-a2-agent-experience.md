## Design (the residue, decided here)

**R1. The event's shape (X35).** One batch streams several ops, each with its own target, so the frame carries the batch's ops in op order rather than one target and one text: `{toolUseId, appSessionId, artifactId, kind, state: 'streaming' | 'committed' | 'dropped', commitSeq?, at, name, color, ops: ProvisionalOp[]}` (`name` and `color` the agent as presence names it, `actorDisplay` over the session actor, so the layer is drawn and labelled as the committed write will be) where a `ProvisionalOp` is one of `replace_text {block: string | null, old, text}`, `replace_block {block, text}`, `insert {after?, before?, text}`, `set_cell {table, row, col, text}`, `delete {block}`, `move {block}`; `text` is the op's cumulative streamed `new` or `markdown` so far, so a frame replaces the one before it. The ledger's `target: {block?} | {paths?} | {view?}` is kept as the per-op target for G2's kinds (`paths` for a publish's files, `view` for a dataset) in the shared type `src/shared/artifacts/provisional.ts`. Reported for the ledger.

**R2. The channel.** The frame rides the document port as a host-only frame `MSG_PROVISIONAL = 102` (`[102, json]`), decoded by the provider and raised through `DocumentHandle.onProvisional(listener)`. One port carries the Y frames and the layer, so their order is the port's order and the composition hold sees both.

**R3. Rule 2's mechanism.** `update` frames gain `seq` (the row's global seq) and `step2` gains `revision` (the doc's revision at the diff), additive; the provider keeps `integrated`, the highest seq it has applied through content frames (never advanced by an ack, which passes the hold), exposed in `SaveState.integrated`. The host sends the `committed {commitSeq}` frame from the write's own frame, queued **ahead of the write's update frames** in the service outbox (`DocumentService.announceWrite`, valid only inside a write), so it is revealed with the commit and arrives first; the renderer records the pending withdrawal and withdraws in the provider's state listener once `integrated >= commitSeq`, in the same task as the update's application. Under the hold the `committed` frame passes (it is not content) and the update is buffered; at release the update applies and the withdrawal runs in the same `process()`: no gap, no flash, and nothing vanishes during a composition. A reconnect's `step2` carries `revision >= commitSeq` and withdraws the same way. An X24 additive touch; reported for the ledger.

**R4. What passes the hold.** `streaming` and `dropped` frames pass the hold like awareness: the layer keeps moving while the owner composes elsewhere (rule 4 forbids the composing block, not the page), and a refusal withdraws at once (the owner accepted a seen-then-withdrawn batch). Only the withdrawal on commit waits, through R3.

**R5. The target.** A `replace_text` without `block` is resolved on the host once `old` has parsed (it precedes `new` in the schema and in every observed call): the first top-level block whose text holds the quote, through `DocIndex`; none found, `block: null` and the renderer draws nothing for that op (the transcript shows the call). Named blocks are passed as named. `delete` and `move` stream no text; presence outlines them.

**R6. Presence.** From the frame in which `artifact` parses, the emitter publishes `{...actorDisplay(actor), editing: {blocks}}` on the session's replica (`store.service.replica`, held with `holdPresence` so a turn end mid-stream leaves it to the hold) and extends `blocks` as targets resolve; the tool's own commit publishes its cursor and blocks as today and the 4 s hold after the answer is unchanged. A `dropped` releases the presence to the idle name. The actor and colour are the committed write's (`actorDisplay` over the session actor).

**R7. Suggest mode.** `mode` follows `ops` in the schema; when `mode: 'suggest'` parses (or the artifact's `agentEditsAsSuggestions` is set at the first frame), the layer's doc is the suggestion doc: a layer already drawn on the main doc is dropped there and continues on the suggestion doc.

**R8. When the layer ends.** `committed` from the tool's `inFrame`; `dropped` from the tool's refusal (every refusal path, including a write that failed), from a `tool.result` for the id that no `committed` preceded (the engine denied or the model abandoned the call), from the turn's end (`turn.ended`) and from the process's exit. A call that never arrives (the stream aborted by an interrupt) ends at the turn's end.

**R9. The parser.** A tolerant partial-JSON reader (`src/shared/maws/partial-json.ts`): the accumulated text is completed (an open string closed, a dangling key or comma dropped, open containers closed, a cut `\u` escape dropped) and parsed; run per 16 ms batch tick, not per delta.

**R10. The layer's drawing (the document editor).** A Tiptap extension `Provisional` beside `Presence`: `replace_text` dims the matched `old` range inline and draws the streamed text after it in the agent's colour; `replace_block` and `set_cell` draw a labelled provisional block after the target (the target dimmed for `replace_block`); `insert` draws it after or before the anchor. Widgets are non-editable, take no focus and no selection; an op whose target is the block the owner is composing in is not drawn while the composition stands (the plugin tracks `compositionstart`/`compositionend`) and is drawn after. The decoration set changing calls the editor's `moved`, so `layoutKey` bumps once per animation frame; nothing writes Y, no version, provenance or history changes (rule 1). The label is the agent's name as presence names it.

**R11. The guard's home.** `test/live/support/tool-time.ts` holds the constants, `readToolSpans` (built here to the ledger's shape because A1 has not landed; the architect keeps one when both have) and `expectToolTime`; the seven live specs call `guardToolTime(journalPath)` after their last turn. The heavy fixture is `e2e/tool-time-heavy-live.spec.ts` as the ledger names it: a Playwright spec that launches no app and drives the real tool server through `scriptedSession` over a temporary store, added to gate.yml's `LIVE_E2E` and `package.json` so it runs on every gate that runs live specs. The render bound is set from this branch's live timings (M8) or, failing a run, from the latest recorded live journals, stated in the hand-back.

**R12. The layer's e2e.** The perf seam gains `stream {session, toolUseId, name, deltas, tickMs?}`, feeding the session's emitter the partial-JSON pieces as the normaliser would, so `e2e/document-provisional.spec.ts` (group `document-app`) proves the four rules without a model; the paid run (M8) proves them on the installed engine.

### Design (decided here)

**G-R1. A publish's frame carries the files that changed since the call's last frame.** A `streaming` frame of an `artifact_publish` lists, in input order, a `file {path, text}` op for each file whose text grew or appeared since the frame before (the op's shape unchanged); the store keys a layer's `file` ops by path, a later text replacing the earlier and a new path appended, and every other op list still replaces the one before (R1). The `committed` frame names the seq and carries no new files. While the layer streams, every file but the last path is whole (a JSON object's members stream one after another) and the last is open; at the commit all are whole. Why: R1's cumulative frames would send a whole-deck publish (the heavy fixture's 100 slides, about 1 MB) again at every tick to every connection, the host's own agent replicas included, a cost Decision Log 8 named; per-path frames send each file's growth once. A view that attaches mid-stream (a tab opened on the artifact during the call, a reconnect, a second window) draws every file streamed so far: when a connection of a doc completes its sync, the host sends that connection alone one complete frame per open call on the doc (every file so far), after its step 2, so the layer starts from the replica's state; a reconnect drops its streaming layers first (`layer.ts`) and takes them back from that frame. The host, not the store, restates them, because only the host holds the call's input, and asking for it would be a second channel beside the port. An X35 amendment of this child's own R1, reported for the ledger (the architect's condition, 2026-10-22: proven by a test that attaches after N files have streamed and sees all N).

**G-R2. What of a file is drawn: a slide's or a board's element tree as far as it parses, over the live slide.** The loader reads the file's text so far (parse5 drops a start tag the text ends inside, so an element appears once its tag closes; its text grows as it streams). An open file's tail is the live slide's: an element the text has not reached yet is the live one in its live place (children of a container both hold merged the same way, by id; a table's cells by position in their row), so an unfinished file never reads as removals. A whole file is the slide: a live element it leaves out will be removed. The result is compared with the live slide element by element: **changed** (tag, style, attributes, text or raw markup differ), **new** (no live element of that id; an element the agent left without an id is new), **removed** (whole files only), the rest unchanged; an open element whose text is still a prefix of the live text, with the same style and attributes, is unchanged until it diverges. Not drawn, shown at the swap: a slide's own section style and attributes, its notes, the index's keys other than `order` (a new slide's place) and a new board's frame (faces, sections, design-system overrides, titles), a file the loader would hold, and a file sent as `null`. The provisional slide is drawn by the canvas's own code over a scratch doc (the deck's publish writing the provisional file into it, property by property), so what the layer draws is what the commit will draw.

**G-R3. On the stage and on a board the layer draws only what differs, beneath the human's states.** A **ghost canvas** (a `SlideCanvas` over the scratch doc, at the live canvas's scale, between the live canvas and the overlay, `pointer-events: none`, `aria-hidden`, `inert`, no `describe`, so no node in it takes focus or a name) shows the changed and new elements and the elements the provisional layout moves (their box in the ghost differs from their live box after both layout passes), everything else in it hidden with its layout kept; the live canvas **veils** the same elements and the removed ones (hidden, layout kept). Changed and new elements carry the agent's colour as an outline, and the slide carries the label "<name> is writing…" in that colour at its top left from the frame that names its file, before any element differs. Why only what differs and not the whole provisional slide drawn opaque over the live one: the human's ARMED and EDIT text box is inside the live canvas's node, so an opaque layer above the live canvas would cover it, which rule 4 forbids; drawing the differences leaves every other pixel the live slide's, and keeps the overlay's frames and handles true to what they surround.

**G-R4. Rule 4 on the canvas: the human's elements are protected.** Protected: the element in EDIT, the ARMED element, every selected element while a gesture drags, and (through ARMED and EDIT) the element an input method composes in. A protected element is never veiled or ghosted, and a ghost element whose box meets a protected element's box, or whose subtree holds it, is held back (not drawn) until the protection ends, then drawn at the next state change. The swap needs nothing new: the commit's update is held with the Y frames under a composition or a drag (B2's one hold with two reasons), so `integrated >= commitSeq` and with it the withdrawal wait for the release, and the release applies the write and withdraws the layer in one task. The layer is driven imperatively from the store's and the session's listeners (as `SlideCanvas` is from its observer), not through a React render, so the withdrawal, the unveiling and the write's DOM patch land in the same task.

**G-R5. The filmstrip shows every slide being written.** A slide with a file in a layer shows its whole provisional slide over its thumbnail (opaque: a thumbnail holds no text box) inside a ring in the agent's colour; a slide file new to the deck shows as a ghost entry in the filmstrip at its place in the streamed `order` (after the last slide until `order` names it), not a button, not sortable, taking no focus, withdrawn in the task the real slide's entry arrives. ## Surprises & Discoveries

1. **The engine names a `maws` tool_use block `mcp__maws__<tool>`** (M4; verified against `spikes/protocol-probe/fixtures/pass3/*`: every `content_block_start` of a MAWS tool carries the qualified name). 2. **Names stream through their prefixes** (M4): the partial reader closes an open string, so `artifact: "plan"` is read while `"plan-2"` streams and a block id `p1` while `p12` does.
