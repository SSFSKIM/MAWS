## 1. Purpose

**Higher purpose.** MAWS is a general workstation for supervising Claude Code agents, built to the baseline of the reference apps and then past it (charter §1, D-5, D-20). P0 reached the baseline, P1 moved the agent workflow out of the terminal, P2 made the daily surfaces at least as good as Claude Desktop's, P3 added the co-work layer: documents, decks, designs, design systems and dashboards with a store, a protocol and a history. The owner's verdict at P3's sitting (P3 §13, M.1): "not yet … the structure is very solid … we just have to smooth things out". No task named still needs Claude Desktop or a terminal; what remains is that the surfaces are not yet fluid.

**What this phase is for.** P4 makes daily use fluid, fast and unsurprising on every surface the owner uses daily, the five artifact editors and the session-supervising surfaces alike (the sidebar, the transcript, tabs, the panel, notifications, the agents views), at the level of Google Docs, Google Slides, Canva, Claude Desktop and Codex (charter §3 P4; the owner, Decision Log 1). Where a reference does a thing well, MAWS does the same thing, established from the reference's own behaviour (`research/p4-ux-prior-art.md`: the vendor's documentation, open-source implementations and the readable sources, with live checks where nothing is published) and then proven on MAWS itself by live user tests: fresh agents operating a dedicated build through `cua_repl` in the owner's real scenarios, using every corner, trying to break it, comparing the same task in the reference app, and reporting to the owner, who gives the verdict (Decision Log 1, 3). The phase starts from P3's carried list and the owner's nine observations: comments beside the passage, click-to-edit on slides and designs, the main-area float, cross-surface tab drag, artifact search, the per-session launch profile, the inline artifact link, the direct send from the editor header, Drive through the gws CLI, Google Fonts fetched once.

**The measure (Decision Log 1, 3, 8, 10).** "Done to this extent": the design items landed; the agent's edit shown as it is written, with MAWS's own tool time under a guard the paid live specs enforce (the owner's worry was MAWS's tool performance and nothing else, Decision Log 8) and the parity question against Claude Desktop answered by one bounded bench whose result decides whether the deferred edit-protocol levers return (Decision Log 10); the debt waves landed beside the design; each of seven surfaces passed its loop (an assessment round, the owner's verdict, an enhancement wave, a re-assessment, the owner's pass); then the owner's declaration after daily use. UX improvement is a hill climb that is nearly endless (the owner, Decision Log 1), so the phase ends at a stated extent, read as how far it took the surfaces rather than as completeness, and carries what remains forward as tracker rows or jobs.

**What it must not be.** Not new breadth: sharing, simultaneous editing, a web-hosted MAWS and the teachers product are P5 (charter §3, the owner 2026-10-19; the P3 spec's §9 keeps them as P5's seed). Not a rewrite of P3's structure: the store, the editors, the protocol and the history stand (P3 §4.10, binding); P4 changes behaviour inside them. Not a regression on P3's §2: every item verified at wave S stays verified, and a P4 landing that trips a P3 e2e group fixes the regression, never the expectation, unless a dated line here says the behaviour changed. Not a write under the owner's `~/.claude` or `~/.claude.json` (D-1; the launch profile goes through the launch's own options). Not an effort decision or an inference optimisation (Decision Log 8: slowness from anything but MAWS's own tools is accepted; the parity bench of Decision Log 10 asks one bounded question, whether Desktop is equally slow on the same edits, and is not a close criterion by itself). Not a close on agents' opinions alone: an assessor's findings are evidence for the owner's verdict, never the verdict (Decision Log 1). Not an open-ended loop: a surface closes on the owner's pass, and the phase on the owner's declaration.

## 2. Acceptance

1. **A document's comments sit beside the passage.** With the window wide, every open thread of the shown tab is a card in a margin right of the page, its top at the passage's first line, cards stacked without overlap in document order; clicking a passage or a card activates that thread, which sits exactly at its anchor, shifted slightly left, its neighbours pushed; typing above a passage moves its card the same frame; scrolling runs no layout; an agent's edit, selection or reply never changes the active thread. Narrower, cards shrink, then become an icon rail at the anchors (icons that collide merge into a count), then today's drawer under a 720 px body. View › Comments offers Expand, Minimize and Hide, persisted; an All-comments panel filters Open, Resolved, All, This tab, All tabs, a search; opening it takes the cards out of the margin and closing it brings them back with the same active thread. The page is centred when no thread is open and moves left when the first appears (the owner, Decision Log 6). ⌘⌥M comments on a selection; in a card R replies, E resolves, J and K move, U or Escape returns to the text; Escape on a non-empty draft keeps the draft (Decision Log 5). Resolving removes the card and keeps the mark; a passage deleted shows its thread as "The commented passage was removed", and undo brings the card back. (P4-B1; the prior art's L1 to L7, P1 to P6, R1 to R6, N1 to N6, Z1 to Z6, C1 to C8, K1 to K4.)
2. **A slide's or a board's comment is a pin and a popover.** One pin per commented element at its top right, following the element's moves in the same frame; a click opens a popover placed by `placeCard`, one at a time; Escape, a click on the pin or on the empty stage closes it and selects nothing; a non-empty draft survives closing; resolving the last thread removes the pin, deleting the element keeps its threads as orphans; the filmstrip badges a slide with open threads; presenting shows no pins. The card inside the popover is the document's card (X36). (P4-B2; the prior art's V1 to V15.)
3. **Slides and design boards edit on the first gesture.** A first click selects a text element and the editor underneath is already focused with the caret hidden; a second click places the caret; a drag of 3 px moves; a double-click anywhere edits the deepest text under the pointer in one gesture, a grouped heading and a table cell included, with the word selected; a printable key on a selected text element replaces its text; Enter selects all; Escape steps out one level; Backspace on a selection deletes the element; a Korean composition started on a selected, not yet editing, element lands composed, the first syllable intact, on the real Mac through the 2-Set input method (the spike's proof before the plan). A double-click on an inactive design board activates it and edits in the same gesture. (P4-B2; the prior art's P1 to P12, K1 to K6, I1 to I5, L1 to L8; the owner's choice of Canva's select-first model, Decision Log 6.)
4. **A named Google Fonts face is fetched once into the deck.** Naming a Google Fonts family in `deck.json` fetches it once into the deck's assets if its licence is OFL, after which the editor, the presenter and every export render it offline; a non-OFL face is refused by name and renders through the fallback as today.
