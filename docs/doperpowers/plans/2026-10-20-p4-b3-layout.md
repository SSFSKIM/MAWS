## Decision Log

Residue decisions, grilled against the code at `65143ebe4`; each is the executor's to apply, and a milestone that finds one wrong records a dated line here rather than diverging.

1. **2026-10-20 — The state's home.** The ledger's `MainSplit` is the existing `TabSet` (`src/shared/ipc/schema/layout.ts`): `TabSetSchema` gains `floating: z.strictObject({ sessionTabId: z.string() }).optional()`, absent while docked (never `null`, never `undefined` written). `main.tabs` keeps version 2 and B3 takes no X15 slot: a new reader reads an older value unchanged; an older build reading a value written *while a split floats* rejects `main.tabs` (strict) and falls back to its default tabs, the same downgrade cost `regions` 4 accepted at P3-G; while docked the value is byte-identical to today's.
2. **2026-10-20 — Which pane floats.** A pane can float exactly when its active tab is a `session` tab (an `agent` tab has no composer and clamps). The squeezed pane floats; with two sessions, whichever is squeezed past the threshold floats; with no session, both sides clamp at their floor. "A" is the other pane, of any kind (the prior art's open decision "two sessions in the split", settled by keying on the squeezed pane's kind as §3.4 suggested).
3. **2026-10-20 — The pixel rule.** `splitDrag(width, x, canFloat: {left, right})` in `main/split.ts` is pure: `x` is the left pane's pointer-implied width in px from the region's left edge. Each side's floor is `min(320, floor(width / 2))` (a main region narrower than 640 px splits evenly rather than overlapping; `MAIN_MIN_WIDTH` is 480). Left: `canFloat.left && x < 160` → armed left; else `x < floor` → held at the floor. Right mirrored on `width − x`. Armed iff below the threshold at each move: the same limit both ways (no hysteresis), so 159 arms and 161 disarms with the pane held at 320. The divider's 1 px column is ignored in the arithmetic (the worked numbers: W = 1500, x = 400 gives 400). The model's `setSplitRatio` drops the 25-75 % clamp for a sanity clamp to [0.01, 0.99] (a non-finite ratio reads 0.5); the pixel bounds are the divider's and the render's.
4. **2026-10-20 — The drag is transient.** The divider keeps the live width and the armed side in the main area's own state (or a transient store field); nothing reaches `main.tabs` until release. Release, not armed → `setSplitRatio(left / width)`; release, armed → `floatSplit` with that pane's session tab, `splitRatio` untouched (so Dock returns `r0` with nothing to restore). `pointercancel` or a lost capture → nothing written (docked at `r0`). Keyboard on the divider keeps the Resizer's rules within the pixel bounds (arrows 8 px, Shift 32, Home/End to the floors, Enter and double-click to 0.5) and never floats. The generic `app/Resizer.tsx` stays as it is for the regions; the split divider owns its pointer handling (the Resizer clamps before `onChange`, which hides the raw value the arm needs).
5. **2026-10-20 — Rendering through the gesture.** While armed, S stays mounted at 0 px (its column and strip collapsed, the divider at the edge under pointer capture) so a disarm is lossless; on float S's `Pane` is not rendered in the main area and the card is S's only mount; A's `Pane` keeps its position among `MainArea`'s children through arm, disarm, float and dock, so A's editor never remounts. While floating there is no divider and no `DropEdge`. The rendered widths clamp the stored ratio to the floors at every width (the resize rule: a window resize only clamps, never floats or docks).
6. **2026-10-20 — Validity is normalised, not policed.** After every model transition (`onSet`) and at hydration (`repairMain`), `floating` is dropped when the set no longer has two panes, its tab is not the active tab of a pane, or that tab is not a session tab. This one rule gives the §3.3 rows: A's last tab closing → S shows full width; S's session tab closing → A alone (S's pane emptied) or the split docked at `r0` on S's next tab; any tab change in S docks; a tab set switch leaves the other set's float stored and hidden.
7. **2026-10-20 — Focus.** A float (release or Expand) marks S's session for focus on mount (`markFocusOnMount`), so the card's composer takes the keyboard, and sets `focusedPane` to S: the card stands for S (the panel shows S's session, ⌘W closes it), as P3-G's card stands for the shown session. A press or focus in A focuses A as today; a press or focus in the split card focuses S. While a split floats with its card closed to the pill, `focusedPane` moving to S (a sidebar click, attention navigation, ⌘⇧\) opens the card and focuses its composer. Dock marks S's composer for focus and keeps `focusedPane` on S.
8. **2026-10-20 — One card at a time.** `floatPanel` (the divider past the maximum, ⌥⌘B, the header's Float) docks a floating split first; `togglePanelFloat` with a split card up docks that split and does nothing else (⌥⌘B docks the split card first; a second press floats the panel). A split float asked while the panel stands in main (maximized or floating) docks the panel first (`revealMain`). `cardSession` answers the panel float's shown session when the panel floats, else the current set's floating session, else null; `cardHost` answers which region the card is in (`'panel' | 'split' | null`).
9. **2026-10-20 — Both cards at the bottom right (parent Decision Log 15.7; X30 amendment).** P3-G §D1.4's panel card sits at the panel body's bottom left (`left: CARD_MARGIN`); it moves to the bottom right. Both cards: 16 px from the right and bottom edges, resized by the top edge, the left edge and the top-left corner (the mirror of today's top, right and top-right), the size written on pointer-up; both pills at the bottom right. **The column over a Browser tab follows the card to the right** (the spacer after the page, the card's left edge its resize): the owner's binding P3 ruling is that the card reserves a live column instead of floating over a page; its side was the old corner's. This reading amends P3 §4.9's word "left" and goes back as `[parent-impact]` for the architect's ruling; the side is one constant, so keeping it left is a one-line change.
10. **2026-10-20 — The split card's layer.** Rendered inside `MainArea`'s grid on the content row, spanning every column (below the strips and the banner row, never over a tab strip), `data-floating-layer data-markup-occluder`, pointer-transparent except the card and the pill; the card is the shared `Card` with its Dock wired to `dockSplit`. Over a Browser **main** tab in A, the pane reserves the same right column (`reserveColumn`) as the panel's Browser pane does, since a page is a native view the card cannot float over. Its size key in `regions.floatingCard` is `main:<A's active tab id>` (the D1.5 rule: the geometry belongs to what the card sits over), pruned with the live main tabs as panel keys are with the live panel tabs.
11. **2026-10-20 — The X5 note.** The split's float changes nothing the panel host is handed: `maximized` stays the panel's own (`panelInMain`: maximized or floating), and the shown session follows the focused pane as always. Because a float puts `focusedPane` on S (7), a card up over either region hands the host the card's session while the card holds focus, exactly as the panel's float does: the host sees the split's float as the panel's. `panelInMain` is never true for a split float; its readers (visibility, cover, `ArtifactTab`'s `visible`, navigation) learn the split card through `cardSession` / `openCardSession`, which now include it.
12. **2026-10-20 — Expand (X32 amendment; keymap).** `main.expandArtifactPane`, title "Expand pane", no chord, `sink: 'menu'`, `menu: 'view'`, `scope: 'app'`: on the focused pane, floats the other pane when its active tab is a session; on a floating split's A, docks (a toggle); otherwise refused with a toast naming why (as wave S F6's float refusal does). The header control is drawn by the main surface in `EditorHeader`'s `controls` slot of a main tab whose body registers (so every artifact editor gets it, no editor registers it): shown while this pane's other pane shows a session ("Expand pane") or while this pane is a floating split's A (pressed, "Restore split"); absent otherwise.
13. **2026-10-20 — Raise on a request (X30 amendment, both cards).** When the card's session gains a new open permission request or question (an item id `selectPendingOf` did not answer before) while the card is closed to its pill, the card opens (Codex forces its chat open on pending input). A pill the owner closes while that request is still pending stays closed for it; the next new request raises it again (Codex keys its exception by the pending input's id, LC-3 section). It never takes the keyboard by itself; the docked request's own focus rule applies once the card is open.
14. **2026-10-20 — The transcript's scroll across the round trip.** Today a transcript always mounts at its end. A one-shot carry per session, in `src/renderer/src/transcript/view/`: every mounted transcript records `{following, offset, signature}` (the row count and the last row's key) as it unmounts; a float or dock transition (either card) marks the session's next mount to restore it: following → the end; else the same offset when the signature is unchanged; else the end (Claude's rule, §4.5). An ordinary tab switch keeps today's behaviour (the mark is set only by the card transitions).
15. **2026-10-20 — The rect API (X37).** `useFloatingCard` gains `rect: {left, top, width, height} | null` (client px of the open card, null for a pill or no card) and `host: 'panel' | 'split' | null`, published by the card from a layout effect and its resize observer. B3 changes no editor's layout; each surface's response is a residue row (the deck's inspector under a bottom-right card first, obs 4).
16. **2026-10-20 — Drag state (X38).** `dragging: {source: 'main'; tabId; fromPane} | {source: 'panel'; tabId; toolId; artifactId} | null` replaces today's `{tabId, fromPane}`. Targets decide acceptance from `dragging` (a `dragover` cannot read the payload) and act on the payload at `drop`. `application/x-maws-tab` keeps its payload (the main tab's id); `application/x-maws-panel-tab` carries `JSON.stringify({toolId, tabId})`. `effectAllowed = 'move'` everywhere; an accepting target sets `dropEffect = 'move'` and calls `preventDefault`; a refusing target never does (the no-drop cursor). `dragend` and `drop` are also heard on `window`, and an unmounting source clears `dragging` (INV-5).
17. **2026-10-20 — What drags from the panel.** The panel's Artifacts tab, and a grip in its record bar ("Drag to move to a tab"), are `draggable` only while the instance shows a record; no other panel tab is draggable (the panel strip has no reorder today, so T12 and T13 read as "nothing accepted in main").
18. **2026-10-20 — Targets, highlights, labels.** The panel is one target: the region's content (header and body) with one whole-panel highlight. A main strip keeps the midpoint insertion line and wins over the body under it. A pane's body is a target for a panel record (append; a whole-pane highlight). `DropEdge` keeps its right-15 % hit zone and draws its highlight over the right half (the pane that will open). An HTML5 drag image is fixed at `dragstart`, so the outcome label (Z7) is drawn in the target's highlight: "Move to panel", "Move to tab", "Open in split" (Codex's words where it has them).
19. **2026-10-20 — The hand-off of the editor's state (the carry's core).** The per-scope hold (`artifacts/registry.ts`) is what an editor keeps per tab: the replica with every edit typed (acknowledged or not), the deck's current slide, the design's viewport and selection, history and rail state. A move hands the hold over instead of disposing it: `stageArtifactEditorState(fromScope)` detaches the hold (no dispose) into a staging slot keyed by artifact id, synchronously before the source lets go; `adoptStagedArtifactEditorState(artifactId, toScope)` binds it to the target scope before React renders it; `holdArtifactEditorState` adopts a staged hold for the same artifact, kind and editor as a fallback; anything staged and not adopted by the end of the move's task is disposed. Because the replica travels, a move needs neither side's close guard for the moved artifact (nothing can be lost): the source closes without its guard; only a *different* record the target must let go of (T3, T4) asks its guard, and an activate-existing drop closes the source through its guard (C4: a cancelled dialog is a refusal). Every await (a guard) happens before the hand-off; from the hand-off to the target's adoption the steps are synchronous, so INV-2 and INV-4 hold at every await.
20. **2026-10-20 — The move operations** live in `src/renderer/src/shell/transfer.ts`: `moveArtifactToPanel(mainTabId)` and `moveArtifactToTab(panelTabId, {pane?, index?})`, and `transferRefusal(request): string | null`, the one reason read by the drop targets, the menus' disabled state and title, and the keymap actions' toast. The target panel of a main→panel move is the one shown once the tab is gone (`closeTab`'s rule; T18), else the pinned Artifacts tab with no session. The existing "Show in panel" (`main/Tab.tsx`) and the record bar's button route through these. Reasons: no panel can take it (`SHOW_IN_PANEL_COPY.noPanel`, kept); the panel shows no artifact (for "Move to main area" and a drag from a list); the panel tab is closing; a kind M5 refuses (if any).

## Surprises & Discoveries

- 2026-10-20: the transcript always mounts at its end (`transcript/view/Transcript.tsx`), so §4.5's scroll rule ("the same `scrollTop` when the content is unchanged") is not free across the pane-to-card remount; Decision Log 14 adds the one-shot carry.
- 2026-10-20: the record bar's "Open in tab" (`tools/artifacts/RecordView.tsx`) opens the main tab before the panel's Back asks its guard, so a refused guard leaves two views (a copy), against INV-4; M4's hand-off replaces it.
- 2026-10-20: `requestOnScreen` (`attention/navigation.ts`) and `visibleSessions` (`session/visibility.ts`) count a session "in front" whenever its tab is a pane's active tab and the panel is not in main; a split float hides S while its tab stays active, so both must read the split card instead (M2).
- 2026-10-20: the deck's and the design's view (current slide; viewport and selected board) already live in their tab states, so the hold hand-off carries them; the document's scroll and selection live only in the mounted editor (M5).
