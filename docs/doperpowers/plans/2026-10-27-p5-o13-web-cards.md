## Purpose

Desk Web is the owner's phone: when a session on the mini asks a question or wants a permission while the owner is away from a Mac, the phone buzzes, the card opens, and the answer moves the session on. P5 cuts each machine over to the endpoint module, which refuses every DX6 request card and answer from the day it enrolls (RL §9). Without this child, the first cutover of a machine the owner answers from the phone, the mini's, would end the phone loop.

After this child:

- the phone is a device of the org in its own right, class `web-cards`: a channel key the browser holds and cannot export, and a passkey, both listed in the signed org state after one approval from the owner's Mac;
- a question or a permission prompt on a cut-over machine reaches the phone as a push, opens as a card drawn from the machine's own sealed channel (nothing the hub can alter), and is answered with a Face ID gesture whose assertion the machine checks against the exact request still pending;
- the phone can do nothing else on a cut-over machine: send, steer, spawn and every other action are refused at the endpoint, which is the owner's protection against a hostile deploy of the hub-served code (RL §5.10);
- machines not yet moved keep their DX6 cards on the same phone, side by side, through every deploy;
- desk-web's other views freeze: they stay as they are, unmaintained, and stop pretending to control a machine that has moved;
- hub v2's first production deploy, and the org's root and the phone's approval, run from steps committed beside O11's staging runners, never from a wrapper under `/tmp` (P5 Decision Log 40).

What someone can do afterwards, on staging first and on production once O14 cuts the mini over: with the Mac closed, a `claude` in a terminal on the mini asks a question; the phone shows "A session is waiting for your answer"; the card opens with the question's choices; a tap and Face ID answer it; the terminal shows the answer within a second or two of the gesture. The same for a permission prompt, Allow or Deny. A push for a session the owner doesn't own never arrives.

## Acceptance

P5's O13 items 1–5 are this child's acceptance, with the clauses Decision Log 39 moved here, checked as follows (commands in Concrete Steps). Where an item needs another child's code, the item says so; until then it is recorded as met for this child's part only, with the joint clause listed as outstanding, as O10 does.

1. **Registration.** On the phone, desk-web's Device panel shows a pairing field; the owner's Mac runs `deployment admin pair --class web-cards`, which prints a pairing code; pasted into the phone, the panel reads *waiting for approval* within 2 s, the Mac command shows the device's id and its passkey's relying party and asks `approve? [y/N]`, and after `y` the phone's panel reads *listed, version <n>* within 10 s. `GET /api/org/state` on the hub shows the member's new device entry `{class: 'web-cards', keyStorage: 'webcrypto', channelPub, passkey}` with no `stepUpPub`. The channel key is a non-extractable `CryptoKey` in the phone's IndexedDB: `crypto.subtle.exportKey` on it throws, and no fetch, log line or storage record ever carries its bytes (M3's suite reads the store and checks `extractable === false`). The passkey is a credential of its own, not the org key's PRF passkey.
2. **A full round trip.** On a cut-over machine, with the phone's app closed, a permission prompt and a question each: push to the phone (a notification reading *A session is waiting for your answer*); on a tap, show their card contents over the phone's channel (the question's text and choices; the tool, its verb and its input); are answered with a passkey gesture, after which the card settles and the machine's session shows the answer. The machine checks that the assertion's challenge is the action's digest and that the member owns the session: a replayed assertion, one over another action's digest, and an answer on a session the member doesn't own are each refused, the answer never reaching the session. This holds for one lane A and one lane B session. The push reaches the owner's phone and no other member's device: with a third session on the same machine owned by a second member, the owner's devices (the phone, and a second `web-cards` device of the owner's subscribed at the run's push sink) each receive one notice per owner's session and none for the second member's, as O11 M8 framed the clause for its sink. The live run is M7's, on staging against the real endpoint module enrolled there and the owner's real phone; lane B's engine is M7's harness until `mawsd` exists (O2, O10 M10), and the run on production is O14's mini brief, with this document's rollout steps.
3. **The class's bound.** From the phone's identity, an `act` of `session.send`, `session.steer`, `sminos.steer`, `sminos.spawn`, `sminos.command`, `endpoint.grant`, a `sub` of view `full`, a `call` of `session.events`, a lane B permission answer carrying `updatedInput` or a scope other than `once`, and a lane A `decide` with a `message` are each refused `class` at the endpoint, and a `query` of any operation on a session is refused `grade`. The phone's client offers none of them: its code builds only `sub cards`, `session.answer`, `sminos.answer` and `sminos.decide`, and a suite pins that nothing else leaves it.
4. **Side by side.** With one machine cut over and one not, both in one project: the moved machine's sessions answer over the channel, the other's over DX6, in the same project view and from the same push subscription, and every existing `apps/desk-web/e2e` suite (`phone.spec.ts` included) is green unchanged.
5. **The rest freezes.** desk-web's grid, project, session, org, key, machines and notifications views draw as at master `cb5f388dc` but for one seam: a session whose machine the org state lists (cut over) shows its composer and controls disabled with the reason *moved to MAWS: its cards are on this phone's channel*, and no DX6 request is sent for it. A cut-over machine drops out of the machines dialog's list (P5 Decision Log 37, accepted). `apps/desk-web/CLAUDE.md` says which views are frozen and that the cards, the device and push are the maintained ones.

### Constraints (bind every milestone)

- **The trust model is RL's; the phone proves presence, not intent.** Every answer from the phone carries a WebAuthn assertion over the action's digest (RL §5.10), checked by the endpoint with `verifyWebAuthn` (O10 §C) against the device's listed passkey, the hub's origin and the stored counter. The phone's code is served by the hub, so nothing in this child relies on that code for anything but drawing and asking: the machine decides (O10 M5), and the accepted residual is RL §5.10's.
- **One protocol package.** RL2's channel, RL4's action and its WebAuthn assertion, RL5's state and its verification run in the browser from `apps/org-protocol` as the endpoint runs them: desk-web imports the package directly (`org-protocol: workspace:*`, its `.ts` imports admitted by desk-web's `allowImportingTsExtensions`), never through a copy. The reference client (`apps/org-protocol/test-support/machine-socket.ts`, `RelayApp`) and the endpoint suites' device carrier (`test/endpoint/carriers.ts`) are working app-side implementations to copy. ### M2 — The pairing code, its MAC, and the owner's admin command

**At its end.** A new device can be paired the way RL §5.3 describes: the approving side shows a one-time code carrying the org's root pins and a pairing secret; the device posts its keys with a MAC under that secret; the approving side checks the MAC and signs the device into a successor. The approving side is a command on the owner's Mac, `deployment admin`, which can also root an org, list devices and remove one, pending O8's Org page. It is proved against the suites' local hub and on staging with the staging admin device.

**Touches.** `apps/org-protocol/src/pairing.ts` (new), `src/index.ts`, `vectors/` (a pairing vector), `test/`; `apps/desk-hub/deploy/admin.ts` (new), `bin.ts` (the command), `org-admin.ts` (reused: `successor`, `deviceEntry`, the state's signing), `staging-run.sh` (an `admin <args…>` step: the staging admin keys and the owner's staging token read inside, a pairing code to the terminal alone, never the log), `README.md`; `apps/desk-hub/test/deploy/admin.test.ts` (new).

**Decisions.**
- **The code and the MAC are the package's** (§A), not desk-web's or the hub's: both ends must agree byte for byte and O8's app will be the third consumer. The MAC input is length-prefixed under its own domain, as the enrollment MAC is, over the org, the member and the device entry's fields, so a key, class, storage kind or passkey the hub substituted fails on the Mac. Rejected: reusing `enrollmentMacInput` with a device's fields in a machine's slots, which would let a machine's MAC and a device's collide.
- **The admin command is the owner's hand before O8** (P5 §8 names the Org page as the admin's app, and it is unbuilt). It lives beside `org-admin.ts` because the signing it needs is already there, and it is reached through `deployment` so the README stays the one place. It differs from the test commands in its scope: it acts on the deployment `--config` names, production by default, and refuses a throwaway org; `checkTestHub` does not apply to it, and so the Constraints make it the owner's alone against production. Its device is its own (`init`: a channel key, a step-up key and the org's recovery key, generated once into `<keys>` 0600 and `<keys>.recovery` 0600, never overwritten; the recovery key the owner moves offline **[owner, pending: where the recovery key lives; written as: a file the command tells the owner to move to offline storage]**), class `native-mac`, `keyStorage: 'software'`. Rejected: reading MAWS main's device keys (`<profile>/device/keys.json`, O10 M9), which couples a script to the app's private files as O6 refused for sminos, and would make the app's Touch ID-gated step-up key sign root states from a terminal; and rooting the org from desk-web's browser, which puts the root keys in a browser's IndexedDB with no backup. When O8 lands, the app's device is added as the owner's second device, endorsed by this one, and this device retires honestly.
- **`genesis`** builds version 1 (`prev` null, `issued` now, `root.keys` the admin device's step-up key, `recoveryPub`, one member: the signed-in person as `/api/me` names them with `role: 'admin'` and the admin device, no machines, delegations or projects, `machineNames`, `retired` and `revoked` empty), signs it with the step-up key (`signOrgState`) and posts it; it prints the genesis hash (`stateHash`) for `orgGenesis` in the deployment's config, which the owner commits. It refuses when the hub already holds a state.
- **`pair --class web-cards|native-mac [--ttl <s>]`** makes `k` (16 bytes), prints the code once and copies it to the Mac's clipboard (`pbcopy`, so Universal Clipboard pastes it on the phone **[owner, pending: the code's transport; written as: the clipboard, no QR]**), then polls `GET /api/devices/pending` every 2 s for up to `PENDING_DEVICE_TTL_S` for an entry of the requested class whose MAC verifies under `k` (others are ignored and named in the log as not this pairing's); shows the entry (id, class, storage, the passkey's `rpId`); asks `approve? [y/N]`; signs a successor adding the device to the member's entry with an `endorsement` by the admin device (the member's earlier device) and the root signature, posts it, and prints the version. A `not-head` refusal re-reads the head and signs again, once.
- **`devices`** lists the member's devices from the head; **`remove-device <id>`** signs a successor dropping the device, its key into `revoked` (RL5), and prints the version; the direct RL4 revocation to each machine is O8's app's, and until then machines learn it at the hub's `state` notice (O10 M2's sync), within its 10-minute poll at worst.
- **Where it may write.** The deployment's own org, only when the hub serves a chain whose genesis is the command's anchor, or no state yet for `genesis`; a throwaway org is refused (`--test-org` is not an option). The anchor is `<keys>.org.json` `{hub, org, genesis, version}`, written by `genesis` and, where the config pins `orgGenesis`, checked against it; every other subcommand reads the chain from its genesis, verifies it with the package (`verifyPinnedChain` over the anchor's roots), refuses a chain whose genesis differs, and moves `version` forward as `access-sync` moves its checkpoint, so an older head served as the head is refused. Against the suites' local hub the anchor is the one `genesis` just wrote; against staging it is staging's pinned genesis.

On staging, by the executor with the staging admin keys: a `web-cards` test device (the package's `TestDevice` with a `SoftwareAuthenticator`) paired and removed under `staging-ssfskim`, the versions printed.

### M3 — The phone registers as a `web-cards` device

**At its end.** desk-web on a phone can become a `web-cards` device of the org: a Device panel takes a pairing code, makes a non-extractable WebCrypto channel key and a passkey, posts the pairing entry with its MAC, waits to be listed, and from then holds the signed org state verified from the code's pins; its push subscription is bound to the device. The panel says what state the device is in and lets the owner forget it.

**Touches.** `apps/desk-web/src/cards/device-store.ts`, `register.ts`, `org-store.ts`, `cards-passkey.ts` (new); `src/store/idb.ts` (`DESK_DB` version 2, stores `device` and `org`); `src/push/device.ts` (`device` on the subscription); `src/views/cards/DevicePanel.tsx` (new), `src/App.tsx` (the panel reached from the phone menu and the top bar); `package.json` (`org-protocol`); tests, `e2e-hub/cards-device.spec.ts` (new); `e2e/fixtures.ts` (`seedKey`'s upgrade handler creates the two new stores too).

**Decisions.**
- **The channel key** is `crypto.subtle.generateKey(P-256, extractable: false, ['sign'])`, kept as a `CryptoKey` in IndexedDB (§B) beside the org key, which already lives that way. Its public point is exported raw (the package's `B64` of 65 bytes). The device id is 16 random bytes base64url, chosen by the phone.
- **The passkey** (`cards-passkey.ts`: `createCardsPasskey()` at registration, and `assertWith(record)`, the `Assert` M4's answers take, which runs `navigator.credentials.get` with the challenge it is given) is a credential of its own, made at registration with `navigator.credentials.create` (`rp.id` the hub's hostname, `alg -7` only, `userVerification: 'required'`, `residentKey: 'discouraged'` since every assertion names the credential, no PRF), and its public point taken from `response.getPublicKey()`'s SubjectPublicKeyInfo (the last 65 bytes). The org key's PRF passkey (`src/key/passkey.ts`) is not reused: it retires with the org key at O14's final brief, and its assertions are never sent anywhere. Pitfall seen in the code: `getPublicKey()` is a WebAuthn level 2 method; the owner's phone is iOS Safari, where it is available from Safari 16.4, and the registration refuses with a plain reason on a browser without it rather than parsing the attestation object.
- **The pins** come from the code (§A: `roots`, `recoveryPub`), never from the hub. The org store (`org-store.ts`) holds the head, the chain window and the transitions it has adopted in IndexedDB, syncs from `GET /api/org/state?since=<held>` on each app start and on a channel's `state` message, and verifies the first sync with `verifyPinnedChain` and every later one with `advanceHead`, as the endpoint does (O10 M2). It implements the package's `OrgView` for the channel (M4). A head that stops listing the device, or a member removal, moves the device to `removed`, which the panel says; the keys stay until the owner forgets them.
- **Registration's states** (§B): `none` → `pending` (posted, `expires`) → `listed {version}` | `expired` | `removed`. The phone polls the head every 5 s while `pending`, for the entry's TTL; the panel says *waiting for approval on your Mac*, then *listed, version n*. A pairing code for another org or member than `/api/me`'s is refused before anything is generated.
- **Push binds to the device**: once listed, `PUT /api/push/subscriptions` carries `device`, and the phone's `resyncPush` at start hands it over again with it, so revoking the device ends its pushes at the hub (P5 Decision Log 41). A phone not yet registered keeps today's unbound subscription.
- **The Device panel** is new UI (the maintained part), reached from the phone menu and the top bar's dot menu: the state, the pairing field, the passkey's relying party, Forget (deletes the record and the key; the org-state entry stays until `remove-device`), and the push state as the Notifications dialog shows it. It draws nothing of the org state but the version.

**Does not touch.** The channel (M4); the cards (M5); the service worker (M6); any frozen view.

**Proves.** Acceptance 1's phone half: unit suites for the state machine and the store (`// @vitest-environment happy-dom` where a DOM is needed) with a fake hub; `e2e-hub/cards-device.spec.ts` in Chromium with the virtual authenticator against the local hub rooted by `deployment admin genesis`: the code pasted, `pair` run by the test as a child process approving the entry, the panel reaching *listed*, the head's entry matching the phone's keys, `extractable === false` on the stored key, a second registration refused while one is listed, Forget emptying the store; a code for another member refused; the pinned sync refusing a chain whose genesis differs (a hub serving a throwaway org); the subscription record at the hub carrying `device`.

### M4 — The channel from the phone, and the cards client **[needs O10 M7/M8]**

**At its end.** From the browser, desk-web opens an RL2 channel to a machine through the hub's relay with the package's `connectChannel`, subscribes to the `cards` view of a session on either lane, draws the pending set into cards, and answers a card with an action carrying a WebAuthn assertion, seeing `ack`, `done` and the card's settlement. Nothing else crosses the channel. The client is a library with no view, proved under vitest against the real endpoint on the local hub.

**Touches.** `apps/desk-web/src/cards/carrier.ts`, `client.ts`, `cards.ts`, `answers.ts` (new); tests, `apps/desk-web/test/local/endpoint.ts` (new: the in-process endpoint on the local hub) and `src/cards/*.local.test.ts` (the suites that start the local hub, a wrangler dev child, named `.local.test.ts` and run in a project of their own as the root `vitest.config.ts` isolates `test/endpoint/*.local.test.ts`); `apps/desk-hub/deploy/staging-deploy.sh` (exports `VITE_DESK_WEB_BUILD=<label>` before the web build).

**Decisions.**
- **The carrier** (§C) is one WebSocket per machine to `/api/machines/<ref>/socket` (the ref from the notice or the org state; the hub takes a ref or a name), `binaryType: 'arraybuffer'`, speaking the multiplex with the package's `encodeChannelFrame`/`decodeChannelFrame`: `open` with a tag, then one `ByteDuplex` per channel. `connectChannel` dials once per catch-up page; a dial reuses the machine's socket while it is open and reconnects it with `src/hub/socket.ts`'s backoff otherwise. `presence` frames are kept per machine for the cards list (M5). The phone stays on the relay (P5 Decision Log 39): no direct dial, no endpoint record read.
- **The identity** is `DeviceIdentity {org, member, device, clientClass: 'web-cards', channelKey, build: 'desk-web/<label>', capabilities: []}`, `label` the web build's (`import.meta.env.VITE_DESK_WEB_BUILD`, exported by the deploy scripts before the build as the `--web` label; `dev` otherwise). The `OrgView` is M3's store. `caps` is read for `stateVersion` only.
- **A subscription** is `sub {sub, session: {lane, id}, view: 'cards', cursor: null, credit: 1 MiB}`; `snap` replaces the card set whole (both lanes are snapshots, M8); lane A's `ev` of `raised` adds and `settled` removes; `ended {reason}` ends it with the reason shown (`removed`: the session is no longer the member's; `unavailable`: the session or its bridge is gone; `restart`: `mawsd` is restarting, resubscribe after backoff). Credit is topped up at half. A channel's close resubscribes through a new channel after backoff, every resubscription a snapshot.
- **A card** (§D) is drawn from what the snapshot carries and nothing else: lane A from `raised` (the tool, its input, the session), lane B from `request` (the `permission.requested` or `question.requested` event) and `item`. Its digest is computed by the phone from what it draws: lane A `desk-hub/protocol`'s `inputSha256` over `raised.input` as the card does today; lane B `requestDigestOf(appSessionId, request)` from `src/shared/host/request-digest.ts` (desk-web reaches `src/shared` as `@shared` already), the same function the endpoint and the engine host apply. The endpoint's `requestDigest` beside a lane B card is compared with the phone's and a mismatch makes the card not answerable with the reason *the request changed*, since an answer sealed to either would be refused `digest-mismatch`.
- **An answer** builds the action with the package's shape (§D): lane A `sminos.answer {session, answers, digest}` or `sminos.decide {session, decision: 'allow' | 'deny', digest}` with `context {lane: 'A', session, launch}` and `session` the engine session id; lane B `session.answer {appSessionId, requestId, answer}` with `context {lane: 'B', launchId, engineSessionId}` and `session` the `appSessionId`, the `answer` a `RequestAnswer` that carries `scope: 'once'` and no `updatedInput`, `message` or `deny_and_stop`. A question's answer is MAWS's `Question` stepper's answers as today's card hands them to DX6 (`RequestCard.tsx`); a lane B question's answer shape is `RequestAnswer`'s question form. Then `navigator.credentials.get` with `challenge` = `actionDigest(action)`, `rpId` the hub's hostname, `allowCredentials` the device's one credential, `userVerification: 'required'`; the assertion's `credentialId`, `authenticatorData`, `clientDataJSON` and `signature` go into `action.webauthn` base64url; `act` is sent. The outcome is `ack` then `done` within 60 s; `refused {reason}` is shown by reason (`class`, `grade`, `stale`, `context`, `digest-mismatch`, `step-up` for a bad assertion, `read-only`, `unavailable`).
- **The fresh lane B session** (O13 carry): a card whose `context.engineSessionId` is null is drawn but not answerable, with the reason *this session hasn't been prompted locally yet*; the next snapshot after its first turn makes it answerable.
- **A lost acknowledgement** (O13 carry; P5 Decision Log 64): the phone never sends `query`, which its class is refused. A card whose `act` got no `ack` within 10 s, or no `done` within 60 s, is `uncertain` with Retry, as today's card is: Retry reseals under the **same `opId`** with a fresh nonce, `created` and assertion (a new gesture: the challenge covers them), and the ledger answers the stored result if the first copy ran; a snapshot showing the card gone settles it as `closed`. Only a card that is still pending after a refused reseal is answered anew under a new `opId`. Rejected: adding `name` to sminos's RL10 binding so a `web-cards` query could be answered (an O12 change), which the reseal makes unnecessary.
- **The lane A context's `launch`** comes from the lane A `cards` snapshot (§D, O13's ask of O10 M8: the body gains `launch`); until it is there, a lane A answer from the phone cannot carry the mandatory context and this milestone's lane A proof is outstanding, recorded as such.
- **What the client never does** is pinned by a suite that drives every exported function with a recording carrier and asserts the set of message kinds and action names that crossed (Constraints, "Nothing but cards").

**Does not touch.** Views; the service worker; the DX6 path (`src/hub/project-client.ts`), which keeps serving machines not yet moved.

**Proves.** Acceptance 2's channel half and acceptance 3, under vitest (`// @vitest-environment node`: Node's WebSocket and WebCrypto) against `test/local/endpoint.ts`: the real endpoint (`createEndpoint` over `test/endpoint/fake-services.ts`, the fake engine host with M7's `opCommands` and a fake sminos on RL10, assembled as `test/endpoint/sub-harness.ts` does) enrolled on the local hub through `OrgAdmin`, a `web-cards` device registered by M3's code path (the store seeded in Node) and a `SoftwareAuthenticator` standing in for the browser's passkey behind the same `answers.ts` interface. Cases: a channel opens through the relay and `caps` names the class; a cold `cards` subscription shows a question and a permission already pending on a lane A session and on a lane B one; a question answered and a permission denied, each `done` and the fake engine host's or sminos's record showing the answer once, the assertion verified at the endpoint; an assertion replayed, one over another action's digest, and an answer on a session the member doesn't own each refused and nothing run; a changed input refused `digest-mismatch`; a null `engineSessionId` card not answerable, answerable after the status moves; an `act` whose `ack` is withheld by a tamper relay resealed under the same `opId` and answered from the ledger with one effect; every item of acceptance 3's list refused as named, and the never-sends suite; a channel dropped mid-subscription resubscribed as a snapshot with no card lost; a device removed by a successor ended `removed` and its next handshake refused.

## Surprises & Discoveries

- Observation: Production holds no signed org state, and nothing in P5 roots it before O8's Org page; the hub accepts a genesis only from `ORG_ADMIN_EMAIL`'s Access identity, and `deploy/org-admin.ts` refuses production by name as a test device should.   Evidence: `apps/desk-hub/deploy/config.json` has no `orgGenesis`; `org-chain.ts`'s genesis rule (O11 M3); `org-admin.ts`'s header comment.
- Observation: O11 M3's pairing exchange stores the entry and leaves the MAC to the approving device, and no function in the package or the hub defines a device's MAC input; `enrollmentMacInput` is a machine's.
  Evidence: `apps/desk-hub/src/objects/org-chain.ts` `keepPending` ("the MAC is the approving device's to check"); `apps/org-protocol/src/enrollment.ts`.
- Observation: A lane A action's `context` must carry the bridge's `launch` (O10 M7 compares it for equality), and the lane A `cards` snapshot carries only `{raised}`, so a `web-cards` device holding nothing but the cards view cannot build a lane A answer. O10's own suites take the launch from the fake sminos's `member` frame, which the phone never sees.
  Evidence: `src/endpoint/plane-a.ts` `#cardSnap` on `origin/p5-o10-m8`; `test/endpoint/actions.test.ts` lines 464–506.
- Observation: The hub's v2 push payload (`OwnerNotice`) carries no `v`, alias, title or count, so today's service worker draws it as the plain *Something needs you* prompt landing on the grid.
  Evidence: `apps/desk-hub/src/objects/registry.ts` `notify`; `apps/desk-web/src/sw/notice.ts` `parseNotice`.
- Observation: The phone needs no name lookup to reach a machine: the Machine socket takes the ref the notice carries (`/api/machines/<ref or name>/socket`), and lane B's listing and notify already use the `appSessionId` the `sub` takes.
  Evidence: `apps/org-protocol/test-support/machine-socket.ts`; `src/endpoint/rl10/members.ts` `listingId`.
- Observation: Hub v2 needs no Durable Object migration for its first production deploy: `wrangler.jsonc` keeps `v1`'s three classes, and the Registry's, chain's and relay's new tables are created by each object's `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS`.
  Evidence: `apps/desk-hub/wrangler.jsonc` line 32; 23 such statements across `registry.ts`, `org-chain.ts`, `org-records.ts`, `relay.ts`.
- Observation: No view that survives the freeze renders transcript rows, so the engine's `answeredWithoutRequest: true` rows (doperpowers PR #411) need nothing here.
  Evidence: M6's record; the cards draw `raised` and `request` events only.
