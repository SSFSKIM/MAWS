## Purpose

**Higher purpose.** The charter's: a general, state-of-the-art workstation for supervising AI coding agents on the Claude Code engine, for its owner and a few colleagues, with supervision of many sessions and depth in one weighing equally (charter §1). The owner's work is not on one machine. It lives in projects whose sessions run on a MacBook, a Mac mini and Linux hosts, and the desk (desk §Purpose) was built to reach them from a browser. Two products grew toward the same job from two ends: MAWS with the depth, the desk with the reach. This phase makes them one.

**What it is for.** One MAWS app, signed in as one person of an org, shows every machine that person may see, the org's projects, and the sessions in each, and drives each session at MAWS's own depth on whatever machine it runs. A session opened from MAWS has the full SDK surface anywhere (lane B). A session started in a terminal is controlled through the bridge mod, with the gaps shown honestly (lane A), on its own machine as much as on another. People cooperate mainly through their own agents, which message each other with `sminos say` as today, each session labelled with who owns it (owner, 2026-10-26).

### The scene at its best

The owner opens MAWS on the MacBook. The sidebar lists projects, not folders: `MAWS` holds `maws-fe @macbook`, `maws-45 @mini` and `ci-opt @dc-1`, each row with its machine. `maws-45` was opened from MAWS, so it runs under the mini's machine daemon. Its transcript streams as if it were local. Its permission card shows the exact command. The image dropped on the composer reaches the model on the mini. The diff, the file tab and the terminal in the panel are the mini's worktree. `maws-fe` was started in a terminal on the MacBook. It is no longer read-only: send, steer, interrupt, answer and the model menu work through the bridge, and what the mod can't reach is greyed with its reason.

"+ Session" asks for a machine as well as a project. The lid closes. `maws-45` keeps working on the mini with every MAWS tool, and its next question buzzes the phone. The answer from the phone moves it on. When the MacBook wakes, every stream resumes from its cursor within a second. Opening MAWS on the mini itself shows the same sessions in the same state. Later a colleague joins the org. They see the projects' listings and their own sessions. Their agent asks `maws-fe` for a column name with `sminos say`, and the message arrives marked as coming from their agent, owned by them.

### What it must not be

- **Slower or weaker locally.** MAWS with no sign-in works as today, and a local session's events reach the screen within OX7's budget of today's path. The hub is never on the local path.
- **A thin remote viewer.** A session on a machine with no app keeps its MAWS tools, derivations and approvals. The machine is the authority (RL §1).
- **A second store of transcripts.** They stay on the machine that ran them (charter D-12; desk §What it must not be).
- **One key for everyone, or a hub that can act.** Every action and event names its writer, and the machine checks the writer (RL §5).
- **A patched engine** (charter D-1).
- **A web edition first.** The web port waits until this matures (owner, 2026-10-26). Client classes keep its place (RL §5.10).

## Parent-level acceptance

P5 closes when all of the following hold on the owner's MacBook, the mini, one devcloud Linux VM and the phone, checked by hand or by the named gate and recorded with evidence in Outcomes.

1. **Local parity.** Signed out, MAWS on the MacBook does everything it did at master c3507722d, with every e2e group green on the daemon shape, and the local-latency bench (OX7) within budget. Every project, session, artifact, library item, draft and layout from before the upgrade is present after it, with no re-index.
2. **Sessions outlive the app with their services.** With the app quit, a lane B session's `maws` tool calls (an artifact write, a dataset refresh) complete, the index and attention keep deriving, and reopening shows no visible gap: the journal re-serves what the ring dropped.
3. **One sidebar across machines.** Signed in, the sidebar groups sessions from the three machines by org project, each row with its machine. States match `sminos list --org` within 2 s. Sessions in no project sit under their machine.
4. **Lane B at full depth anywhere.** From the MacBook, a lane B session on the mini and one on the Linux VM each: stream with token deltas, send, steer, interrupt, approve and deny, answer a question, take an image, show the diff, open and edit a file, run a terminal in the worktree, and open, edit and screenshot an artifact. On the bench, remote delta-to-paint p50 is at most the local p50 plus one round trip plus 10 ms, a command's round trip is at most two round trips plus 20 ms, open to first row is ≤ 1.5 s, and resume is ≤ engine start plus one round trip, on each transport the app uses (§5).
5. **Spawn anywhere.** "+ Session" on the mini or the VM starts a lane B session that is in the sidebar within 5 s of the engine's first event.
6. **Lane A in MAWS.** A terminal session on the MacBook and one on the mini can be sent to, steered, interrupted, answered and approved from MAWS, and their model and effort set. Every control the bridge can't reach is disabled with its reason. A terminal session without the bridge stays read-only, as today.
7. **The laptop asleep.** With the MacBook asleep for an hour, sessions on the mini and the VM keep working, their approvals wait, and a question and a permission prompt each reach the phone and are answered there. Within 1 s of the network being reachable after wake, every subscription resumes from its cursor, with no loss and no duplicate (RL3).
8. **Two clients.** MAWS on the mini and on the MacBook attach to one session at once. Both stream it, and an answer from one settles the card in the other.
9. **The machine decides.** The trust cases of RL's acceptance hold: hub-placed actions and events are refused, an altered request card is refused by digest, and a removed device is refused once the new org state arrives.
10. **Agent tools on a machine with no app.** On the Linux VM with no app attached, `artifact_screenshot` and `artifact_export` work through the machine's render service, and browser control is offered or refused by capability, never failed mid-call.
11. **Owners are visible and carried.** Every session row shows its owners. A `sminos say` across sessions carries the sender session's owners, signed by its machine (RL9), and the receiver draws it as a peer message from that agent and owner.
12. **The people model is in.** A second member, added by an org-state edit on the staging org, sees listings per each session's visibility and their own sessions in full; with sessions at the default `listed`, `converse` and `approve` are refused on sessions not shared with them.
13. **Upgrades mid-turn.** With a lane B session mid-turn and running a background shell: `mawsd` restarted, and the app upgraded one minor, leaves the turn completing, no input applied twice and the pending permission shown once; after O16 the same holds for an engine-host upgrade.
14. **The laptop's cost.** With the app closed and no session working, `mawsd` and the engine host on the MacBook stay under an idle CPU and memory bound set by O2's measurement of today's supervisor, memory read as the two processes' total physical footprint (Decision Logs 48 and 57), and idle sessions are released by the laptop's policy (§2).
15. **The local boundary.** Every local socket is owner-checked and refuses symlinks, authenticates before any session data (RL §5.11; a refusal line naming its protocol, build and live count, readable by the same uid only, is allowed, Decision Log 29), and the daemon's artifact endpoint is reachable only through a unix socket or a per-boot token, never an open loopback port.
16. **The owner's verdict.** After a week of daily use across the machines, the owner declares MAWS the one interface, and says what still sent them to a terminal or to desk-web.

## Design

### 1. The shape

```
  member's Mac: MAWS app                            hub (Worker + DOs)              any machine (one OS account)
  ┌───────────────────────────────┐                 ┌────────────────────┐          ┌───────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ renderer (no keys, no paths   │                 │ Registry: org state│   wss    │ mawsd, the machine daemon (Node)      │
  │  of its own; machine-scoped)  │  wss, sealed    │ Machine DO: relay  │ ◀──────▶ │  endpoint module: keys, policy, RL1–4 │
  │ main: windows, client port,   │ ◀─────────────▶ │ Project DO: log    │          │  services: index, read model, sessions│
  │  device keys, connections     │                 └────────────────────┘          │   attention, owner channel, git, scm, │
  └──────────────┬────────────────┘                                                 │   files, library, connectors, catalog │
                 │ same-uid socket (the app's own machine, no hub, no sign-in)      │  ├─ engine host (E10 supervisor):     │
                 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────▶ │  │   Query per session, session hosts │
                                                                                    │  │   └ claude -p (lane B)              │
                                                                                    │  ├─ pty host                          │
                                                                                    │  └─ render service (Chromium; U4)     │
                                                                                    └──────────────▲────────────────────────┘
                                                                                     RL10, local   │
                                                                                    ┌──────────────┴────────────────────────┐
                                                                                    │ sminos daemon (Python, its own unit): │
                                                                                    │  log, delivery, lane A plane          │
                                                                                    │   └ bridge mod in terminal claude     │
                                                                                    └───────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**[binding — the owner's direction (2026-10-26) and the survey's sizing.]** Every machine runs one machine daemon, `mawsd`. It holds the endpoint (the machine's only hub connection, its key, its trust boundary and policy: RL §2), as a module, and MAWS's machine-bound services, which leave Electron main. The engine host stays a separate process under it, so restarting or upgrading `mawsd` never ends a session (Desktop's helper lesson, RL §2.1); once O16's holder lands, an engine-host restart re-adopts its sessions too. The Electron app becomes a client of daemons: its own machine's over a same-uid socket, others' through RL2 channels. The Query of a lane B session stays on the session's machine (owner, 2026-10-26). The alternative, Desktop's SSH split with the Query in the app, costs about the same code (survey §6: 10k–16k new and changed lines against 11k–20k) and leaves a second client, a sleeping laptop and an app-less Linux VM unsolved.

### 2. The machine

**Processes.**
- **`mawsd`** is plain Node, run by a LaunchAgent on macOS and a systemd user unit on Linux. It is the services host of survey §3: main's moved services under a second composition root, built from the same injected dependencies. It is the engine host's single client, as main is today, so E10's single-client machinery (the connect burst, the undelivered tail, `ready`) stays correct; the multi-client fan-out with a cursor per stream and client (RL3) is new code in `mawsd`. The endpoint is a narrow module with its own package and tests; a lint rule lets only it import the key and policy code (architect's ruling, RL Decision Log 6).
- **The engine host** is E10's detached supervisor. It holds the Query of each lane B session (its `Session`), the `maws` tool server and the hook callbacks (survey §6; RL at 65d7fa11e). Restarting `mawsd` drops its client connection, not its children. Through most of P5 it keeps spawning `claude` directly, so an engine-host crash, or an upgrade across an OX1 major, ends its lane B sessions, which reopen with `--resume` (today's behaviour); within a major, an old engine host keeps serving its sessions until it is idle and is then replaced. O16 hardens it as the holder (holder design §3.1–§3.3): a session host per process, the seq ring, the stdin offset, the writer fence and re-adoption, so an engine-host upgrade on a server that never idles ends nothing.
- **The pty host** is forked by `mawsd`, so terminals stop dying with the app (survey §3 S2), with a binary `pty.data` frame.
- **The render service** is Chromium owned by the daemon, for offscreen capture, export and the artifact scheme of agent tools (§6). Two shapes are open and U4's spike decides between them: a second, windowless instance of the app binary beside a plain-Node `mawsd` (headless Chromium on Linux), or `mawsd` itself running as windowless Electron (Electron's headless platform on Linux), which moves capture and export verbatim, keeps `safeStorage` continuity (U7 disappears) and drops a render protocol, at about 150–200 MB more resident memory. The criterion: the single-process shape wins if it runs reliably under a LaunchAgent and on a devcloud VM and its idle memory fits acceptance 14.
- **The sminos daemon** stays its own Python process and unit, and becomes the endpoint module's local client: its log, delivery and lane A plane stay; its uplink, enrollment, HMAC coding and policy move to the endpoint (RL §2.1, RL10).

**Why a services host and not a merge into the supervisor.** The index is synchronous `node:sqlite`, and the library and full-text fill would share one loop with event batching, the `maws` tool server and Yjs commits (survey §3). A merge stays possible later.

**Two composition roots until M2 closes.** The moved services stay a library with two roots: `mawsd`'s and main's embedded one (they already take injected `register`, `emit` and `call`; `src/main/index.ts:226-760`). A profile setting flips between `embedded` and `daemon`; the component harness runs embedded; if a landing step breaks OX7 or daily use, the owner's profile flips back instead of living with it. The embedded root is deleted at O15.

**One daemon per profile.** Today a supervisor is per profile (`src/engine-host/supervisor-files.ts:216-219`), and the e2e harness gives every app a fresh profile (`e2e/support/launch.ts:116`). So the LaunchAgent or systemd unit serves the default profile, and every other profile (an `electron-vite dev` run, an e2e run) gets a private, app-started daemon that exits when idle, which is E10's mechanism generalised. Tests never touch the owner's daemon. A second profile's daemon, with its own `MachineRef`, is also how one Mac stands in for two machines in tests (O6).

**`mawsd` has a command line**, the only window into a machine with no app: `status`, `restart`, `logs`, `enroll`, `keys`, and applying a pending revocation (RL §5.2).

**Settings split three ways.** One `settings.json` today mixes fields of three owners (survey §2 item 12, §7.7). The client's (theme, notifications, layout, window state) stay with the app. The machine's (engine, launch profile, supervisor, MCP servers, sessions) move to `mawsd`. The person's (Discover's catalog sources, Send-to destinations, connector accounts) belong to the signed-in person and live with the client device until they move into the person's signed member entry (RL5), so two Macs agree; machine-side credentials, like an MCP server's OAuth, sign in per machine, with consent opened on the client and the callback relayed to the machine (survey §2 item 7).

**Idle release.** Interest is the union of every client's open tabs and subscriptions, reported to the daemon (OX5), replacing the sweep's read of `layout.json` (survey §7.6). Release follows the machine's role: a laptop keeps today's rule, released 10 minutes after the last interest, because 20 idle sessions are several GB the owner gets back; a server (the mini, a VM) releases after 24 hours by wall clock with no interest and no turn, since today's rule would stop every session on a machine no app attaches to (survey §7.9). O16 replaces the server rule with the holder's verified-empty rule (owner F2, 2026-10-22). A session can be marked keep. Release is strict on every machine (owner, 2026-10-27): a session holding any background work is never released, whatever its clock reads. That means a background Bash job, a running subagent, a Monitor, a session cron, an MCP task or a workflow. Ambient tasks, which the engine marks as its own housekeeping (every `skip_transcript` task, such as a memory extraction, and auto-started watchers), are not background work: E10's rule stands (`src/engine-host/budget.ts:40-48`), since a watcher would otherwise hold its process forever. Tasks are read from `background_tasks_changed`, a level signal that stays known across interrupts. Crons have no stream signal, so the engine host reads Stop's `session_crons` through a new Stop hook callback. Durable crons appear in no Stop, so it also reads `<cwd>/.claude/scheduled_tasks.json`, which a durable `CronCreate` writes, at each Stop and at a resume's launch. It counts the entries whose `createdBySessionId` is any engine session id the session has had (Decision Log 46). A work state the daemon can't read counts as work, and that state is narrow: crons after a cron tool ran in a turn that ended without Stop (an interrupt, an API error), until the next Stop; a resume that doesn't follow a release, until its first Stop, since `--resume` resurrects the session-only crons and one can fire before that Stop (Decision Log 47), unless the engine host's state file shows that the session's last Stop listed no session cron and no cron tool ran after it (Decision Log 49); a scheduled-tasks file that exists but can't be read; and a re-adopted process until a task snapshot or change arrives. O2's probe found no keepalive in `-p`, so Decision Log 29's conditional fourth state does not exist.

**Install, versions and runtime.** `mawsd`, the engine host, the pty host and the endpoint ship as one headless package, versioned apart from the app, with its own Node runtime on Linux; on a Mac with the app the unit may run the app's binary as Node (survey §7.4). Every hello carries a protocol major and minor and capabilities (OX1), never an exact build match. Until signed builds exist (owner, 2026-10-26: an Apple Developer ID is coming), machines install from a source checkout, and device and machine keys are software keys (RL §5.1). The package's decisions are O2's: the Linux runtime and `node:sqlite`'s Node floor, node-pty's Linux build, the uid as the boundary where the darwin-only peer-auth addon is absent, the devenv step for a devcloud VM, the unit's program path when the owner runs from a checkout, and the login-shell capture under a unit that has no login shell. The org-project rule (§3) is sminos's; `mawsd` implements it against a conformance fixture shared by both repositories, with sminos's as the reference.

**Inference per machine.** A machine's sessions use that machine's Claude login or its gateway (charter D-19's relay where ClaudeUsage runs, else the machine's own login), recorded in the session record as its inference route (RL6), never as a token. Account-managed inference through a server-side gateway is a later phase (owner, 2026-10-26).

### 3. The app

The renderer keeps almost all of its code; it never held Electron or Node (research §0.6). Main shrinks to the client: windows, the client port, device keys, the connection manager, voice, and the in-app browser for the person's own browsing.

- **The client port (OX4).** Every effect that only a client can perform (pick and save dialogs, reveal, open-external, notifications, dock and badge, focus) is one injected port that the daemon calls through the client that asked. The daemon never imports Electron.
- **The connection manager.** One connection per machine in use: the local daemon's socket, and an RL2 channel per remote machine. RL2 runs over either carrier: a direct socket to the endpoint on the tailnet, tried first with a short timeout, or the hub's relay (RL1's multiplex). Every machine in the acceptance set is on the owner's tailnet, so remote depth runs at network latency rather than an edge round trip, and survives a hub outage; the hub stays for discovery, presence, listings, the log plane, the phone and machines off the tailnet. O11's U2 measurement sets the default. A machine reachable only directly is drawn as hub-offline, channel-up. A machine's departure greys that machine's sessions and nothing else (survey §7.11).
- **Machine-scoped addresses (OX2).** Every machine-bound id is addressed `machine/id`, where `machine` is a stable id minted once per profile at its first run, by whichever of the app and `mawsd` runs first, into `<profile>/machine.json` (Decision Log 27), and never changes, independent of the machine key, which a rotation can replace, so a signed-out Mac has one and neither a rename, an enrollment nor a key change alters it; the org state's machine entry carries the ref beside the key (RL5's `ref`, `[A-Za-z0-9_-]{16,64}`), and the enrolled name is shown from the org state. Renderer stores key by address; the layout's keys carry the machine. Ids are addresses in the renderer: its client lifts every branded id it receives to `machine/id` and lowers it on send, so the 218 call sites that carry an id route by the address they hold, and the 75 that carry only a path take an explicit machine (survey §4.4; Decision Log 21).
- **Org projects (OX3).** A project is the org's project, as sminos derives it (`.smns`, else the repository's remote on an enrolled machine, else the owning repository), not a local folder. Each machine contributes checkouts of it. The same repository on the MacBook and the mini is one project with two checkouts, and sessions with no project sit under their machine's own project (owner, 2026-10-26; survey §7.10).

### 4. Sessions in MAWS: the two lanes

**[binding — owner decisions of 2026-10-19, 10-22 and 10-24, kept.]** The lane follows where a session started. A session opened from MAWS is lane B on any machine whose daemon runs; a session started in a terminal or as an engine `--bg` job is lane A, controlled by the bridge mod and never moved live (F7). The app draws controls from each session's capability list (RL7, from lane-b-control-map's shared shapes), never from the lane name.

- **Lane B** is MAWS as it is today, served from the session's machine: every control request, every dialog, the `maws` tool server and hooks, rewind, media.
- **Lane A** replaces the read-only foreign-live pane (charter D-12, amended here). The bridge reaches send, steer, interrupt, answers, approvals, model and effort, slash commands, side questions, compact, MCP toggles and media through MW1–MW5. Approvals race the terminal's own dialog and the card says so. A terminal session without the bridge mod stays read-only, with "Take over here" as today. After a lane A process ends, its conversation reopens as lane B with `--resume`.
- **Local lane A needs no sign-in.** The app reaches the bridge through the local daemon, which already hosts the endpoint module.

### 5. Remote surfaces

Everything the panel shows for a local session works for a remote one, over the session's channel.
- **Diff, files and the file editor** read and write through the machine's fs service with ranged, inode-checked reads.
- **The terminal panel** is a shell in the remote worktree from that machine's pty host, with offset de-duplication on the channel (RL §4.8). It is machine-level execution, so it needs the machine owners' standing or an explicit shell right (OX9). This is MAWS's own terminal, not a terminal into a Claude Code session, which F5 (2026-10-23) still rules out.
- **Artifacts and the library** are served over the daemon's HTTP artifact endpoint, which the local app reaches directly and a remote app through the channel; edits travel as Yjs updates as today.
- **Drag and drop** uploads bytes to the session's machine (into its attachments directory) before the message is sent; paths in a message are the machine's.
- **The browser for the person.** A remote dev server is reached by forwarding its port through the channel to the app's in-app browser. How the agent's own browser works on a remote session is unknown U3 (§6).

### 6. Agent tools bound to Chromium

Some `maws` tools need Chromium: `artifact_screenshot`, `artifact_export`, Send to's PDF and DOCX, and browser control (survey §5). On a machine with no app these are agent capabilities lost, not views. So they move onto the machine:
- **Capture and export** go to the render service, which answers with files on the machine or bytes. The attached client is the wrong home, since its fonts and its copy of the artifact are not the machine's.
- **The artifact scheme** becomes an HTTP endpoint on the daemon, keeping today's ranges, sandbox CSP and separate origin; the local app's `protocol.handle` proxies to it.
- **Browser control.** The daemon owns a stable per-session cua socket, checked against the engine pid it holds, so a session launched with no app still gets a browser for that launch (survey §7.5). Where the tab engine runs for a remote session is U3: on the session's machine (the app if it runs there, else the render service), viewed from a remote app by screencast, is the recommended direction, because the agent's tab must reach the machine's localhost.
- **Pane display** (`show_pane`) stays client-bound: it opens in the app of the device that sent the turn's input, else the most recently focused attached app. With notifications, it is the only client-bound tool; this narrows RL §3.1's list, and the divergence is recorded there.

### 7. People

The org is the owner alone for the next months, and the people model is built in from the start, so a second member is an org-state edit, not a redesign (owner, 2026-10-26).

- **Owners.** A session belongs to the person who spawned it: a terminal session to its machine's owner, a MAWS spawn to the signed requester, a `sminos spawn` to the spawning session's owners, copied at spawn (RL §5.8). Owners can add owners ("A, B and 43+").
- **Visibility** is set per session by its owners, like a shared document: `private`, `listed` (the default: every member sees owner, machine and state, contents locked), `org-view` or `org-converse`. Shares grant `view`, `converse` or `approve` (owner, 2026-10-26; RL §6.2).
- **Machines** have owners (members or the org) and per-machine rights per member; spawning on someone else's machine needs its owners' grant (owner, 2026-10-26; RL §6.3).
- **Agents talk as today.** A message between agents of different people is today's `sminos say`, plus the owner label of the sending session, signed by its machine (RL9). It never counts as a person's words and never approves anything, which is today's engine rule as well (identity §2). No new delivery policy (owner, 2026-10-26).
- **Credentials.** The session record names its principal and its inference route. Who may drive a session on someone else's personal login is decided by the later account-managed inference phase, not built here (owner, 2026-10-26, after the terms finding of identity §3).

### 8. The interface

- **The sidebar** keeps Codex's flat project tree (charter D-2) with org projects. Each project lists the person's own sessions; a per-project toggle adds other people's, each with its owner and machine (owner, 2026-10-26). Rows carry a machine chip; an offline machine's rows grey with "machine offline".
- **The project channel.** Each project gets a channel view, opened like a tab: the desk's group chat (desk §The scene) in MAWS. It shows everything the project's sessions sent to a person (to-human messages, questions, permission cards) across machines, agent-to-agent traffic folded to one row, and people's own messages. The cards in it are the same pending requests attention shows, so answering in either place settles both; the channel adds no second state. To-human messages and asks travel on each machine's channels, not the hub's log (RL §6.2), so the channel assembles them per machine, and an offline machine's rows show from the app's cache. **[review: taste]**
- **"+ Session"** gains a machine picker (default: this Mac), the checkout and worktree of the project on that machine, and says which lane and which inference route the session will get.
- **The Org page** in Settings: sign-in, members and their devices, machines with enrollment codes, per-machine rights, and the recovery key. Desk-web's org, key and machines views (`apps/desk-web/src/views/{org,key,machines}`) are its reference.
- **Overview and attention** span machines; the dock badge and ⌘⇧J count every reachable session.

### 9. The phone

Desk Web pauses, and its phone loop stays: push notifications, and question and permission cards, and nothing else (owner, 2026-10-26). It moves to the `web-cards` client class over RL2 in WebCrypto with a per-action WebAuthn assertion (RL §5.10). Desk-web's other views freeze and stop being maintained. A MAWS web port is a later phase.

### 10. Local parity and the latency budget

### O10: The endpoint module and the protocol package (desk side) — plan

**Purpose.** It gives every machine one authenticated, encrypted way in. The endpoint module inside `mawsd`:

- enforces the machine's policy and each session's grades;

2. **Resume without loss.** Against O11's staging hub, a device opens a channel to a machine and subscribes to a lane A and a lane B session. It resumes by cursor, with no loss and no duplicate, across:
   - a dropped hub socket;
   - a hub deploy (stale-generation);

   - an action replayed by the relay is refused;

## Cross-child contracts

- **RL1–RL10** as the remote layer's Interfaces section states them, lifted whole and binding (RL §Interfaces).
- **OX1 Wire compatibility.** Every hello (app to `mawsd`, `mawsd` to the engine host, endpoint to hub, device to endpoint) keeps the socket preamble's stable fields (`t`, `protocol` as an integer, `token`; `src/shared/host/socket-protocol.ts:6-8`), with `protocol` read as the major, and adds `minor`, `build` and `capabilities` beside them. Peers accept any minor of their major; changes within a major are additive; a removal or rename takes a major, with the old form served for one release.
- **OX2 Machine-scoped address.** `MachineRef` is a stable id minted once per profile at its first run, by whichever of the app and `mawsd` runs first (`[A-Za-z0-9_-]{16,64}`, at least 128 random bits), kept in `<profile>/machine.json` through one module both use, and never changes, independent of the machine key; RL5's machine entry carries it as `ref`, set at enrollment, immutable and unique, and every contract field named `machine` carries the ref. The enrolled name is shown from the org state. Every machine-bound id travels as `{machine, id}`; layout, drafts and stores key by it; the local machine is a machine like any other. In the renderer an address is the string `<ref>/<id>`, joined and split only by O6's codec, and drafts stay in their session's machine's index, reached through the address (Decision Log 27).
- **OX3 Org project.** `OrgProject {id, name, checkouts: [{machine, path, projectId}]}`, derived from sminos's project identity. Every checkout yields a project: a repository with no remote, or a folder with no repository, is a project of its own on its machine rather than folded into the machine's project, so every project the sidebar shows today stays. Only sessions with no checkout belong to their machine's project. A checkout is a project someone opened on its machine; a session belongs to its machine's project when its directory's org project has no checkout on any visible machine, or when it has no project. `OrgProject.id` is a key enrollment doesn't change (`smns:`, `remote:`, `repo:`, `folder:` or `machine:`), and the record carries `kind` and `smnsId`, sminos's id for joins. Persisted keys survive enrollment (Decision Logs 21 and 27).
- **OX4 Client port.** `{pick, save, confirm, reveal, openExternal, notify, badge, focus}`, called by the daemon through the requesting client; a request with no client answers `noClient` and the caller degrades, never hangs. `openExternal` takes a URL or a path, and `focus` either brings the window forward or requests attention (the dock bounce). `confirm` is a yes/no question parented to the client's window (Decision Log 20).
- **OX5 Interest.** Each client reports its open sessions and subscriptions; the daemon's idle release and visible set read the union.
- **OX6 Session record.** RL6, plus the lane B binding (holder design §3.1) and the inference route.
- **OX7 Local latency budget.** §10. Delta-to-paint is measured as delta to frame: from the engine's write to the end of the frame that renders it on the renderer's main thread, GPU presentation excluded (Decision Log 20).
- **OX8 App ↔ hub.** The app's own hub connection, beside RL2's channels: the org-state chain, signed listings (the sidebar and its toggle), the project log plane (`sminos say` traffic and the project channel's agent rows), push registration and device enrollment. O11 builds the hub half; O6 and O8 consume it; the app verifies RL8 signatures against the org state it holds.
- **OX9 Namespace grades.** Every forwarded namespace declares its grade in the registry: reads of a session's checkout (`fs.read`, `git.diff`, `scm.status`, `artifacts.read`) are `view` on that session; writes (`fs.write`, git mutations, `artifacts.edit`) are `manage`, or `spawn` on the machine; `pty.*` is the machine owners' or an explicit shell right; machine settings, connectors and the catalog's machine half are `machine-admin`. The endpoint refuses an undeclared namespace. The grades are three kinds, and no kind is ranked against another: the session ranks `list` to `manage` in RL §5.5's order; the machine grades `spawn`, `shell` and `machine-admin`; and the routing classes `client` (served by the app, never sent to a daemon) and `internal` (a daemon's own services only), which no caller holds. A command whose grade depends on its input declares a floor and is graded by its effect at the endpoint (Decision Log 20).
- **OX10 The reaping gate (proposed).** desk-sminos-orchestrator proposed it. O12's M4 provides it and O16 consumes it. It binds once O16's execution document adopts it, and U6 gates it. Its current text is O12's execution document (M4 and Interfaces §C): the engine host serves `MAWS_HOLDER_SOCKET` (0600). The observer bridge sends `hello` and a 5 s heartbeat. The host arms an acknowledged fence with a lease (`arm {fence, leaseMs}`, answered `armed`) and signals only after `armed` and within the lease. While armed, the observer's `turn.step` asks `turn {fence, origin}` and gets `proceed` or `stop`. With no answer, it proceeds only after the lease has expired plus one heartbeat. A stopped turn's trigger stays owed. That needs a delivery settlement correlated with the turn's verdict, because today sminos's `deliver_next` settles an inbox delivery when `push_live` hands it to the engine, before `turn.step` decides. O16 defines that settlement when it adopts OX10, and O12 builds the sminos side. O16 also decides whether a session with no observer connected may be released on verified-empty alone.
- **The protocol package.** RL2's handshake and frames, RL4's canonical action, RL5's org state and RL8's signatures are one package with shared test vectors, `apps/org-protocol` in the `apps/*` workspace. O11 creates it with RL5 and RL8 and O10 extends it with RL2 and RL4. It is imported by the hub, `mawsd`, the app's main and desk-web's WebCrypto build. No consumer reimplements them.
