## Purpose

After O3-C the spine (the session index and read model, attention's core, the owner channel and mods, the session service with intents, worktrees, prompt history, side threads, yield, observed facts and the sweep, settings' machine half, the library's derived index, lane A's local driver and the design-system defaults relay) is a library that any process can compose, and everything that stays in the app reads it through one view. It still runs in Electron main. So today, with the app quit, nothing folds a session's events into the index, attention stops deriving, a remote device's subscription to a lane B session pauses `no-app`, and no second client can read the machine's sessions.

O3-D moves the spine's process. After it, on the daemon shape (`supervisor.shape: daemon`, the owner's since O2):

- **`mawsd` composes the spine and serves it to every client.** The app, a second local client and the endpoint's remote devices read the same services on `mawsd`'s `machine.sock`, each with its own session streams and cursors. The app forwards the moved namespaces there and reads its view through a mirror in main, synchronous for its readers.
- **The engine host's one client is permanent.** `mawsd`'s connection to the engine host no longer recycles per app attachment: it folds every event, with or without an app, and the engine host learns whether an app is attached from an explicit signal. A pane, capture, export or dataset refresh with no app attached settles `noClient` at once, as today.
- **The services outlive the app.** Quit MAWS; a lane B session keeps working, its events keep folding, and reopening shows it with no gap.
- **The laptop pays for none of it while idle.** `mawsd` starts without the spine, loads it when something needs it, releases it when nothing does, and re-executes into its cold core when release alone leaves it over acceptance 14's bound; a measured scene decides which, before the cutover is built on an assumption.
- **Nothing regresses.** Signed out, on the daemon shape, MAWS does everything it did; the embedded shape still runs the spine in main, and the owner's profile can flip back.

**How to see it working.** On a scratch profile on the daemon shape, open MAWS, start a session, quit MAWS mid-turn with the session kept; `mawsd logs --follow` shows the turn's events folding (`[spine] …`); `mawsd status` shows the spine `loaded`; reopen MAWS and the session is in the sidebar with its digest current and its pending permission once. Wait with the app closed and no session working: `mawsd status` shows the spine `cold` and `mawsd`'s pid has changed (the re-execution), with the engine host's pid unchanged. Concrete Steps has each command; the e2e groups named in Acceptance prove each behaviour on the gate's Macs.

### Orientation

Terms used throughout:

- **The spine:** §5's set that moves here, as `startSpine` composes it (`src/services/spine-root.ts`); **the checkouts** (`startCheckouts`): git, scm, files and `git.lsFiles`, which stay in main until O3-E and read the spine through the view.
- **The daemon shape:** `supervisor.shape: daemon`; the app reaches `mawsd` through three detached transports over its sockets (`src/main/hosts/daemon.ts` `daemonHostSpecs`): `engine-host.sock` and `pty-host.sock` are relays to the hosts, one app at a time (`src/mawsd/relay.ts`), `machine.sock` serves many clients at once (`src/mawsd/daemon.ts`, `serveSocketClients` with `many`). **The embedded shape** (`embedded`): main composes the spine and reaches the engine host directly.
- **The front** (`ServicesFront`, Interfaces §B): what `machine.sock` becomes: the spine's commands and events, the daemon's own namespace, the hosts' commands forwarded, session streams with cursors, and the client port's requests back to the asking client.
- **The seam** (`LazySpine`, Interfaces §A): how `mawsd` loads, holds, releases and re-executes the spine.
- **The mirror** (`createMirrorSpineView`, Interfaces §F): main's `SpineView` on the daemon shape, fed by the front's snapshot commands and events.
- **The attachment** (Interfaces §D): the engine host's knowledge that an app is attached, told by `mawsd` under a capability.
- **A client** of the front: a welcomed connection on `machine.sock` (the app's `machine` transport, the CLI, a second local client in a test, O6's link from another profile's app). **The app** is the client whose hello's `appPid` matches the app attached on `engine-host.sock`.
- **Cold, loaded, released:** the seam's states; **re-execution:** `mawsd` exiting 75 after a release so its unit restarts it into the cold core.

How the parts fit on master. `mawsd` (`src/mawsd/daemon.ts`) runs one `HostSupervisor` over two on-demand detached hosts; its engine-host link is `readyBy: 'external'` and recycled at each app attach and departure, so between attachments it never says ready and the engine host sees one client per attachment, with E10's burst and tail per connection. `machine.sock` serves `daemon.status`, `daemon.restart` and `daemon.endpoint` through a `createHostRuntime` per client. The endpoint seam (`DaemonServices`, `src/mawsd/endpoint-seam.ts`) gives O10's module `dispatch`, `onEvent` (engine-host events, only while an app is attached), `onAttachment`, document channels, `hold` and `interest`. The app's router binds `machine` embedded (`src/main/ipc/router.ts`), so the four `machine`-owned commands run in main; main's `machine` transport exists on the daemon shape and carries only `daemon.*`. In main, `startMain` composes the spine through `src/services/entry.ts` and hands its view to every staying reader (O3-C's reader map, its document's Interfaces §C); `src/shared/ipc/wire.ts` builds the manifest from `engine-host`, `pty-host` and `machine` owners, `HOST_EVENTS` and `PTY_HOST_EVENTS`.

### M2 — The spine in `mawsd`: cold, loaded, released, re-executed, and the measured reading

**At its end.** `mawsd` can compose the spine from `out/main/services.js`'s chunk, loaded on demand and never at start: `src/mawsd/spine-seam.ts` (Interfaces §A) takes `spine.lock` and runs `openSpineData` and `startSpine` with `mawsd`'s bindings at the first trigger, serves the spine's commands and events through the front's core on `machine.sock` (Interfaces §B, its command table, event fan-out and forwarded owners; not yet the streams or the client port), releases the spine's data after a quiet period, collects through the idle collector's `dropped()`, and, as a unit daemon, exits 75 so its unit restarts it cold. `daemon.status` carries the spine's state. ### Interfaces and Dependencies

#### A. The spine seam (`src/mawsd/spine-seam.ts`)

    export type SpineState = 'cold' | 'loading' | 'loaded' | 'refused' | 'releasing' | 'yielded';

    export interface LazySpineOptions {
      profileDir: string;
      build: string;                               // `${version}+${commit}`, the lock's buildId
      load(): Promise<typeof import('../services/entry')>; // the deferred chunk (a test brings a stand-in)
      deps(): Omit<SpineDeps, 'data'>;             // mawsd's bindings, built once the chunk is loaded (below)
      quietMs?: number;                            // SPINE_QUIET_MS, 60_000
      onState(state: SpineState, detail: string | null): void; // daemon.status.spine, the log
      log(line: string): void;
    }
    export interface LazySpine {
      state(): SpineState;
      // Loads if cold (or refused: a refusal is retried at the next trigger), then answers; rejects with the refusal while
      // the lock is held by a live process, or while yielded.
      ensure(reason: 'command' | 'event' | 'endpoint'): Promise<SpineRoot>;
      root(): SpineRoot | null;                    // loaded only
      quiet(quiet: boolean): void;                 // told at each evaluation of the daemon's facts (clocks.ts)
      yield(pid: number | null): void;             // a live pre-O3-D app's pid: release now and refuse every load while it lives; null lifts it
      release(): Promise<void>;                    // stops every part (SpineRoot.stop), drops every reference, then the collector's dropped()
      stop(): Promise<void>;                       // the daemon's exit order: release without re-execution
    }

States and transitions: `cold` → `loading` at a trigger (a `machine` command of the spine's from any client, an endpoint `dispatch('machine', …)` of one, a `session.eventBatch` from the engine host); `loading` → `loaded` once `openSpineData` holds the lock and `startSpine` returned, or → `refused` (the lock held by a live process, found after `openSpineData`'s own 5 s wait: every spine command answers `host_unavailable` naming the holder, the daemon's `spine.refused` carries it, and a trigger tries again only once a minute has passed since the refusal); `loaded` → `releasing` once quiet has held for `quietMs` → `cold`, then, for a unit daemon, `stop('spine released', EXIT_RESTART)` (D6); any state → `yielded` on `yield(pid)` while that pid lives (checked by pid and start time every 5 s), which releases a loaded spine and its lock, → `cold` when it is gone. Requests that arrive while `loading` wait for it; while `releasing` they wait for `cold` and re-trigger. Quiet is the daemon's `clocks.quiet()` (no client, no hold, empty interest) and no working session: no engine status with `process` other than `idle` or `exited` and `view.midTurnCount() === 0`, read from the spine's view at each evaluation. The coalesced parts (`session.status`, `session.queue`, `session.brokerItems`, `session.catalogChanged` per session; `engine.budget`) are kept in the core as the latest per key while cold and replayed through the spine's `hosts.onEvent` listeners once loaded, before live events.

`mawsd`'s `SpineDeps` bindings: `profile` the profile; `machine` its ref; `hosts` an adapter over its `HostSupervisor` whose `dispatch` and `forward` to the engine host pass through `interceptEngine` first (so `host.configure`, `host.interest`, `host.stopAll` and `host.exitWhenIdle` from the spine reach the clocks as the app's did), whose `onEvent` validates against this build's registry, and whose `setResync` runs the resync at once when the link already streams (D8); `bus` the front's binding (§B); `clientPort` §E's; `interest` the union (`union()`); `browserSocket` the attached app's path from `machine.client`, null with no app; `trash` D10's; `engineEnv` one `captureShellEnvironment()` per load; `env` the daemon's; `packaged` as `daemon.ts` reads it; `appPath` the app root above `out/` (`join(dirname(modules.self), '..', '..')`, the asar in a packaged app); `devRoot` `process.cwd()`; `home` `homedir()`.

- **D1. 2026-10-29, O3-D's planner: the verification.** This document gets an independent spec review (a general-purpose reviewer at the frontier) and doperpowers:adversarial-reviewer's buildability review of its execution section, repeated until a round yields nothing worth changing; the rounds are recorded below. - **D2. The split judgement, at dispatch (the composite's instruction).** O3-D's residue has distinct state owners (the engine host's attachment, `mawsd`'s core, main's mirror) but none of them lands usefully alone: a retagged command with no server, a mirror with no front, or a permanent connection with no spine folding in `mawsd` is an invalid intermediate state, and O3 Decision Log 3 already rejected the permanent client before the spine moves. The differing verification strategies are milestone boundaries here, not children. Rejected: a decomposing pass (every cut found landed an incomplete cutover or re-made O3's rejected cut).
- **D3. Lane B events reach a post-D local client on the front, with a stream and a cursor per session, not on the engine-host relay.** The relay's raw delivery had its catch-up from the engine host's per-connection burst, which the permanent connection ends; a second local client cannot attach to the relay at all (one app at a time); and §8 binds a cursor per stream and client. So the front carries every event a client receives, the relay only requests, and O10 M8's catch-up becomes one module (Interfaces §C). Rejected: raw delivery on the relay plus a replay on the front (two sources for one stream: ordering and duplicates at every reattach); the app following only its interest set (the renderer receives every session's batches today, and narrowing it is O7's to design with its subscriptions).
- **D4. Two capabilities, one per direction.** `host.attachment` on the engine host's welcome says it serves the signal and on a client's hello that it will send it; `machine.spine` on an app's hello says it runs no spine of its own. Only `mawsd` sends the first; only a post-D app sends the second. Rejected: one capability (the engine host would then wait for a signal an embedded app never sends, or `mawsd` could not tell a pre-D app from an embedded one); `WIRE_CAPABILITIES` gaining both for every peer (the same confusion, built in).
- **D5. §11's mechanism is decided by M2's scene, not assumed.** Release alone stands only if every one of three valid runs reads the released set under the bound by master's own margin; else re-execution is the product's path and the re-executed window is the acceptance reading. The arithmetic of O3 Decision Log 15 predicts re-execution, and the code for both is built in M2 either way, since release precedes re-execution and the measurement needs both. Rejected: building re-execution only after the cutover misses (a second measured series and a re-plan on the critical path).
- **D6. Re-execution is exit 75 for a unit daemon, after a 60 s quiet; a private daemon releases but never re-executes.** The units restart on 75 (never on 0), so no self-spawn is needed, and the restart is the one O2 already tests (`daemon.restart`); a private daemon has no unit and is started by the app on demand, so with no client its spine's release is enough and its own exit clock keeps O2's rule (an enrolled private daemon must stay for the hub, and an e2e daemon must stay for its scene). The quiet period is short because the measured window's 120 s settle must find the released state; the cost is a cold load when the app returns within a minute of quitting, bounded by the cold-start budget. Rejected: a self-spawned successor (a lock hand-over with a window, a second process tree under launchd's job); exit 0 (the unit would not restart it); the idle time (10 minutes) as the quiet period (the measured settle could not see the release).
- **D7. The measured scene runs the daemon as the harness's unit.** `e2e:daemon-idle`'s daemon is the app's private one, which never re-executes (D6); to measure the product's unit path the harness starts `mawsd run --unit` in the scratch profile before anything else and restarts it on 75 as launchd would, following the new pid; the app's launch, where the scene has one, finds the daemon up (the lock arbitrates, as on the default profile). The existing `daemon idle` case's `daemon.kind === 'private'` check becomes `unit`, and its validity rule admits the deliberate exit 75 as a restart, not a lost process. Rejected: measuring the private daemon's released state as acceptance 12 (it is not what the owner's LaunchAgent runs).
- **D8. The spine, started on a link that already streams, runs its resync at once; the core holds no batch.** `resyncDigests` reads every row's status and its events after what it folded, through the fold filter, so everything that arrived before the spine subscribed is recovered from the engine host's ring and journal, and the live events it then hears pass the filter once. Rejected: a bounded hold of batches in the core (a second recovery path with its own loss rule, which the resync makes redundant).
- **D9. A `session.eventBatch` wakes the spine; the replace-whole parts coalesce and replay at load.** As §11 advises. A broker item alone does not wake it: the phone's lane B cards come from the engine host's `session.pendingItems` through the endpoint (O10 M8), and attention derives for the app when it attaches. Rejected: waking on any engine-host event (a periodic status would keep the spine resident on an idle laptop).
- **D10. `trash` is `mawsd`'s own move into the user's trash directory.** OX4 names no trash, and the session service deletes files on the machine; a GUI trash through the app would hang nothing but would make a machine-side deletion depend on a client. Rejected: adding `trash` to OX4 (a contract change for a file move).
- **D11. Launch mode `scenario` runs where the spine runs.** A scenario launch is a development or e2e launch on a non-default profile, whose private `mawsd` inherits the environment; main makes the scenario path absolute before the launch so `mawsd`'s `devRoot` is not consulted for it. In scenario the view is unavailable (O3-C's D10), so the endpoint's checkout source reads no scenario project (O3-C's D28 question): a remote spawn never runs in a scenario's directory. Rejected: forcing scenario launches embedded (every scenario-driven e2e group would leave the daemon shape O3-B put it on).
- **D12. Q1 applies to the daemon shape.** There Quit asks about unsaved files only, sends no stop-all and offers no keep; the embedded shape keeps today's question until O15, because a utility engine host ends with the app and the detached one's keep is O2's. Rejected: dropping the question on both shapes (a utility host's sessions would end mid-turn with no word).
- **D13. The restored marks and the project rows travel as optional fields of the events that already carry their change** (`attention.changed.silent`, `session.projects.changed.rows`), and the in-process view reads the same fields from M1. Rejected: a machine-only event for each (two more names for state that changes exactly when the existing event fires).
- **D14. The mirror snapshots through the existing listing commands plus three additions** (`session.visible`, `library.fileLinkedPaths`, the rows field), follows the existing events, and lets an event win over the snapshot it raced (Interfaces §F) rather than ordering the two. Rejected: one `machine.snapshot` command carrying everything (a second shape for every record type, to be kept equal to the listings); a resync before `ready` on the `machine` transport, as the engine host's link does (the spine's events have no delivered cursor, so an event emitted between the snapshot's read and `ready` would be lost rather than re-sent).
- **D15. The client port is an event and a command** (Interfaces §E). Rejected: a reverse request kind (breaking within the major).
- **D16. `machine.identity` answers cold**, from the ref `mawsd` read at start and its name, so a link's first request needs no spine. Rejected: loading the spine for it (the e2e:machines link would load B's spine at every reconnect).
- **D17. The ledger covers claimed acts (`machine.op`); a local client's own acts run unclaimed**, as the engine host's local acts do beside `host.op`. The file is `<profile>/spine-ops.jsonl`, a spine file under the lock. Rejected: every act through the ledger (a claim for an IPC call that has no retry).
- **D18. The rewind repair is a last-act note beside the session's intent**, written by the `session.rewind` intercept before the forward and read at resume. Rejected: the ledger as the note's home (the local rewind is unclaimed, D17).
- **D19. Engine statuses in the mirror come from the engine host** (`engine.sessions`, `session.status`), not from a `machine` command: the quit guard's mid-turn count must not depend on the spine being loaded. Rejected: a `session.engineStatuses` machine command.
- **D20. The settings' machine half in main starts from the file, read-only, then follows the front.** Rejected: waiting for `machineSettings.get` before the first window (the cold start would hold the window).
- **D21. The `SingletonLock` signal is verified before it is relied on** (M5's probe), and the first signal (the refused hello's `appPid`) stands alone if it fails, with the residual recorded for the architect. Rejected: relying on Chromium's Linux behaviour for macOS.
- **D22. The O6 link over `mawsd` follows no session stream.** `e2e:machines` reads B's index, drafts and transcripts by command and its events are the spine's; a live B stream in A's transcript is O7's. Rejected: following A's visible B sessions (interest per machine does not exist in main yet).
- **D23. One branch, one PR, one bump.** M1's wire change is additive and the gate's own cross-build covers it, but landing it alone would spend a `WIRE_MINOR` on a change no build serves. Rejected: landing M1 or M3 ahead as their own PRs to shrink the panel's diff (two bumps, and §9 names one).
