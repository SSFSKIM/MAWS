## Purpose

After O3-C the spine (the session index and read model, attention's core, the owner channel and mods, the session service with intents, worktrees, prompt history, side threads, yield, observed facts and the sweep, settings' machine half, the library's derived index, lane A's local driver and the design-system defaults relay) is a library that any process can compose, and everything that stays in the app reads it through one view. It still runs in Electron main. So today, with the app quit, nothing folds a session's events into the index, attention stops deriving, a remote device's subscription to a lane B session pauses `no-app`, and no second client can read the machine's sessions.

O3-D moves the spine's process. After it, on the daemon shape (`supervisor.shape: daemon`, the owner's since O2):

- **`mawsd` composes the spine and serves it to every client.** The app, a second local client and the endpoint's remote devices read the same services on `mawsd`'s `machine.sock`, each with its own session streams and cursors. The app forwards the moved namespaces there and reads its view through a mirror in main, synchronous for its readers.
- **The engine host's one client is permanent.** `mawsd`'s connection to the engine host no longer recycles per app attachment: it folds every event, with or without an app, and the engine host learns whether an app is attached from an explicit signal. A pane, capture, export or dataset refresh with no app attached settles `noClient` at once, as today.
- **The services outlive the app.** Quit MAWS; a lane B session keeps working, its events keep folding, and reopening shows it with no gap.
- **The laptop pays for none of it while idle.** `mawsd` starts without the spine, loads it when something needs it, releases it when nothing does, and re-executes into its cold core when release alone leaves it over acceptance 14's bound; a measured scene decides which, before the cutover is built on an assumption.
- **Nothing regresses.** Signed out, on the daemon shape, MAWS does everything it did; the embedded shape still runs the spine in main, and the owner's profile can flip back.

**How to see it working.** On a scratch profile on the daemon shape, open MAWS, start a session, quit MAWS mid-turn with the session kept; `mawsd logs --follow` shows the turn's events folding (`[spine] …`); `mawsd status` shows the spine `loaded`; reopen MAWS and the session is in the sidebar with its digest current and its pending permission once. Wait with the app closed and no session working: `mawsd status` shows the spine `cold` and `mawsd`'s pid has changed (the re-execution), with the engine host's pid unchanged. Concrete Steps has each command; the e2e groups named in Acceptance prove each behaviour on the gate's Macs.

### Orientation

Terms used throughout:

- **The spine:** §5's set that moves here, as `startSpine` composes it (`src/services/spine-root.ts`); **the checkouts** (`startCheckouts`): git, scm, files and `git.lsFiles`, which stay in main until O3-E and read the spine through the view.
- **The daemon shape:** `supervisor.shape: daemon`; the app reaches `mawsd` through three detached transports over its sockets (`src/main/hosts/daemon.ts` `daemonHostSpecs`): `engine-host.sock` and `pty-host.sock` are relays to the hosts, one app at a time (`src/mawsd/relay.ts`), `machine.sock` serves many clients at once (`src/mawsd/daemon.ts`, `serveSocketClients` with `many`). **The embedded shape** (`embedded`): main composes the spine and reaches the engine host directly.
- **The front** (`ServicesFront`, Interfaces §B): what `machine.sock` becomes: the spine's commands and events, the daemon's own namespace, the hosts' commands forwarded, session streams with cursors, and the client port's requests back to the asking client.
- **The seam** (`LazySpine`, Interfaces §A): how `mawsd` loads, holds, releases and re-executes the spine.
- **The mirror** (`createMirrorSpineView`, Interfaces §F): main's `SpineView` on the daemon shape, fed by the front's snapshot commands and events.
- **The attachment** (Interfaces §D): the engine host's knowledge that an app is attached, told by `mawsd` under a capability.
- **A client** of the front: a welcomed connection on `machine.sock` (the app's `machine` transport, the CLI, a second local client in a test, O6's link from another profile's app). **The app** is the client whose hello's `appPid` matches the app attached on `engine-host.sock`.
- **Cold, loaded, released:** the seam's states; **re-execution:** `mawsd` exiting 75 after a release so its unit restarts it into the cold core.

How the parts fit on master. `mawsd` (`src/mawsd/daemon.ts`) runs one `HostSupervisor` over two on-demand detached hosts; its engine-host link is `readyBy: 'external'` and recycled at each app attach and departure, so between attachments it never says ready and the engine host sees one client per attachment, with E10's burst and tail per connection. `machine.sock` serves `daemon.status`, `daemon.restart` and `daemon.endpoint` through a `createHostRuntime` per client. The endpoint seam (`DaemonServices`, `src/mawsd/endpoint-seam.ts`) gives O10's module `dispatch`, `onEvent` (engine-host events, only while an app is attached), `onAttachment`, document channels, `hold` and `interest`. The app's router binds `machine` embedded (`src/main/ipc/router.ts`), so the four `machine`-owned commands run in main; main's `machine` transport exists on the daemon shape and carries only `daemon.*`. In main, `startMain` composes the spine through `src/services/entry.ts` and hands its view to every staying reader (O3-C's reader map, its document's Interfaces §C); `src/shared/ipc/wire.ts` builds the manifest from `engine-host`, `pty-host` and `machine` owners, `HOST_EVENTS` and `PTY_HOST_EVENTS`.

### Interfaces and Dependencies

#### A. The spine seam (`src/mawsd/spine-seam.ts`)

    export type SpineState = 'cold' | 'loading' | 'loaded' | 'refused' | 'releasing' | 'yielded';

    export interface LazySpineOptions {
      profileDir: string;
      build: string;                               // `${version}+${commit}`, the lock's buildId
      load(): Promise<typeof import('../services/entry')>; // the deferred chunk (a test brings a stand-in)

      onState(state: SpineState, detail: string | null): void; // daemon.status.spine, the log
      log(line: string): void;
    }
    export interface LazySpine {
      state(): SpineState;
      // Loads if cold (or refused: a refusal is retried at the next trigger), then answers; rejects with the refusal while
      // the lock is held by a live process, or while yielded.
      ensure(reason: 'command' | 'event' | 'endpoint'): Promise<SpineRoot>;
      root(): SpineRoot | null;                    // loaded only
      held(event: { name: string; payload: unknown }): void; // an engine-host event while cold or loading (the hold, below)
      quiet(quiet: boolean): void;                 // told at each evaluation of the daemon's facts (clocks.ts)
      yield(pid: number | null): void;             // a live pre-O3-A app's pid (§L): release and give the lock up, then refuse every load while it lives; null lifts it
      release(): Promise<void>;                    // waits for root.settled(), stops every part (SpineRoot.stop), drops every reference, then the collector's dropped()
      stop(): Promise<void>;                       // the daemon's exit order: release without re-execution
    }

    // src/services/spine-root.ts, split for a process that loads more than once (M2): the lock is the process's, the data a load's
    export function takeSpineLock(profile: string, options: { build: string }): Promise<SpineLock>;        // once per process; released at exit, or by a yield
    export function openSpineData(profile: string, options: { build: string; lock?: SpineLock }): Promise<SpineData>; // under a given lock, the split and the machine half; without one, O3-A's one-shot form (main's)
    export interface SpineRoot { readonly view: SpineView; settled(): Promise<void>; stop(): void }         // settled: no session setup, mutation, sweep run or owed write in flight (each part reports)

States and transitions: `cold` → `loading` at a trigger (a `machine` command of the spine's from any client, an endpoint `dispatch('machine', …)` of one, a `session.eventBatch` from the engine host); `loading` → `loaded` once the lock is held (`takeSpineLock` the first time; afterwards the one the seam holds), `openSpineData` ran under it and `startSpine` returned, or → `refused` (the lock held by a live process, found after the lock's own 5 s wait: every spine command answers `host_unavailable` naming the holder, the daemon's `spine.refused` carries it, and a trigger tries again only once a minute has passed since the refusal); `loaded` → `releasing` once quiet has held for `quietMs()`: from then on the root admits no new client work (a request waits) while engine-host events keep folding into the still-loaded spine, and the seam waits for `root.settled()` with no deadline that hands anything over (a wait past 30 s logs the waiters every 30 s and goes on; a release never closes the index under a writer). With `reexecute` the order is terminal: the engine-host link is ended first with the transport's terminal draining close (`detached-transport.ts`'s `stop()`, which reconnects nothing; `recycle` would), every batch the drain delivers folds into the loaded spine (R4: the engine host's delivered cursor moves as it writes, so nothing received before the close may be left for the successor's burst, which starts after that cursor), `settled()` is awaited again, the parts stop → `cold`, then `stop('spine released', EXIT_RESTART)`. Without `reexecute` (a private daemon, and a unit daemon while the switch is off, the product's default by D38) the parts stop → `cold` with the link open, the hold starts at that moment, and a hold that gathers a `session.eventBatch` is a trigger: the spine loads again at once rather than losing it (D5, D6); any state → `yielded` on `yield(pid)` while that pid lives (checked by pid and start time every 5 s): a loaded spine admits no new work, waits for `settled()` and gives the lock up only then; a yield whose writers have not settled within 60 s ends the process instead (`stop('yield', EXIT_RESTART)` for a unit daemon, exit 0 for a private one, whose old app starts its own daemon anyway, §L), since an exit ends every writer and releases the lock by the exit path, and an in-process hand-over with a writer alive is the two-writer state §2 forbids (D27); → `cold` when the pid is gone. Requests that arrive while `loading` wait for it; while `releasing` they wait for `cold` and re-trigger. Quiet is the daemon's `clocks.quiet()` (no client, no hold, empty interest) and no working session, read from the spine's view at each evaluation: no live status (`process` in `starting`, `initializing`, `ready`) whose `keepAlive` (the engine host's own hard reasons, `src/shared/engine/status.ts`) names `turn`, `busy` or `background_tasks`, or whose `engineState` is `running` or `requires_action` (R4: an engine-started turn runs with a null `turn` and an empty `keepAlive`, `src/engine-host/process-table.test.ts`'s fixture, and `budget.ts` checks the engine state apart for that reason); `pending_request`, `queued_messages`, `held_peer_message` and `stateful_mcp` keep the process, not the spine (nothing arrives until an answer, which is a trigger); none `starting` or `initializing` (`midTurnCountOf` counts those as launching); and the root not busy (`settled()` would resolve). A kept session that is `ready` and `idle` with an empty `keepAlive` is not working: it permits the release and is not ended by it.

**The hold (§11's "`mawsd` holds what arrives in order until the spine has loaded and folded it").** From the trigger until the load's barrier below has passed, the core keeps every engine-host event in arrival order in `held` (never during a release, whose events go through the loaded spine's listeners as the transition above says; a non-re-executing release starts the next hold when its parts stop); the coalesced parts (`session.status`, `session.queue`, `session.brokerItems`, `session.catalogChanged` per session, `engine.budget`) are kept as the latest per key for as long as the spine is cold, so a periodic status wakes nothing (D9). The load is one ordered barrier (E10's "the resync before `ready`", reproduced inside `mawsd`): (1) `startSpine` returns with its listeners subscribed to the adapter, which delivers nothing yet; (2) the coalesced parts replay through them; (3) the digest's resync (`resyncDigests`) runs over the journal for the rows the engine host lists live, under E10's own contract carried into the adapter's immediate-run path (`RESYNC_BOUND_MS`, abort when the link leaves `ready`, the guarded dispatch, the abort-or-completion race, `host-supervisor.ts`'s `sayReady` path), the hold still accumulating, so the fold filter's position is the journal's, never a held tail's (R2: a held tail replayed first would advance the high-water mark past pages the resync has not read); a resync that ends at its bound leaves the unpaged rows folding from the tail, as E10's does; (4) the hold drains in order to every listener, launch-aware: a held event of the session's current launch (the status the listing gave) folds into the digest through the fold filter, which deduplicates what the resync paged; a held event of another launch (a relaunch during the load: R3, the filter resets on any different launch and would re-admit the new launch's paged prefix) reaches the raw listeners (attention's core, the owner channel, side threads, the replay seam) and is not folded; and for a session the listing shows `exited` (whose journal the resync does not page), its held batches fold before its exited status is applied, as the engine host delivers them (R3: a crash in a held final batch must yield the digest phase `failed`, not `exited`); (5) live delivery begins. A listener that reads raw batches sees every held batch once. The session source as O3-C left it cannot do step 4 unchanged (R4): `src/services/sessions/index.ts`'s `hostEventSource` owns its fold filter privately and folds every batch it is given, the coalesced and the listed `exited` statuses both land before the drain, and the read model marks a batch after an `exited` status as history, which keeps the phase. M2 therefore changes `sessions/index.ts` (the fold takes a launch context from the adapter: fold, raw-only, or lifecycle-first), `resync.ts` (the listed statuses of exited rows applied after their held batches) and `read-model.ts` (a held batch of the launch's own stream is not history before its status), with the cases M2's proofs name; the executor keeps the embedded root's behaviour unchanged (main delivers no hold). The hold is not bounded by a byte count during a load, which takes seconds (its size is logged at the drain); it is discarded, counted in `daemon.status.spine.heldDropped` and logged, only when the load ends `refused` or `yielded` (no spine of `mawsd`'s will ever fold it: the window of §L), and as a last resort past 256 MiB, which no load reaches and which is a fault to report, not a design loss. What is consequential never depends on the hold alone: broker items (permissions, questions, plans) are the coalesced `session.brokerItems`, and `error`, `interrupted` and `exited` derive from the coalesced `session.status`. After a re-execution the engine host's connect burst arrives while the spine is cold: its first batch is the trigger and the burst is the hold's content, which is how §8's "E10's burst and undelivered tail now cover `mawsd`'s own restarts" holds (D8).

`mawsd`'s `SpineDeps` bindings: `profile` the profile; `machine` its ref; `hosts` an adapter over its `HostSupervisor` whose `dispatch` and `forward` to the engine host pass through `interceptEngine` first (so `host.configure`, `host.stopAll` and `host.exitWhenIdle` from the spine reach the clocks as the app's did), whose `onEvent` validates against this build's registry and delivers the replay above before live events, and whose `setResync` runs the resync at once on a link that already streams, holding live batches meanwhile (D8); `bus` the front's binding (§B); `clientPort` §E's; `interest` the union (`union()`); `browserSocket` the attached app's path from `machine.client`, null with no app; `trash` D10's; `engineEnv` one `captureShellEnvironment()` per load; `env` the daemon's; `packaged` as `daemon.ts` reads it; `appPath` the app root above `out/` (`join(dirname(modules.self), '..', '..')`, the asar in a packaged app); `devRoot` `process.cwd()`; `home` `homedir()`.

**Against the Purpose.** What the plan set out to prove it proved, and two things it assumed it had to change. **Retrospective.** (1) The largest cost was M5b's e2e fix rounds (D49 to D51): ten hosted groups red on the cutover, each a behaviour the embedded shape gave for free (a badge's recount, a status's one connection, a settings write's refusal) that the mirror had to state. Most were found by the hosted set, not by the task review; a cutover of this shape is worth a hosted run of the whole set before its first review, not after. (3) Two checks passed for the wrong reason and were caught only by reading their output: the machines scene's primed host (D54) and the spine case's vacuous park check (D56(3)). A liveness poll whose subject can be null should fail on null. (4) One writer of the spine at every commit held across seven milestones and a two-executor M5; the ledger and the dated Decision Log carried every ruling between executors without a lost decision. Its fix wave ran as four parallel executors in separate worktrees, grouped by shared files, and landed cleanly; the two follow-ups were test mocks that missed a new export, which a whole-suite run in the combined tree before the push would have caught.
