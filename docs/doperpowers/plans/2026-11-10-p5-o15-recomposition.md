## Purpose

P5 moved everything that belongs to a machine out of the app: sessions, terminals, the index of transcripts, attention, files, git, connectors and the library now run in `mawsd`, the machine daemon. `mawsd` is a background process, one per profile, started at login by a LaunchAgent for the owner's main profile and started by the app for every other profile (`src/mawsd/`). The app is a client of it. Three things remain before P5 can close: the first two were known when this document was written, and the third was found on 2026-11-10 by reading the code against M4's table before the final build was fixed (Decision Log 6).

**The app still contains a second way to run the machine.** `src/main/app.ts` can compose the same services inside Electron's main process (the *embedded shape*, P5 §2's second composition root), and it does so in three cases: a saved setting `supervisor.shape: embedded`, an environment variable `MAWS_HOST_MODE=detached` or `utility`, and, without anyone asking, whenever the main profile's LaunchAgent is absent, turned off or fails to start. In that last case MAWS looks normal and every promise of P5 is quietly off: sessions end with the app, and other machines cannot reach this one. After M1 and M2 the app has one arrangement on every profile. On the owner's Macs the difference shows in one place: when `mawsd` cannot start, MAWS opens a small window that says why, shows the daemon's last log lines and offers the repair, and it runs nothing of the machine's until `mawsd` answers. A developer sees it too: a development build on the main profile of a Mac with no installed MAWS shows that window, which names `pnpm dev:scratch`.

**P5's acceptance has never been checked as a whole on one build.** Each child proved its part when it landed, on the build of its day. The evidence inventory counts 67 claims in the 16 items: 31 have something seen on the owner's production machines, only 3 of those in full, and 36 have nothing live (11 a gate or a bench only, 10 staging only, 15 neither). After M3 to M5 every claim has a dated result on one final build, or the owner's ruling on it, written into P5's Outcomes, and the owner's verdict on the week closes P5.

**Some claims could not pass, or could not be seen, on the build as it stood.** The last sentence of P5's acceptance 7, every subscription resumed within 1 s of the network being reachable after a wake, held only by luck: nothing in the app reacted to the network's return, an attempt begun before it could hold a link for about 23 s, and the org feed could stay dead after a wake with nothing to notice it. Main's log had no file and no times, so the claim could not have been read off the machine either. The same read found smaller things: a replay's source that no log names, a tool description that tells the model the app must be open, a peer message drawn with no owner, a connection failure logged with an empty reason, and a refused start of the daemon that the repair window would have described wrongly. M2b fixes them before the final build is fixed. After it, resume after a wake is a property of the design, in the case §F states, that a gate case proves on both carriers, main writes a log file with a time on every line, and M4's rows name methods that the build can answer.

**How to see it working.** After M2, in the suites: an app launched with a daemon that cannot start opens the repair window and nothing else, and its Try Again opens the app once the daemon can start; a profile saved on the embedded shape opens on `mawsd` with every session and setting present. After M3, on each Mac: `ps` shows no engine host or pty host whose parent is MAWS, and Settings › Background supervisor has no "Run sessions under the machine daemon" switch. After M2b, in the measured group `e2e:remote-latency`: with the app's sockets gone silent and a wake of an hour injected, every open subscription of the link is answered within 1 s of the path's return, over the direct carrier and over the relay, with one upgrade and nothing lost or repeated; and `<profile>/logs/main.log` holds main's lines with their times. After M5: P5's Outcomes holds the table of 67 results and the owner's words.

## Acceptance

O15 is done when all of these hold.

1. **One arrangement.** No launch of the app, on any profile and with any environment or saved setting, opens the machine's data, takes `spine.lock` (the lock the one writer of a profile's machine files holds), or starts an engine host or a pty host from the app's main process. The suites show it by a check that the app's bundle holds no module of the services' composition, and by launches that read which process holds the lock.
2. **The repair window.** A launch on the main profile whose daemon cannot start shows one window that names the reason in a sentence, the daemon's last log lines where there are any, and the actions that apply; nothing else of MAWS opens. The retry action opens the app in the same process once the daemon starts. Closing the window quits MAWS. A profile with a private daemon is outside this: it starts as it does today, and a private daemon that fails to start, like any daemon that fails after the launch, shows where it shows today, in the supervisor's status row and Settings › Background supervisor.
3. **Nothing lost.** A profile last run on the embedded shape, with `supervisor.shape: embedded` saved, opens on `mawsd` with its sessions, projects, artifacts, library, drafts, layout and every other setting as they were, and no re-index.
4. **The acceptance as a whole.** Each of the 67 claims of P5's acceptance has, in P5's Outcomes, one of: a pass seen on the final build with its evidence; a citation of earlier evidence with the reason a new check adds nothing; or the owner's ruling, with a tracker row where something is carried.
5. **The record.** P5's Outcomes and retrospective is written; the risk row on the org root's key says what the key is; the items other children handed to O15 by name are each closed or carried; the owner's verdict (acceptance 16) is recorded in their words.
6. **Resume after a wake, and the log that shows it.** In the gate's wake case (§I), with six subscriptions on the link of which two are session streams whose cursors the engine host's ring has evicted, every subscription is answered within 1 s of the path's return: over the direct carrier, and over the relay while the direct path answers TCP and hangs above it. One upgrade is made on the carrier that came up, and no event is lost or repeated across the cut. The org feed comes back with one open, connected and its listings read within 2 s of the hub's path. The 1 s is claimed for the common case §F states, a handshake with no catch-up and at most six subscriptions, and for §F's term `J` only as far as the gate's journals show it. Main's log is a file under the profile with a time on every line, written by the profile lock's holder alone, and it carries the lines M4's 7e reads (§H, §K).

## Constraints that bind every milestone

1. **`mawsd`'s side does not change.** `src/services/` (the library both roots compose), `src/mawsd/` and the engine host keep their behaviour. `detached` stays: it is the transport `mawsd` and the app's relay links use, and the engine host's own mode. Only the app's use of it to reach an engine host directly goes. M2b adds log lines to the engine host's session stream and to the endpoint (§H) and changes nothing else of either.
2. **No bound moves.** OX7's limits (p50 26.45 ms, p95 42.56 ms, P5 Decision Log 33), acceptance 14's bound (99,145 KiB and 1.12 s per 600 s, Decision Log 128) and every other measured limit keep their recorded numbers. A ported test that cannot hold its limit on `mawsd` stops the work with the numbers; it is not loosened.
3. **Rollback stays possible.** Each machine keeps its previous build, cf30dafdd, beside the new one. So no stored file takes a version an older build cannot read: the client settings file stays at version 21 (a build that meets a newer version falls back to defaults and would overwrite the owner's settings), and the index is not touched.
4. **The wire is additive.** OX1: no command, event, capability or schema member that a process of another build sends or reads is removed or narrowed. If a change here has to be named in `src/shared/ipc/wire-manifest.json`, it is an addition and takes a minor.
5. **The older-`mawsd` paths stay.** Where the app answers `artifacts.export` or `delivery.send` itself because the connected `mawsd` does not advertise `machine.render` or `machine.delivery`, and where the artifact scheme serves locally before a welcome, the code serves an app that meets an older daemon, not the embedded shape. It stays, and M5 files its retirement.
6. **No Electron and no typecheck on the MacBook.** The executor runs no Electron, no e2e, no packaged build and no typecheck (`tsc`, `pnpm typecheck`, any `tsc --noEmit`, whatever the heap) on this Mac: every suite runs through `scripts/ci-gate.sh` from the branch's worktree. What may run locally is a focused run alone, one at a time: eslint on one file, vitest's node project on one test file. Lint over the repository, vitest over several files or a directory, and the unit suite are class 1a's on CI. After every commit that touches product or test TypeScript, a static-only or class 1a run comes before any run that holds the CI Mac.
7. **Tests never touch the owner's daemon.** A test or harness launch on the main profile is refused before any work, as today (`testLaunchRefusal`).
8. **The repository is public.** Evidence that shows the owner's sessions (screenshots, transcripts, logs with their content) stays on the architect's disk under `~/.local/share/maws-architect/o15-evidence/`; the record carries times, counts, builds and verdicts. Neither of the owner's email addresses is written anywhere.

**Decisions.**

- *The launch has two outcomes and no third* (§A's table). The rows that returned `detached` with a reason now return that reason as a failure. ### M2b — Resume after a wake by design, main's log on disk, and what the readiness read found

**At its end.** A wake is noticed with the sleep's length. After a sleep of 10 s or more the app drops every socket it held before the sleep, and for the next 60 s it learns that a path is reachable from bare TCP connects, and each carrier makes its own attempt when its path answers. In the common case §F states, each open subscription of a link is then answered within 1 s of its carrier's path being reachable, on a network inside §F's round-trip times and with §F's term `J` as far as the gate shows it. The org feed and each project socket take the same drop, reopen with one open in flight, and are checked for life by a protocol ping at all times. Main writes every log line to `<profile>/logs/main.log` with its time, and the lines M4's 7e reads exist there and in `mawsd.log`. A peer message in the session pane shows its sender's owners under P5 Decision Log 111's rule. `host.log` says when a launch's ring first evicted and when a read was served from the journal. `artifact_export`'s description no longer says it needs the app. A failed hub connection of `mawsd` names its error code and addresses.

**Decisions.**

- *A wake is noticed with its length* (§D). One module, `src/main/net/wake.ts`, listens to `powerMonitor`'s `suspend`, `resume` and `unlock-screen`, and nothing else in main does: the remote source, the org feed and the project sockets take its notices. A `resume` reports the time since the `suspend`; for a wake that sent no `resume`, a 1 s timer reports a tick that came 10 s or more late as a wake of that length. Electron's documentation says of `resume` only "Emitted when system is resuming" and names no kind of wake, so nothing here depends on which wakes send it. A dark wake, in which macOS runs for a short time with the display off, is a wake like any other: the notice cannot tell them apart, the sockets are dropped and the paths probed, and if the network is up the links come back until the Mac sleeps again; its cost is one reconnect. The timer cannot tell a sleep from a main process that did not run for 10 s. That is reported as a wake too, rightly, since the peers saw the same silence, and at most once a minute, so a main process that keeps stalling does not reconnect in a loop. `unlock-screen`, a window's focus and the address-change poll keep the probes they make today. The timer alone was not taken: it would add up to a second to every wake that does send `resume`.
- *A sleep of 10 s or more drops every socket from before it, at once.* Each link's channel, its relay socket and any attempt in flight; the org feed's socket; each project socket. None is pinged first. A connection that is only read from, or whose far end dropped it during the sleep, reports nothing until a write fails; a ping's 2 s deadline is time the bound does not have; and resuming from a cursor is the designed path, so dropping a channel that happened to be alive costs one reconnect and the reopening of the byte streams on it (a remote terminal, a forwarded port), which end with their channel at any loss. The threshold is the endpoint's own limit. It closes a channel at the third ping period in a row in which it heard nothing, 15 to 28 s after the last message it heard, and that is as little as 10 s after the sleep begins when the sleep starts with one of its pings unanswered (`PING_MS` and `#arm` in `src/endpoint/channels.ts`: two more periods of at least 5 s). A second channel from the same device does not replace the first there: channels are keyed by their carrier, so the endpoint keeps both until the old one's own silence closes it. The hub closes no silent app socket by a timer (`apps/desk-hub/src/objects/relay.ts`: "The ends drive the heartbeats; this object keeps no timer"), so it gives no later threshold, and whether one of its sockets outlived the sleep is the network's to know, not the app's. A test holds `WAKE_DROP_MS` to twice the endpoint's shortest period. The alternative, ping first and keep what answers, is today's probe: it is right for a focus, an unlock and a sleep under 10 s, and stays there.
- *For 60 s after a wake or an address change, a probe that costs nothing finds the path, and each carrier tries on its own* (§E, §F). In this window the direct carrier and the relay are two lanes of a link, each with its own targets, its own probing and its own attempt. A lane's targets, each tailnet address of the cached endpoint record for one and the hub for the other, are probed with bare TCP connects, one every 100 ms. The first that connects is closed and is the signal that the path is reachable, and the lane makes its attempt at once, whatever the other lane is doing. The first lane to complete a handshake is adopted. One prober serves the hub for every link's relay lane, the org feed and the project sockets. When the window closes, today's backoff takes over. A probe and not the open itself, for two costs. The direct listener takes a token from its per-source bucket (a burst of 3, then 5 a minute; `HANDSHAKE_LIMITS`, which the app's `DIRECT_BUCKET` mirrors) at every upgrade request, before any key work (`#upgrade` in `src/endpoint/direct-listener.ts`), so repeated opens would empty it in under a second and the open meant for the handshake would be answered 503. And every open that reaches the hub costs it an Access check and a registry read, and on the feed a whole snapshot. A TCP connect that closes costs neither, because it never becomes an upgrade. The probe's socket is closed, not handed to the WebSocket client: the client takes a ready socket only through its HTTP request's options, the hub's would need TLS started on it, and the dial inside the window would then differ from the dial outside it; the round trip this costs is in §F's budget. Two lanes that race, and not one attempt at a time, because a path whose TCP connects can still hang above TCP, at the hub's TLS or upgrade or at the listener's: with one attempt at a time the hung one holds the working carrier behind it for as long as its deadlines last. The endpoint keeps a device's channels by carrier, so two handshakes in flight from one device are legal, and the cost is at most one token and one relay socket a try. For the same reason a lane's attempt has the window's deadlines, 2 s for the relay socket's open and for each handshake message where today's are 10 s, so that a hung attempt frees its lane; a lane whose attempt fails backs off 250 ms, growing to 2 s, and goes on probing meanwhile; and a lane makes at most six attempts in a window, the direct lane dialling only while the bucket would keep a token after it. When direct wins, the relay's attempt is aborted. When the relay wins, the direct lane goes on, and its channel, when it completes, takes the relay's place by today's switch: a swap in the first seconds after a wake ends fewer byte streams than the 60 s recheck would end a minute into use, and aborting a direct attempt the bucket was already charged for, only to dial it again, would charge it twice. An attempt in flight when the window opens is aborted: it began before the path returned and would hold the link to its deadlines, about 23 s today (the record fetch, the direct race, the relay open, the handshake). The lanes use the cached endpoint record at once while the hub's is read in the background, and never a relay socket from before the window; and in the window or out of it the cached relay socket is closed when its channel is lost for `timeout`, since reusing a dead one cost 10 s a handshake. *The floor* keeps the window from ever leaving a link worse off than today's code. A probe is given 3 s, so that a slow or lossy path still answers one; the thirty in flight for a target are thirty sockets that carry nothing. And 10 s into a window in which the link still has no channel, or sooner once no lane is left running, today's attempt runs beside the lanes on today's backoff, whole and under today's deadlines: the record read from the hub, the race over the direct addresses, the relay. It is adopted if it completes first, and nothing takes it over. So a link is without today's attempt for 10 s at most, and only while its lanes are still trying. Three alternatives lost. One attempt at a time with the other target remembered was this document's first text and the review's first finding (Decision Log 8). Shorter timeouts on sequential attempts cannot give the bound: an attempt begun just before the path returns holds the link until its deadline. Staggered WebSocket opens, with the handshake on the first to connect, was the architect's first decision and was withdrawn at the desk track's read of the listener and the hub, for the two costs above (Decision Log 6).
- *The 1 s is shown by arithmetic, for the common case, with one term open* (§F, "The budget"). The common case is a handshake with no catch-up, one dial. When the two ends hold different org heads, as after a machine key or the org state changed during the sleep, the handshake takes one more dial for each page of catch-up; that recovery stays exactly as it is, and such a wake is recorded with its count of dials and is outside the bound. The bound is claimed for up to six subscriptions on a link, two open sessions with their feeds, and a sitting with more is recorded with its count. In that case the direct carrier fits while six tailnet round trips and `J` come to at most 800 ms, and the relay while seven round trips to the hub, three from the hub to the machine, and `J` come to at most 500 ms: the hub's own work on an upgrade was measured on staging at 220 to 240 ms and is allowed 300 ms, the largest single share of the relay's second (Decision Log 6). `J` is the endpoint's work between a `sub` and its `subbed`. For a cursor the engine host's ring still holds it is a few reads from memory. For a cursor the ring has evicted, which is the case after an hour asleep with the session working, it is a read of the launch's journal from its first row, and nothing in the design bounds that yet: §F names the term, lists the candidate designs without choosing, and waits for a measurement that is running (Decision Log 8). Past these limits the bound does not hold by design and M4 records the round trips beside a miss. Outside the window the bound is not promised: a path that returns more than 60 s after the wake is found by today's backoff, or by the next address change, which opens a window of its own within the poll's 2 s.
- *The org feed and the project socket* (§G) take the wake drop and reopen with one open in flight, the first at the hub prober's first answer. Never overlapping opens: each feed open makes the hub send the grid, the state version and every listings page, and each project open its welcome, machines, presence, pending, ops and deliveries, so an open closed unused costs a whole snapshot. Their liveness check, on both and at all times, is a WebSocket protocol ping with a deadline for its pong. Never an application message: the Registry drops one, so the deadline would always fire, and the project socket refuses it and writes an audit row for each. The hub's runtime answers a protocol ping itself without waking the object (Cloudflare's Durable Objects documentation, "Use WebSockets", "Automatic ping/pong handling"; measured on staging, Decision Log 6). When the app drops one of these sockets it terminates it and counts it ended in the same turn. It never waits for the hub's close frame: neither object completes a close handshake, and a client `close(1000)` ends as 1006 about 10 s later. Today both already open the next socket without waiting, since each forgets the old socket before it closes it; what changes is that the old one is terminated, not left half closed for those 10 s. The 1 s bound is acceptance 7's for subscriptions over links and is not claimed for these two sockets. §G states their target, 2 s to the feed's last listings page. The gate case holds the feed to it on loopback: without a bound, the defect this milestone removes, a feed that stays dead after a wake, could return with the gate green. 7e records it live. Both sockets have the links' floor: one that has waited 10 s on the hub's prober opens anyway.
- *What "resumes from its cursor" means.* A session's stream resubscribes with the cursor of the last event it delivered, and the endpoint answers `subbed` with `replay` when it can go on from there. A feed (attention, artifacts, a checkout, the browser inventory, a project's channel feed) resubscribes with no cursor and is answered with a snapshot: that is RL3's design for a feed, not a loss (`SubscribeRequest` in `src/main/machines/remote/subs.ts`). Acceptance 7 cites RL3, so both are resumed when the endpoint's `subbed` arrives, and that arrival is what the 1 s is measured to. A session stream answered `snapshot` or `missing`, because the launch changed during the sleep or the journal no longer holds the range, is resumed as RL3 defines it, and the record says which answer each got. The org feed has no cursor and reads its listings whole; the project socket resumes after its offset. M4's 7e row is worded this way.
- *Main's log is a file, and a private one* (§H): `<profile>/logs/main.log`, every line with its time, 8 MiB and one previous generation, written beside stdout and stderr, which keep every line they have today. Under the profile, not Electron's logs directory, which the profiles of one build share: a scratch launch must not write into the owner's log, and the gate case reads its own launch's file. Only the instance that holds the profile lock writes it. What a launch says before it holds the lock is kept in memory and written then with its own times, so the file has the launch's first lines and a second launch can never rotate the first's file away. Opening it can never stop a launch: a directory that cannot be made or tightened turns the file log off with one line on stderr. The file is a diagnostic on the owner's disk and holds whatever main's console holds, file paths and error texts included. What M2b promises to be free of session content is the lines §H lists, and only those leave a machine as evidence, picked by §K's patterns. M2b changes where lines go and adds §H's lines; it changes no existing line and adds one at a second launch's hand-off. This closes tracker row #586 (the main process's log). The alternative, timestamps on stdout alone, leaves a Dock-launched app with no log, which is the row.

## Interfaces and Dependencies

### §A. The launch outcome (`src/main/hosts/daemon.ts`)

    export type LaunchShape = { daemon: 'unit' | 'private' };
    export type UnavailableKind = 'no-unit' | 'not-loaded' | 'install-refused' | 'not-started';
    export interface Unavailable { kind: UnavailableKind; why: string }   // why: the system's or mawsd's own words, one line
    export type LaunchOutcome = { ok: true; launch: LaunchShape } | { ok: false; unavailable: Unavailable };

`resolveLaunch` answers a `LaunchOutcome`, and its input no longer carries the setting.

| Profile | Build | The main profile's unit | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| not the main one | any | not read | `private` |
| main | packaged | any | the unit is installed or repaired to run this bundle, asked to start, and must welcome a hello within `UNIT_READY_MS`: then `unit`. Anything else is one of the four cases below |
| main | unpackaged | installed and loaded | asked to start, and must welcome: then `unit`. No welcome: `not-started` |
| main | unpackaged | installed, not loaded | `not-loaded` |
| main | unpackaged | not installed, or no unit manager | `no-unit` |

A test or harness launch on the main profile exits before the table, as today. The first row is today's and starts nothing (M2, "What the window does not cover").

*A start that does not end in a welcome, on a packaged build* (Decision Log 6). The rule is written so that it holds whichever step macOS refuses, and so does not depend on what one macOS version does to a loaded agent that is turned off under Login Items.

| Case | What happened | Outcome | `why` |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) | the install or repair was refused: `launchctl bootstrap` answered non-zero | `install-refused` | launchctl's words |
| (ii) | the start was refused: `launchctl kickstart` answered non-zero | `install-refused` | launchctl's words |
| (iii) | the start reported success, no welcome came within `UNIT_READY_MS`, `mawsd` wrote nothing since this attempt began, and no live process holds `mawsd.lock` | `install-refused` | `macOS loaded mawsd but did not run it` |
| (iv) | no welcome, and `mawsd` wrote since this attempt began or a live process holds its lock | `not-started` | the welcome wait's last answer, as today |

Whether `mawsd` wrote is read with no clock. Before the start is asked, main reads the size and the inode of `<profile>/mawsd/mawsd.log` and of `<profile>/mawsd/mawsd.out`, a missing file counting as a value of its own; after the wait it reads them again, and any difference in either is a write. A `mawsd` that starts writes its `starting` line to `mawsd.log` before it takes its lock (`src/mawsd/daemon.ts`), and what it prints before that goes to `mawsd.out`, the unit's standard output and error. The lock is read as main reads it today (`readLock` and `isAlive` of `src/engine-host/supervisor-files.ts`); it tells a `mawsd` that was already running and is silent, which is (iv), from a job macOS never ran. Nothing of `launchctl print`'s text is parsed and no state line is added to (iii)'s `why`: the unit's code reads only that command's exit status (`loaded` in `src/mawsd/units/launchd.ts`). MAWS's unit plist carries `AssociatedBundleIdentifiers` [`dev.maws.app`] (`launchdPlist`, same file), which is why Login Items shows one MAWS row with one switch for the app and its daemon.

| `not-loaded` | Open Login Items…; Try Again; Quit MAWS |
| `install-refused` | Open Login Items…; Reinstall and Start; Quit MAWS |
| `not-started` | Reinstall and Start on a packaged build, Try Again on a development build; Show Log in Finder; Quit MAWS |

Reinstall and Start and Try Again are the same action, `retry`: on a packaged build §A's table installs or repairs the unit before it starts it, and the label says so.

### §C. The e2e fault, the five cases and acceptance 3's scene

`MAWS_E2E_UNIT_FAULT=<absolute path>` is honoured only when `MAWS_E2E=1` and the app is unpackaged, on a profile that is not the main one. While the file exists and its trimmed content is one of the four kinds, the launch's outcome is that failure with `why` `e2e fault`; otherwise the row's own outcome, `private`. A packaged app ignores it. The fault replaces the table's answer, so it proves the window and what main does and does not compose around it. The table's own rows (the unit installed, refused, started, welcomed or silent) are pinned by unit tests over a faked unit runner, as `src/main/hosts/daemon.test.ts` does today, and are seen for real once, in M4's live check of the window. Each of §A's cases (i) to (iv) has its own test there: a `bootstrap` that answers non-zero, a `kickstart` that answers non-zero, a silent start with the two files unchanged and no live lock holder, and a silent start with a file changed or a live holder.

1. *The daemon cannot start.* With the fault `not-started`, the first window is the repair page with that headline; no app window exists; `spine.lock` is held by no process; the profile's `mawsd/` directory holds no lock this launch made.
2. *It can start again.* The fault file removed and the retry action pressed: the repair window closes, the app's window opens, and its session list loads.
3. *Login Items.* With `not-loaded`, Open Login Items… asks for the settings URL (read through the suite's record of external opens where one exists, else pinned in the unit test alone), and the window's next focus starts exactly one attempt.
4. *Closing quits.* The repair window closed: the process exits with code 0.
5. *A saved `embedded`* (`e2e/settings.spec.ts`). A scratch profile whose client `settings.json` holds `supervisor.shape: embedded` beside three other settings off their defaults: the launch runs on `mawsd`, whose pid holds `spine.lock`; the three settings read as written; after the first settings write the file's `shape` reads `daemon` and its version is still 21.

*Acceptance 3's data.* A hand-built profile cannot prove it: `test/support/o2-profile.ts` seeds the index alone, and the library's first reconcile with the engine host deletes rows the host does not hold. The proof is the cross-build scene whose peer runs embedded (M1 keeps it): the peer, a real build with the embedded shape, makes a session, an artifact, a draft and a layout of more than one tab on a profile and quits; the tested build opens that profile on `mawsd`. The scene asserts, after the library's first sync and again after a relaunch of the tested build: the session listed, the artifact in the library and openable, the draft, the layout's tabs, and no index rebuild in `mawsd.log`. It needs a peer that still has the embedded shape and skips, saying so, against a later one; M5 files its retirement.

### §D. The wake notice (`src/main/net/wake.ts`)

    export const WAKE_TICK_MS = 1_000;
    export const WAKE_DROP_MS = 10_000;        // twice PING_MS[0] of src/endpoint/channels.ts; a test holds the two
    export const WAKE_CLOCK_GAP_MS = 60_000;   // the least time between two notices the timer raises

    export interface WakeNotice { sleptMs: number; source: 'resume' | 'clock' }
    export interface WakeSource {
      onWake(listener: (notice: WakeNotice) => void): () => void;
      onUnlock(listener: () => void): () => void;
      close(): void;
    }
    type PowerEvent = 'suspend' | 'resume' | 'unlock-screen';
    export function createWakeSource(deps: {
      power: { on(event: PowerEvent, listener: () => void): unknown; off(event: PowerEvent, listener: () => void): unknown };   // Electron's powerMonitor
      now?(): number;
      log(line: string): void;
    }): WakeSource & { inject(sleptMs: number): void };

| Event | What the source does |
|---|---|
| `suspend` | remembers its time |
| `resume` | `sleptMs` is the time since the remembered `suspend`, or since the last tick when none was heard; forgets the `suspend`; takes now as the last tick; tells `{ sleptMs, source: 'resume' }` |
| a tick, every `WAKE_TICK_MS`, on a timer that does not hold the process | `late` is now, less the last tick, less `WAKE_TICK_MS`; takes now as the last tick; if `late` is at least `WAKE_DROP_MS` and the timer raised no notice within `WAKE_CLOCK_GAP_MS`, forgets the `suspend` and tells `{ sleptMs: late, source: 'clock' }` |
| `unlock-screen` | tells the unlock listeners |
| `inject(sleptMs)` | takes now as the last tick and tells `{ sleptMs, source: 'clock' }`: §I's seam calls it |

Every notice writes `[wake] slept <ms> ms (<source>)`. A consumer drops its sockets when `sleptMs` is at least `WAKE_DROP_MS` and makes today's probe when it is less. The timer reads the wall clock's lateness and no second clock, because whether Node's monotonic clock advances through a macOS sleep is not something this repository shows, and a timer that was due during the sleep fires when the process runs again either way. A main process that a loaded runner stalls for 10 s raises a `clock` notice in the suites too: the reconnect it causes is the designed one, and its `[wake]` line says what happened when a case is read afterwards.

Unit (`wake.test.ts`, a fake power source and a fake clock): a `resume` after a `suspend` tells the time between them; a `resume` with no `suspend` tells the time since the last tick; a tick 10 s late tells one `clock` notice, and a `resume` that follows it within the second tells a short sleep; a tick late by less tells nothing; two late ticks within a minute tell one notice; `unlock-screen` is passed on; `close()` removes the three listeners and the timer.

### §E. The burst window and the probes (`src/main/net/reach.ts`)

    export const REACH_WINDOW_MS = 60_000;
    export const PROBE_STAGGER_MS = 100;
    export const PROBE_CONNECT_MS = 3_000;
    export const WINDOW_FLOOR_MS = 10_000;   // §F row 5, §G rule 3

    export interface Reach {
      /** Opens the window for REACH_WINDOW_MS from now; called while it is open, the count starts again. */
      openWindow(reason: 'wake' | 'network'): void;
      windowOpen(): boolean;
      /**
       * true at the first TCP connect to `target`'s host and port that succeeds while the window is open; false at once
       * when the window is not open, false when it closes first or `signal` aborts.
       */
      reachable(target: string, signal?: AbortSignal): Promise<boolean>;
      close(): void;
    }
    export function createReach(deps: {
      connect?(host: string, port: number, signal: AbortSignal): Promise<void>;   // node:net's connect by default: resolves at 'connect', rejects at 'error'; an abort destroys the socket
      now?(): number;
      log(line: string): void;
    }): Reach;
    export const NO_REACH: Reach;   // its window never opens

- A target is a URL (`ws:`, `wss:`, `http:`, `https:`). Its host and port, 80 or 443 where it names none, are its key: the hub's URL as a link, the feed and a project socket give it is one key and one prober.
- While the window is open and someone waits on a key, a probe starts at once and then every `PROBE_STAGGER_MS`. A probe is one TCP connect given `PROBE_CONNECT_MS`, so at most `PROBE_CONNECT_MS / PROBE_STAGGER_MS`, thirty, are in flight for a key. The first to connect is destroyed at once, the key's other probes are destroyed, every waiter of the key is answered `true`, and the key is probed no more until someone waits on it again.
- When a key's last waiter leaves (its signal aborted) the key's probes are destroyed. When the window closes every waiter is answered `false`.
- Lines: `[reach] window open for 60 s (<reason>)`; for a key, once per round of waiting, `[reach] <host>:<port>: reachable after <n> probes, <ms> ms` or `[reach] <host>:<port>: window closed after <n> probes, none connected (<the last error's words, §H>)`. No line per probe.

The interval is 100 ms because a probe that finds nothing costs one unanswered SYN and a probe that connects ends the probing: nothing argues for the slower end of the range, and 100 ms is a tenth of the bound at worst. A probe is given 3 s, three times the app's standing judgment of a direct open (`DIRECT_DIAL_MS`), so that a slow or lossy path still answers one; the thirty in flight that this allows are thirty sockets that carry nothing. A path whose TCP connect takes over 3 s answers no probe: the floor gives it today's attempt 10 s into the window (§F, row 5), and the window's close leaves today's backoff.

Unit (`reach.test.ts`, a fake `connect` and a fake clock): with no window `reachable` answers `false` at once and connects nothing; with one, a probe at once and one each 100 ms while someone waits, never a thirty-first in flight, a probe that hangs destroyed at 3 s; the first connect answers every waiter of its key, destroys the rest and stops the key's probing; two URLs of one host and port share a prober, and a URL with no port takes its scheme's; the last waiter's abort stops the probing; the window's close answers `false`; `openWindow` while open starts the 60 s again.

### §F. The link in the window (`src/main/machines/remote/`)

    // RemoteLinkDeps gains
    reach?: Reach;                               // NO_REACH by default: no window opens and the link is today's
    directTarget?(url: string): string | null;   // where an advertised direct address is probed: itself by default; null: never probed
    // dialDirect and openRelay each gain a last parameter, signal?: AbortSignal: aborted, the socket is terminated and the open rejects
    // handshake gains a last parameter, options?: { timeoutMs?: number }: connectChannel's own, each handshake message's deadline
    // RemoteTiming gains
    WINDOW_OPEN_MS: number;                      // 2_000: a relay socket's open inside the window (10 s outside it)
    WINDOW_HANDSHAKE_MS: number;                 // 2_000: each handshake message inside the window (10 s outside it)
    LANE_BACKOFF_MS: readonly number[];          // [250, 500, 1_000, 2_000], the last repeated, jittered by BACKOFF_JITTER
    LANE_ATTEMPTS: number;                       // 6: a lane's attempts in one window
    // RemoteLink gains
    wake(sleptMs: number): void;
    // EndpointRecords gains
    cached(ref: MachineRef, signedIn: SignedIn): Promise<Signed<EndpointRecord> | null>;   // the held record if it verifies now (memory, then the file); no fetch

`src/main/app.ts` replaces `directTarget` in the two places it replaces `dialDirect`: the development session's `directMap` maps it to the same address, and the relay-only bench makes it `null`.

*Wake and probe.* `wake(sleptMs)` opens the window. Under `WAKE_DROP_MS` it then makes today's `probe('wake')`. At or over it: every attempt in flight is aborted; the relay socket is closed; a current channel is closed with the reason `wake`, which is a loss like any other (`lost`) and seeks; with no channel the failure count and the loss time start again and the link seeks. It writes one line, `wake after <ms> ms: dropped <the <carrier> channel | no channel>[, the relay socket][, <n> attempt(s) in flight]`. A link that is `refused`, `closed` or held by the bench does none of it. `probe('network')` opens the window too and, with no channel, aborts every attempt in flight before it seeks. Every probe with no channel seeks where today it starts an attempt; with a channel it is today's ping and, on the relay, today's recheck. Each writes `probe <reason>: <ping sent | seeking | refused | closed>`. The network watch in `source.ts` goes on calling `probe('network')` on an address change, and `src/main/machines/index.ts` calls every link's `wake` from the wake source and `probeAll('unlock')` from its unlock, and no longer imports `powerMonitor`.

*To seek* is to bring a channel up by the rule of the moment. With no window open it is today's `startAttempt`, and everything below is off. With one open, the link's two lanes run. It also starts today's `records.resolve` and does not wait for it; a record that comes back with other addresses gives the direct lane its new targets.

*The lanes.* Inside a window a link has two lanes, direct and relay. Each has its own targets, its own probing and its own attempt, and neither waits for the other. The direct lane's targets are `directTarget` of every tailnet address of `records.cached(...)`; the relay lane's one target is the hub's URL, when the session has a hub. A lane with no target does nothing. A lane's attempt is one `connectChannel` (below) on the target that answered: on the direct lane its dial is one upgrade to that address, with no race and no record read; on the relay lane it runs over a relay socket the lane opens in this window, never one from before it; the lane keeps that socket for its next attempts while it stays open, and closes it when an attempt on it ends at a deadline. Each lane attempt runs under the window's deadlines: `DIRECT_DIAL_MS` for a direct dial as always, `WINDOW_OPEN_MS` for the relay socket's open, `WINDOW_HANDSHAKE_MS` for each handshake message. They are 2 s where today's are 10 s: more than four times the budget's largest share of either step (the relay's open, 3 h + H, is 450 ms at the budget's limit), so an attempt on a path a few times slower than the budget's still completes, and one that hangs above TCP frees its lane in seconds.

| # | Event | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a lane's target answers `true`, and the lane has no attempt running and is in no backoff | the lane starts its attempt at once, whatever the other lane is doing. The direct lane does so only while the bucket holds two tokens; with fewer it keeps the answer and attempts when the second is back |
| 2 | a lane's target answers `true` during the lane's backoff | the answer is kept, and the lane attempts when the backoff ends |
| 3 | a lane's attempt ends failed | the lane backs off by `LANE_BACKOFF_MS` at its count of failures in this window and waits on its targets again at once, so a path that returns during the backoff has answered by its end. After `LANE_ATTEMPTS` attempts in one window the lane stops for that window |
| 4 | an attempt completes its handshake | with no channel current it is adopted, after today's checks (the bench's hold, the trust generation). Adopted over direct: every other attempt is aborted and every wait cancelled. Adopted over the relay: today's attempt, if one runs, is aborted; the direct lane goes on, and when its attempt completes it takes the relay channel's place by today's switch (`recheckDirect`'s swap). An attempt that completes while a direct channel is current is closed |
| 5 | the floor: `WINDOW_FLOOR_MS` after the link first sought in this window, or sooner once no lane is left running (each has stopped, or the link has no target), and still no channel | today's attempt starts beside the lanes and repeats on today's backoff until the window closes or a channel is adopted. It is `connect` as it is today, whole: the record read from the hub, `raceDirect` over the direct addresses, then the cached or a new relay socket, under today's deadlines and today's bucket rule. If it completes first it is adopted as row 4 |
| 6 | an attempt is aborted (a wake drop, `probe('network')`, another attempt adopted, the link refused or closed) | its signal fires: an open in flight is terminated, a carrier it holds is closed, and its result, whenever it comes, is closed and never adopted |
| 7 | the window closes | the lanes start nothing more. An attempt in flight runs to its end and is adopted or closed by row 4. With no channel and none of today's attempts running, today's `scheduleRetry` |

Each event is handled in the turn it arrives, and a lane is marked as attempting before anything is awaited. At most one attempt runs for each lane and at most one of today's, so three at most for a link, and only inside a window past its floor; outside a window there is one, as today. The floor's timer is armed at the link's first seek with no channel in a window; a later opening of the same window does not move it; the window's close and an adopted channel clear it. A failed attempt writes `attempt failed (<direct | relay | today's>): <reason>; next in <ms> ms`, once per lane and reason until the next `up`, as the handshake's verdict line is bounded today; an open that failed gives §H's error words as its reason.

*An attempt, and catch-up.* An attempt is one `connectChannel` (`apps/org-protocol/src/channel.ts`), unchanged. It is one dial when the two ends hold the same org head. When they do not, as after a machine key or an org state changed during the sleep, the handshake is refused `behind`, each side takes the other's page, and `connectChannel` dials again, up to `MAX_CATCH_UP_CONNECTIONS` (16) connections; that recovery is kept exactly as it is. So "one upgrade and one handshake" is the common case and not a property. On the direct lane every dial, a catch-up's redials included, is one upgrade and one token, and is made only while the bucket holds two; one that the bucket does not allow fails the attempt, as an empty bucket fails a redial today. Each redial writes `attempt (<direct | relay | today's>): catch-up, dial <n>`, so a wake that needed catch-up shows its count. On the relay lane each redial is one more channel on the lane's socket. The abort reaches every dial without a change to the protocol package: the attempt's signal goes to `dialDirect` and `openRelay`; the `dial` function the attempt hands `connectChannel` throws when the signal has fired, at the first dial and at each redial, and closes the carrier it returned when the signal fires later; a carrier's close ends the handshake's read (`Carrier.close` in `carriers.ts` tells its end listeners), so `connectChannel` rejects; and a channel that still completes is closed by row 6.

*The bucket.* The listener's per-source bucket is a burst of 3 with one token back every 12 s, and the app's bucket mirrors it. A lane dial needs two tokens in the bucket, so the lanes spend two at once and then one each 12 s, six in a 60 s window at most, and never the last one: that one is today's attempt's, at the floor or after the window. A dial that never opens its socket takes no token, as today, and is bounded by `LANE_ATTEMPTS` alone.

*The floor* is what keeps the window from ever leaving a link worse off than today's code. Before it, the link is without today's attempt only while the lanes are still trying, for 10 s at most. After it, today's attempt runs as it would have, with today's deadlines, beside the lanes; nothing takes it over. So a path that answers no probe (a TCP connect over 3 s), or one on which a lane's 2 s deadlines are too short, is served by today's code from 10 s on, and `raceDirect` is then in use inside the window as it is outside it.

*The relay socket.* `lost` closes the cached relay socket when the reason is `timeout` or `wake`, in the window or out of it. The relay socket of the attempt that is adopted becomes the link's cached one; any other is closed.

*The budget*, for the common case: no catch-up, and at most six subscriptions on the link. Six is two open sessions of one machine, each with its stream and its checkout feed (`src/main/lane-b/remote-checkout.ts`: one beside each stream main holds), and the machine's attention and artifacts feeds (one each for a link); the endpoint allows 64 (`MAX_SUBS`), and a sitting with more than six open is recorded with its count and is outside what the bound is claimed for. All `sub`s go at once when the channel is adopted (`subs.onCurrent`, after the pending acts' own resend), so the last step is one round trip for all of them and then `J`.

| Step | Direct | Relay |
|---|---|---|
| the wait for the next probe to start | ≤ 100 ms | ≤ 100 ms |
| the probe's TCP connect | 1 d | 1 h |
| the open: TCP, on the hub TLS, then the upgrade | 2 d | 3 h + H |
| the RL2 handshake: `h1` for `h2`, then `h3` for the endpoint's first sealed message | 2 d | 2 (h + m) |
| every `sub` for its `subbed`: the round trip | 1 d | 1 (h + m) |
| the two ends' handshake work | P | P |
| the endpoint's work between a `sub` and its `subbed` | J | J |
| in all | 100 ms + 6 d + P + J | 100 ms + 7 h + 3 m + H + P + J |

`d` is the round trip between this Mac and the machine over the tailnet, `h` between this Mac and the hub's edge, `m` between the hub and the machine on the machine's own hub socket. `H` is the hub's own work on an upgrade, its Access check and its registry read: measured on staging at about 220 to 240 ms (Decision Log 6) and allowed 300 ms. `P` is the handshake's signatures and key agreement on the two ends: allowed 100 ms. `J` is the endpoint's work between the arrival of a `sub` and its `subbed`, for the slowest of the link's subscriptions: its authorization, and for a session's stream the reads `SessionStreams.#open` awaits before it answers (`src/shared/host/stream-catchup.ts`): the status, the `session.events` read that checks the cursor, then the queue, the pending items and the catalog. With `P` at 100 ms the direct carrier fits in 1 s while `6 d + J` is at most 800 ms (`d` at most 133 ms when `J` is nothing), and the relay while `7 h + 3 m + J` is at most 500 ms (50 ms each when `h` and `m` are equal and `J` is nothing). The hub's own work is the largest single share of the relay's second. M4's 7e records `d`, `h` and `m` as §K measures them.

**J: to be closed by the measurement (Decision Log 8).** For a cursor the engine host's ring still holds, the read that checks it is from memory and `J` is small. The ring holds a launch's last 5,000 events (`RING_SIZE`, `src/engine-host/session-stream.ts`). For a cursor the ring has evicted, `SessionStream.fromJournal` reads the launch's journal from its first row through a fresh normaliser, since a read that does not continue the page before it starts over; `J` then grows with the journal's length and no step of this budget bounds it. An hour asleep with the session working is that case. The loopback resume the gate has measured so far (about 70 ms on the relay for everything, P5 Decision Log 208) was of a cursor the ring held, and says nothing of a journal read. Until the architect's bench of `snapshot` and of the whole opening against journals of 10,000 to 500,000 events is in, this document claims the 1 s only as far as §I's case shows it: two streams whose cursors are evicted, on journals of about 6,500 and 13,000 events. The candidates for bounding `J` by design, none chosen here: an in-memory checkpoint of the journal reader's position and the normaliser's state, kept as events are appended, so that a seek reads at most one checkpoint interval; an index of row offsets; or a stated journal size up to which the bound holds. The engine host's change, if there is one, is designed when the numbers and the architect's choice are here, and not before.

Unit (`link.test.ts`, with a fake reach whose answers the test gives, fake carriers and the fake clock it has): each row of the table; a wake of 10 s with a channel closes it for `wake`, closes the relay socket, aborts a hanging attempt and dials nothing until a target answers; a wake of 9 s sends today's ping and drops nothing; a direct answer makes exactly one dial, to that address, and takes one token; a hub answer opens a new relay socket though one was cached; both targets answer and the relay's open stalls: the direct lane adopts within its own steps and the relay's open is terminated; both answer and the direct handshake stalls: the relay lane adopts, the direct attempt ends at its 2 s deadline, and its later success is the switch; connectivity returns during a lane's backoff: the lane attempts at the backoff's end with no further probe; both lanes complete, relay first: the relay is adopted and then replaced by the direct channel, and one channel and no socket is left over; both complete, direct first: the relay's is closed; an aborted attempt that completes late adopts nothing and leaves no socket; a seventh attempt of a lane in one window is never made; a direct answer with one token in the bucket waits for the second; a catch-up inside the window makes its redials under the bucket's rule, and aborted between two pages it dials no more and leaves no carrier open; 10 s into a window with no channel today's attempt starts beside the lanes, with `raceDirect` and today's deadlines, and repeats on today's backoff; both lanes stopped before 10 s starts it then; a second opening of the window does not move the floor; the window's close with nothing reachable leaves today's retry; a loss for `timeout` closes the relay socket with no window open; the cached record is used with no fetch, and a newer one from the background read adds its addresses; with `NO_REACH` every existing case passes unchanged.

### §G. The org feed and the project socket (`src/main/org/registry-feed.ts`, `src/main/org/channel/`)

    // WebSocketLike gains (the `ws` client's own: its 'pong' event, ping() and terminate())
    on(event: 'pong', listener: () => void): void;
    ping(): void;
    terminate(): void;
    // OrgTiming gains
    HUB_PING_MS: number;          // 15_000
    HUB_PONG_MS: number;          // 5_000
    HUB_WINDOW_OPEN_MS: number;   // 2_000: an open made while the window is open (wsSocket's 10 s otherwise)
    // createRegistryFeed's, openProjectSocket's and createChannels' deps gain (createOrgSession passes them to its channels)
    wake?: WakeSource;     // none: no wake is heard
    reach?: Reach;         // NO_REACH by default

Both sockets follow the same five rules. "Dropped" means: terminated, forgotten and counted as ended in the same turn (the feed tells its `onClose` listeners; the project socket tells `onClosed(1006)`, so the tab reads as catching up), with nothing waited for from the old socket.

1. *A wake.* At `WAKE_DROP_MS` or more: the window is opened, the socket is dropped if there is one, and the socket is reopened by rule 3 with its backoff at the first step. Under it: a socket that is open is pinged now (rule 2's deadline); with none, the reopen is tried now. A parked feed, and a project socket ended by a terminal code, stay as they are.
2. *Liveness.* From a socket's `open`, a `ping()` every `HUB_PING_MS`. A ping with no `pong` within `HUB_PONG_MS` drops the socket and reopens it by rule 3. Only a `pong` answers a ping. 15 s is the period of `mawsd`'s own heartbeat to the hub, and a dead socket is then found within 20 s; 5 s is far above the 8 to 64 ms the pongs took on staging.
3. *Reopening.* One socket at a time, as today. Before an open the socket waits on `reach.reachable(<the hub's URL>)`, for `WINDOW_FLOOR_MS` at most. An open made while the window is open is given `HUB_WINDOW_OPEN_MS`, not `wsSocket`'s 10 s, for the lanes' reason (§F): a hub whose TCP answers and whose upgrade hangs then frees the socket in 2 s. An open that the reach's answer started (`true`: the hub's path answered a probe; `false`: no window is open, or it closed) is today's open, and one that fails takes the usual backoff before the next wait. An open started at the floor, with no answer yet, keeps its wait: an answer that comes before that socket has opened terminates it and opens again at once; and if it ends with nothing from the hub, the next wait starts with no backoff, the floor having paced it. So these sockets are never idle longer than 10 s where today's code would be opening, and a hub that returns late in the window is still met within a probe's interval.
4. *At `unlock-screen`*, heard from the wake source, the feed does what its `probe()` does: with a socket, a ping now; with none, today's reconnect. The project socket has no unlock probe today and gains none: rule 2 covers it.
5. *Its own end.* Every close the app decides (a wake, a missed pong, a session change, `close()`) is a `terminate()`. `wsSocket`'s `close(1000, 'done')` is no longer used for it.

Lines. The feed: `[org-feed] wake after <ms> ms: dropped the socket | no socket`; `[org-feed] no pong within 5000 ms: dropped the socket`; `[org-feed] probe: ping sent | no socket`; today's `[org-feed] connected to <host>`; `[org-feed] listings: last page, <n> records in <pages> pages` at the page that carries `last`. The project socket, under its `[org-channel] <project's first 8>:` prefix: the same wake and pong lines; `welcomed, resuming after <offset>`; `caught up at <offset>`.

*The target.* From the hub's path being reachable, `connected` and `welcomed` come after 100 ms + 4 h + H: about 0.6 s at `h` of 50 ms with `H` at §F's 300 ms (the feed's upgrade alone measured a median of 252 ms on staging, Decision Log 6). The feed's last listings page and the project's `caught-up` follow after the hub's reads and the pages' transfer, which this arithmetic does not bound. The target for an org of the owner's size is 2 s to the last page and to `caught-up`. The gate case holds the feed to it on loopback (§I); 7e records both sockets' times live and holds neither.

### §H. Main's log file and the lines

    // src/main/log-file.ts
    export const MAIN_LOG_HELD_LINES = 1_000;
    export interface MainLog {
      /** Opens <dir>/main.log, writes the lines held so far with their own times, and writes every later line as it comes. Never throws. False: the file log is off. */
      install(dir: string): boolean;
    }
    /** Wraps the console's log, info, warn and error, once. Until `install`, the newest MAIN_LOG_HELD_LINES lines are held in memory with their times. */

    // src/shared/host/error-words.ts
    export function errorWords(error: unknown): string;

*Two steps, and one owner of the file.* `src/main/app.ts` calls `captureMainLog(console)` at its load, right after `refuseTestLaunchOnDefaultProfile()` and before the build line. From then on each console call goes to the console as before, and its text, formatted as the console formats it (`node:util`'s `format`), is held in memory one line at a time, each with the time it was said; past 1,000 lines the oldest are dropped and counted. `install(<userData>/logs)` is the first statement of the start that `startWithProfileLock` runs, which only the instance that holds the profile lock reaches (`src/main/files/profile-lock.ts`). It writes the held lines to the file with their own times, a line `<n> earlier lines were not held` first when any were dropped, and from then on writes each line as it is said. So the file holds the build line and everything a launch said before it had the lock, a launch that ends at the repair window included, and exactly one process ever writes or rotates a profile's file. A second launch never installs: what it says goes to its own stdout and stderr and nowhere else. The first instance learns of it through `answerSecondLaunch`, which logs nothing today and gains one line: `[main] a second launch (<its build, or an unknown build>) was handed to this instance`. The render host's mode, which `src/main/index.ts` loads another root for, captures nothing.

*The file.* Every line is `<ISO-8601 UTC with ms> <text>`, the form `host.log` and `mawsd.log` have. At 8 MiB (`LOG_ROTATE_BYTES`) the file is renamed to `main.log.1` and a new one begun. The writer is main's own, not `openSupervisorLog`: that one creates its directory outside its guard, leaves an existing directory's mode as it finds it, and stamps a line when it is written, where a held line must carry the time it was said.

*It never throws.* `install` makes the directory with mode 0700 if it is missing; requires it to be a directory and not a symlink; sets it to 0700 when its mode is wider; creates `main.log` with mode 0600, or sets an existing one to 0600. Any of these that fails (the parent cannot be written, a file stands where the directory should be, the directory is another user's so its mode cannot be changed) turns the file log off: stdout and stderr go on exactly as today, the held lines are let go, and one line goes to stderr, `[main] the file log is off: <why>`. A write that fails later drops that line and says so once the same way.

*What the file is, and what counts as evidence.* `main.log` is a private diagnostic file on the owner's disk, under the profile's own modes. It holds whatever main's console holds today, which includes file paths and the text of errors (the quit guard's `could not save <path>: <message>` in `src/main/lifecycle/quit-guard.ts` is one); it is never copied into the repository (constraint 8). The lines of the table below are another matter: each carries ids, modes, positions, counts, carriers, reasons and addresses, and no message's text, title or path. Only they are evidence. §K gives the patterns that pick them out, and M4 copies only matching lines off a machine.

`errorWords` gives an error's `message`; then, when it has one, its `code` in parentheses; then, for an error with inner `errors` (Node's aggregate for a connect that failed on several addresses), each inner error's `code`, `address` and `port`. For example `connect ETIMEDOUT (ETIMEDOUT; ETIMEDOUT 192.0.2.1:443, ENETUNREACH 2001:db8::1:443)`.

### §I. The gate case and its seams

*The seam.* `installWakeSeam(env, packaged, wake)` in `src/main/net/wake.ts` sets `globalThis.__maws_wake = { inject(sleptMs: number): void }` when `env.MAWS_E2E` is `1` and the app is not packaged, and returns its removal; otherwise it sets nothing, as `installRemoteBenchSeam` does for `__maws_remote_bench`. A packaged app has no way to be told of a wake that did not happen. A unit test pins both refusals. It is the only seam M2b adds: the reach probes real loopback ports in the scene, and the case reads `<userData>/logs/main.log` through `test/support/main-log.ts`, which parses a line into its time and text.

*The pipe.* `pipeTo` in `e2e/support/remote-scene.ts` gains three states and a record, shown on `aHub` and `aDirect`. `stall()`: every connection open through the pipe is dead from then on, its bytes discarded both ways and neither side closed until the scene stops, and the pipe stops listening, so a new connect is refused. `hold()`: the pipe listens and accepts, and forwards nothing either way, so a TCP connect succeeds and whatever is sent on it goes unanswered: a path that answers TCP and hangs above it. `restore()`: the pipe listens and pipes every connection made from then on; the dead and the held ones stay as they are. `requests()`: for each connection since the scene began, its time and the first line its client sent, or null for one that sent nothing. The dead connections are what a half-open socket is after a sleep. The refused connect stands in for a SYN that nobody answers, which loopback cannot produce; the probe that hangs is `reach.test.ts`'s case.

*The case*, a second test of `e2e/remote-latency.spec.ts`, on a scene of its own with `directPipe`. B runs two sessions on the latency stub, both listed. A is launched once for each carrier over one profile, both times with B's address mapped to the direct pipe (`devOrgDir(true)`): for the direct launch the pipe is open; for the relay launch it is on hold from before the launch, so the launch's own direct race times out and the link comes up over the relay.

1. The link is `up` over the carrier and both of B's sessions are open in A's panes. The log's `subbed` lines since the launch give the link's six subscriptions, §F's six: two streams, two checkout feeds, the attention feed and the artifacts feed.
2. Both pipes are stalled. Each of B's sessions then writes 21 turns that A cannot see, about 6,300 events, past the 6,000 at which the ring first trims (`SessionStream.events`), so each stream's cursor in A is behind what B's ring holds. The ring is filled, not shrunk: its size is a dependency that only unit tests pass (`ringSize` in `src/engine-host/ipc.ts`) and no option reaches a spawned engine host, and a real fill makes the case the production ring and the production journal read. What it does not reach is an hour's journal; that is `J` (§F). At the stub's pace this is about 65 s for each launch.
3. `__maws_wake.inject(3_600_000)`. The log shows the wake line and the link's `wake after 3600000 ms: dropped …` line, whatever it names: by then the link's own silence check has lost the channel.
4. After a wait drawn between 1 and 3 s, `restoredAt` is the runner's clock at the instant of the restore. On the direct launch the direct pipe alone is restored. On the relay launch the hub's pipe is restored and in the same turn the direct pipe is put on hold, so the direct lane's target answers TCP and its upgrade hangs.
5. *Passes when:* the link's `up over the <carrier> carrier` line and a `subbed` line for each of the six subscriptions are in the log, each at most 1,000 ms after `restoredAt`; each stream's line reads `replay` and asks from the cursor it held; B's `mawsd.log` has the matching `sub` lines, and B's `host.log` has a `session.events … from the journal` line for each stream after `restoredAt`, which is the proof that the cursors were evicted; each pane shows each marker of its session's last unseen turn once; the log has no `dropped a repeated event` line. Since step 2, on the direct launch `aDirect` saw exactly one upgrade request and `aHub`, still stalled, none. On the relay launch `aHub` saw exactly one request for `/api/machines/<ref>/socket`, and the log has no `up over the direct carrier` line: the direct lane's dials reached the held pipe and went unanswered, and the relay did not wait behind them. Probes, the connections that sent nothing, are not counted.
6. The hub's pipe is restored where it was not, still inside the window (on the relay launch it was restored at step 4, and that instant is the hub's restore), and the feed comes back: since step 2, `aHub` saw exactly one request for `/api/registry/socket?app=1`, and `[org-feed] connected` and `[org-feed] listings: last page` are each in the log at most 2,000 ms after the hub's restore.

Under `MAWS_REMOTE_LATENCY_PERF=1` step 5's 1,000 ms and step 6's 2,000 ms are held, and the run is void by the file's own load rule, never averaged. Under `MAWS_REMOTE_LATENCY_CHECK=1` the same lines and counts must be there, every time is recorded and none is held. The numbers go to `test-results/remote-wake.json`, with each stream's journal length beside its time. The first test and its bounds are untouched. The case adds about three minutes to the group.

What the case shows of §F's budget: the lanes, the counts, and `J` for two evicted cursors on journals of about 6,500 events (the first launch) and 13,000 (the second), with every round trip near zero. What it does not show: `J` on a long journal, a catch-up, and the hub stalled above TCP while direct works, which is `link.test.ts`'s case; the hub's pipe is not put on hold here because the feed, whose own bound this case holds, opens through it.

The project socket is not in the case. ### §J. The owner on a peer message

sminos writes the sender session's owners into its frame line, between the sender's alias and the arrow or the id (`compose_chat_frame`, `compose_frame` and `sender_labels` in doperpowers' `sminos/skills/sminos/scripts/sminos.py`, pinned there by `sminos/tests/test_words.py`):

    [sminos chat <project> <writer>:<seq> from <alias>, owned by <name>[, <name>…] → <target>[ | unread N]]
    [sminos message from <alias>, owned by <name>[, <name>…] id=<msg_id>]

A `<name>` is an owner's email, or its member id where the endpoint had no email. In place of `, owned by …` the label may be ` (owners unverified)`, ` (unverified, legacy)`, ` (signed by a revoked key)` or ` (unverified)`, or absent. This repository's fixtures hold no labelled frame today (`src/shared/engine/peer-envelope.test.ts`, the lane A tests): M2b adds them, in sminos's own words from that test.

*The alias has two forms, and the parse reads both.* Today sminos writes the alias exactly as its writer wrote it. The desk track will change sminos, after its M3, to write the alias as a JSON string literal whenever it holds any of `,` `(` `)` `"` `→` `[` `]` or a newline, and bare otherwise, so that the label is always outside the alias.

    // src/shared/engine/peer-envelope.ts
    /** The owners a sminos frame line attests for its sender; null for a frame with any other label or none. */
    export function sminosFrameOwners(frame: string): { alias: string; owners: string[] } | null;
    // src/renderer/src/transcript/model/rows.ts: PeerMessage.sender gains
    owners: string[] | null;   // the frame's names as written; null: the frame attested none
    // src/renderer/src/sidebar/owners.ts
    /** A frame's names as member ids: an email that `members` holds is its member's id; any other name stays as written. */
    export function ownerIdsOf(names: readonly string[], members: ReadonlyMap<string, OrgMember>): string[];
    // ownerEntries and ownersLabel gain a last parameter: unknown?: (id: string) => string, FORMER_MEMBER by default

- *The parse.* The sender text is what stands between `from ` and the arrow of a chat frame, or the ` id=` of a message frame. Where it opens with `"`, the alias is the JSON string literal at its start, decoded as one, and the search for a chat frame's arrow starts after that literal, so a quoted alias may hold ` → ` or `]` (`chatFrameSender` learns this; a literal that does not parse makes the line no frame). Otherwise the alias runs to the first `,` or ` (`, or to the end. What follows the alias is the label. `sminosFrameOwners` answers only for a label that is `, owned by ` and then names split on `, `, each one token with no space, comma or parenthesis; for any other label, or none, it answers null. What `sminosFrameSender` returns stays the sender text as written, so lane A's reading of today's frames is unchanged; how lane A draws a quoted alias is the desk track's when sminos ships it.
- *The model.* `derive.ts` already parses each `user.message` of peer origin to decide that its companion event draws it. It keeps, by `messageUuid`, the owners of an envelope whose `frame` is not null, and `inboundRow` puts them on the row of the `peer.message` with the same `messageUuid`. An event with no `messageUuid`, an outbound row and an in-process agent's message have `owners: null`.
- *The drawing.* In `PeerMessageRow` of `src/renderer/src/transcript/view/rows/AgentMessage.tsx`, after the sender's `@ name`, a span marked `data-owners` in the secondary style the sidebar's label uses (`RowTitle`), holding `ownersLabel(ownerIdsOf(owners, members), me, members, (id) => id)`, with `me` and `members` from the sessions store as `useOwnersLabel` reads them. The rule is therefore Decision Log 111's, by the sidebar's own function: nothing when you alone own the sender's session, else "you" first, two names shown and "+N" past them. A name `members` does not hold, as when signed out, is drawn as written. The span's `title` lists every owner.
- *What the label is worth.* It is a display of sminos's frame, not a verification by MAWS: MAWS checks no signature here and draws what the line says. With today's sminos the alias is unconstrained, so an alias that holds `, owned by x` reads as a label on an event sminos left unlabelled, and one that holds a comma before a real label voids it. Until sminos writes such an alias as a string literal, the owner MAWS draws on a peer message is as trustworthy as sminos's frame and no more. M5 writes the tracker row, with the desk track as the fix's owner and the sminos version recorded when it ships.

Unit: `peer-envelope.test.ts` (both frame kinds with one owner and with two, an owner by id; each other label and no label answer null; a name with a space answers null; a quoted alias that holds `, owned by x`, ` → @you` and `]` before a real label yields the decoded alias and the real owners; a quoted alias with no label answers null; a bare alias that holds `, owned by x` and no label reads as owned by x, which is today's limit and is named so in the test); `derive` (a peer message whose envelope carries a labelled frame has `sender.owners`, one without has null, pairing by `messageUuid`); `peer.test.tsx` (no span when the one owner is you; "you, Ana" when you share it; another member's name when you do not own it; a name unknown to `members` as written).

### §K. The 7e probe and the evidence lines (`test/support/wake-probe.mjs`)

One Node script with no dependency, so that it can be copied to a machine and run with `node` alone. It lives in `test/support/`, beside the suites' other standalone script (`latency-engine-stub.mjs`): `scripts/` is M1's ground, and a file here is covered by the unit suite's `test/**/*.test.ts`.

    node test/support/wake-probe.mjs watch --target <name>=<host>:<port> [--target …] [--https <url>] --out <dir>
    node test/support/wake-probe.mjs read <dir> --after <time>
    node test/support/wake-probe.mjs extract <log file> [--since <time>]

- `watch` is the app's own probe, on purpose. For each target a bare TCP connect starts every 100 ms; each is given 3 s; they overlap; one that connects is closed at once. Each try is one JSON line in `<dir>/<name>.jsonl`, written when it ends: its number, `started` and `ended` on the wall clock in ms, `ms`, its duration on Node's monotonic clock, `ok`, and the error's code when it failed. With `--https` it also makes one GET of that URL every 2 s, each given 3 s, and writes each one's start, end and status or error to `<dir>/https.jsonl`: this is Decision Log 6 (k)'s cross-check, a request that succeeds while the probes to the same host fail. It runs until it is interrupted.
- *The clock.* A try's `started` and `ended` are read from the wall clock at those moments, because `main.log`'s times are the wall clock's and the comparison is between the two files. One monotonic clock tied to the wall clock once at the start would be wrong after the sleep if Node's monotonic clock does not advance through it, and this repository does not show whether it does (§D). The monotonic clock gives only a try's own duration, and no try that counts spans the sleep.
- `read` prints, for each target, the first try with `ok` that ended after `--after`, which is the time of `main.log`'s `[wake]` line. `reachable` is that try's `ended`. `uncertaintyMs` is 100 plus its `ms`: the path may have returned at any moment between the start of the try before it and its own completion. It also prints the median `ms` of the successful tries in the 30 s before the last try that preceded the sleep and in the 30 s after `reachable`: that is `h` for the hub's target and `d` for a machine's direct port.
- `m` is read on the machine: the script is copied there and `watch --target hub=<the hub's host>:443` is run for 30 s before the sleep and after the wake; its median is the machine's own round trip to the hub.
- `extract` prints the lines of a `main.log`, a `mawsd.log` or a `host.log` whose text, after the time, matches one of the patterns below, and prints nothing else. It is the only way a line leaves a machine for the evidence directory (§H).

The evidence patterns. Each is matched against the whole text after a line's time; `<n>` is digits, `<id>`, `<ref>`, `<key>`, `<host>`, `<position>` and `<cursor>` are runs with no space, and `…` is any text to the end of the line or to the next literal:

    [wake] slept <n> ms (resume|clock)
    [reach] window open for 60 s (wake|network)
    [reach] <host>:<n>: reachable after <n> probes, <n> ms
    [reach] <host>:<n>: window closed after <n> probes, none connected (…)
    [machines] <ref>: wake after <n> ms: dropped …
    [machines] <ref>: probe <word>: …
    [machines] <ref>: attempt (direct|relay|today's): catch-up, dial <n>
    [machines] <ref>: attempt failed (direct|relay|today's): …
    [machines] <ref>: up over the (direct|relay) carrier…
    [machines] <ref>: the … channel was lost (…); reconnecting
    [machines] <ref>: subscription <id> …: subbed (replay|snapshot|missing) at <position>, asked from <cursor>
    [machines] <ref>: subscription <id>: dropped a repeated event at <position>
    [org-feed] wake after <n> ms: …
    [org-feed] no pong within <n> ms: dropped the socket
    [org-feed] probe: …
    [org-feed] connected to <host>
    [org-feed] listings: last page, <n> records in <n> pages
    [org-channel] <8 hex>: wake after <n> ms: …
    [org-channel] <8 hex>: no pong within <n> ms: dropped the socket
    [org-channel] <8 hex>: welcomed, resuming after <n>
    [org-channel] <8 hex>: caught up at <n>
    endpoint: channel <key> sub <id> …: subbed (replay|snapshot|missing) at <position>, asked from <cursor>
    endpoint: the hub connection …; connecting again in <n> ms
    ring evicted <id> launch <id>: events through seq <n> are the journal's now
    session.events <id> after (<n>|start): from the journal

What `…` stands for in these lines is a carrier's name, a reason word, an error's words (§H) or a subscription's `<what>`: none is a session's text.

Unit (`test/support/wake-probe.test.ts`, which runs the script as a child process): `extract` over a file that holds one sample of every pattern mixed with a line that carries a file path and a line of free text prints exactly the samples; `read` over a recorded directory names the first success after the given time, its uncertainty and the two medians; `watch` against a loopback listener that refuses and then accepts records tries 100 ms apart that overlap, and a first success.

## Surprises & Discoveries

- Observation: the embedded shape was not only a setting. A launch on the main profile whose LaunchAgent is absent, not loaded, refused or silent ran embedded without anyone choosing it (`planShape`, `prepareShape` in `src/main/hosts/daemon.ts`).
  Evidence: the inventory of the embedded root, §1.3, read against the code on 2026-11-10.
- Observation: both of a native Mac's signing keys, the channel key and the step-up key that is now production's root, are plaintext private JWKs in one file; Touch ID is a step in the app's flow and binds nothing to the key.
  Evidence: `src/main/device/keys.ts` (the record and its write), `src/main/device/step-up.ts`; the inventory's §5.
- Observation: a client settings file of a version newer than a build knows is read as the defaults, so a version bump here would cost a rolled-back machine its settings.
  Evidence: `migrateSettings` in `src/shared/settings/defaults.ts`: any other version throws, "which a store turns into the defaults with a warning".
- Observation: the org feed's wake probe returned at once whenever a socket object existed, and the feed never wrote on its socket and had no liveness check, so a feed socket that died silently during a sleep stayed dead until the session's next change or a relaunch.
  Evidence: `probe()` in `src/main/org/registry-feed.ts` at master e8be3867e (`if (closed || socket !== null || parked) return;`); the readiness read of 2026-11-10.
- Observation: the direct listener charges its per-source bucket at the upgrade request, before the WebSocket exists, so anything that opens WebSockets to learn whether a path is back spends the tokens the handshake needs.
  Evidence: `#upgrade` in `src/endpoint/direct-listener.ts` (`this.#limiter.take(source)` before `handleUpgrade`); the desk track's read of 2026-11-10.
- Observation: neither hub object completes a close handshake, so a client `close(1000)` on the feed or on a project socket ends as 1006 about 10 s later; and the hub's runtime answers a protocol ping without waking the object.
  Evidence: the desk track's staging run of 2026-11-10 (Decision Log 6, (d)).
- Observation: sminos's frame line carries the sender session's owners, and no fixture of this repository held a labelled frame, so the label's form had to be read from sminos's own source and test.
  Evidence: doperpowers' `sminos/skills/sminos/scripts/sminos.py` (`sender_labels`) and `sminos/tests/test_words.py`; `src/shared/engine/peer-envelope.test.ts`, whose frames carry no label.
