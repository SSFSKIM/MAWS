## Purpose

After this landing the owner sends without thinking about modes. Enter sends whatever the draft's line count. While the agent works, Enter hands the message to the running turn and ⌘Enter keeps it for after the turn. A message the agent has not read yet is drawn once, in a stack above the composer, where it can be sent now, taken back with ↑, edited or deleted, and it is no longer drawn a second time as a dimmed bubble in the transcript. Send now no longer kills the command the agent was running: the engine moves the command to the background, the turn ends reading "Ended for your message", and the message is answered at once. Stop still stops and holds what was waiting.

Around the box: the button is one small round icon; `/` lists command names, with the description beside the highlighted one, and has `/fork`; the mode menu sets Bypass permissions apart; the model chip reads "Opus 5.5 High"; fast mode is a row with a switch and the effort track can be hit anywhere; a paste stays text; the New chat chooser tells two projects of one name apart; ↑ through history says where it is, Ctrl+R searches it, and ⇧⌘I, ⇧⌘E and ⇧⌘M open the model list, the effort control and the mode menu; `/btw` answers in a small card at the bottom right that says how long it has been thinking.

Three things the child's file promises are not in this landing, because they wait for other landings (the root's ordering, U4b): the three controls of a waiting message that need a new offer are drawn refused for a session on another machine, the `/btw` card always lies over the pane's corner, and a waiting answer to a question keeps today's wording. "The landing's edge" says what each of these looks like to a person until U4b.

How to see it working: the unit tests named under Acceptance replay the probe's recorded sessions and the composer's own cases; in a running build under a scratch config home, the scenes of Acceptance 16.

## Words used here

The child's file defines a *waiting message*, *at the next step*, *after the turn*, *the stack* and *Send now*; they are used here as defined there. This document adds:

- **A pending steer:** the code's name for a message that waits at the next step: written to the engine at priority `next`, not yet started (`PendingSteer`, the `steers` of the queue's state).
- **The host:** the engine host, the process that launches and drives the engine for a session MAWS runs (`src/engine-host/`). **The journal:** its record of every frame written to and read from the engine, per launch; **the normaliser** turns the journal's rows into X1 events (`src/engine-host/normalizer.ts`).
- **A marked end:** a `turn.ended` event that carries `endedForMessage` (Interfaces B): the turn was ended by a message sent now, and no stop took effect.
- **The seam:** `sendHumanPrompt`, the one path a typed message takes. **The port:** the session's controls object (`session/controls/`), which hides whether a session is run by MAWS (lane B in code) or by a terminal (lane A).
- **The helper:** `useRemoteUnavailable` in `lane-b/remote-unavailable.ts` (U9's), which answers the sentence a control is disabled with for a session on another machine, or null.
- **The serving host:** the engine host process that serves this Mac's sessions at this moment. After an app update it can be the earlier build's, until its sessions are idle or handed over (decision 30).
- **Unread:** the mark on a transcript row of a message that waited behind a turn and has neither started nor been dropped (decision 12). It outlives `pending`, which is set only while another turn runs.
- **The stage:** the complete copy of a message that a take-back saves in the session's draft store before it asks for the withdrawal: a draft whose document carries `takeBack`. **The claim:** the condition the stage is saved under, that no draft with a document is kept there. **The fence:** the store's refusal, while a stage is kept, of every save but the one that settles it. **To settle:** to replace the stage by the ordinary draft, or to clear it, with the one form of `replaces` the fence lets through (decision 13).
- **The record:** the session's `queue.transition` and `command.lifecycle` events, as `session.promptHistory` answers them. It says what became of a waiting message: `cancel`, `remove`, `hold`, `promote`, `release`, `drop`, `abandon`, and the engine's `started`.
- **The move:** the engine putting the running call in the background so that a message sent now can be read. It comes 0.8 to 1.0 s after the engine answers `delivering` (the second pass, 27 runs). For a `now` frame it comes 5 to 9 ms after the write where the running call is already a task, and not at all before that (the third pass).
- **The machine's services:** the process that keeps a machine's index, its draft store included (`mawsd`; commands of owner `machine`). On this Mac it can be of another build than the app and than the engine host.
- **Build F:** `f1852de3a`, the build on the owner's machines, which may be gone back to (UC-14).

## Decisions settled at authoring

Each is a means the section left open, with its reason and what lost. Where a decision is also a point for the architect's ruling it says so, and "Reported to the architect" gives the one place a ruling changes.

1. **Eight milestones, in this order.** M1 to M3 are the one design of sending, cut where the state's owner changes: the engine host and what its events mean (M1), the box and its one decision table (M2), the stack that reads both (M3). M4 to M7 are the controls around the box, grouped by the files they share: the palette (M4), the two chips' menus (M5), the editor's helpers and the chooser (M6), the `/btw` card (M7). M8 is the record, the e2e edits and the last run. Each leaves the tree green: a unit test that pins what a milestone changes is edited in that milestone; the e2e specs, which no executor runs, are edited together in M8, so that the architect's runs have one list. Lost: one milestone per item, sixteen boundaries for parts that cannot be told apart in a diff; and two milestones, each past what one reviewer reads in a pass.
2. **The host's record of a send-now is the journal's own rows.** The section says "the host records each send-now it sends". The journal already holds every frame the host writes, the normaliser already reads those rows, and it is the one place that is the same after a reload, in a second window and on a page cursor, for as long as the launch's journal serves the session's rows (Reported, 4, says what reads otherwise once it does not). So the rule is a fold over rows that exist (Interfaces B) and nothing new is journaled or stored. Lost: a new `meta queue` row (a second record of the same fact, and a new action name that the strict `queue.transition` enum must never see); a flag held by the session object (it is gone for a page that reads the launch's journal again). Reported, 1: the fold surfaces as one optional field on X1's `turn.ended`.
3. **The request is written exactly as the probe recorded it:** `{ subtype: 'interrupt', send_now: true, message_uuid }`, with no `reason` and no `cancel_queued` (law 6: what was probed is what is sent, and a replay of the fixture then matches the host's write without an edit).
4. **The command is graded `converse`.** The section says it takes "the grade `session.steer` has", which is `converse` as a floor with a rule that reads the message's content. This command carries no content: the words were graded when they were written, and what it adds is ending the running turn, which `session.interrupt` and `session.steer.cancel` already allow at `converse`. Reported, 8, for U9's review when U4b adds the offer.
5. **An answer without `send_now` reads `stopped`.** An engine that does not know the key takes the request for a plain interrupt: the turn ends, the command is killed, and the waiting message starts the next turn, since nothing held it. That is today's Send now without the pause, and `stopped` is the truthful word for it. By the bundles' text the key is read from engine 2.1.287 on; P1 ran 2.1.322 (Reported, 5). The second pass ran 2.1.283 and found exactly that (`u4p2-old-1.jsonl` seq 79 to 85).
6. **The digest keeps `lastTurnEndedAt` at a marked end and goes idle.** The notice "Turn finished" and the unread mark both hang on that time moving, so neither fires for the cut turn and both fire once, when the message's own turn ends. The phase goes to `idle` rather than staying `working`, so that a session whose next turn never starts cannot read as working for ever. Lost: an edit in main's notifier (U1's file) for a rule the digest can hold in one branch. The second pass found no state frame between the cut turn's end and the message's start in any of 24 runs: the session's state stays `running`, and the first `idle` comes after the message's own turn (`u4p2-a-1.jsonl` seq 47 to 49, and 193).
7. **Two strings the section does not word** (Reported, 3). The turn's line keeps its shape and reads "Ended for your message after 13s", then the counts: the section quotes "Ended for your message", and a line without its duration would be the only one of its kind. The moved command's row reads "Moved `<command>` to the background after 13s": A3 says the row "reads as moved to the background", and today it would read "Ran … in 13s" while the command still runs. The same row is any moved call's, a shell command's or not (decision 33).
8. **⌘Enter is an option on the one seam.** `when: 'after'` rides `HumanPromptOptions` through the port to `sendHumanPrompt`, which then never chooses the steer; the host's queue does the holding, as the section says ("no new host code"). With no turn running the option changes nothing, so ⌘Enter sends as Enter does by the seam's own rule and not by a second test of "is a turn running" in the editor.
9. **The button's word stays its text.** The word is drawn as visually hidden text inside the button, beside a glyph hidden from assistive technology, and not as an `aria-label`. The accessible name, `textContent` and Playwright's `toHaveText` then all stay the word, which is what the section's "tests and screen readers keep finding it" needs: about twenty unit cases and the e2e helpers read the text, not the name. Lost: `aria-label` alone, which keeps the name and breaks every one of them for nothing.
10. **The rows, their ids and their order** (Interfaces H). Five `fixed` rows for the editor's own keys, under ids that name what the key does. `composer.queue` is the only chord the main editor binds; `composer.send` keeps its id and its chord for the three reply fields and is retitled. The three menu chords are app-scoped and registered once in `ComposerAppKeys`. Rows are added where the composer's rows stand, the two rows on ⌘Enter in the order `composer.queue`, `composer.send`: the dispatcher tries rows in table order, and each is delivered only to a binding whose element holds the focus, so the order is not a priority between them.
11. **A fixed key cannot be captured for another action** (U6a's decision 7 left this to "the landing that adds the first fixed row"; Reported, 10). From this landing Enter, Shift+Enter, Option+Enter and ↑ are listed in Settings ▸ Keyboard as the composer's fixed keys, and the capture would still let "Cycle permission mode" take Enter, after which the composer could not send and nothing would say why. So `conflictOf` (`settings-tab/keymap.ts`, U6's) counts a fixed row as a holder of its chord against any row that could take that key from the fixed row's surface: a row of the same scope, an `app`-scoped row, or a `menu` row. A `pane`-scoped row is never delivered from a text field (the dispatcher's `refused`), so `request.accept` and `request.decline` keep Enter and Esc and can be given them back. The refusal is one new sentence, `KEYBOARD.fixedKey` (Interfaces I), because the existing one ends "unbind it first" and a fixed key cannot be unbound. Nothing stored is read differently: a rebinding a profile already holds on such a key keeps winning at dispatch, as U6a's decision 10 has it for every stored rebinding. Lost: leaving it as residue (the list in Settings would name a key as fixed that the same page lets the person take away); refusing at dispatch (it would silently change what a stored binding does).
12. **The transcript hides a message exactly while it is unread, and only a mount with a stack asks for that.** The model gains one mark that follows a message's own life. Where the reducer opens a turn for a prompt that has none yet (`transcript/model/reduce.ts`), it sets `waited` on that turn when another turn is running or an earlier prompt has not started; `derive` marks the prompt's row `unread` while its turn is `waited`, open, not accepted by the engine and not dropped. The mark is true until the message starts or is dropped, and so it holds through the gap that `pending` does not cover: `pending` is derived only while another turn runs, and in `u4p1-a-seeded-1.jsonl` the cut turn closes at seq 46 and the waiting message starts at seq 48. `pending`, its two captions and the held tail are left exactly as they are. `TranscriptSlot` gains an optional prop, `waiting?: 'stacked'`, which the session's pane passes: with it the slot appends no held rows and `withQueueTail` drops the rows marked `unread`; without it everything is as today, on purpose, because the side thread's card mounts the same slot with no composer and its held bubble is its only drawing of a reply that waits. A prompt sent with no turn running is never `waited`, so its bubble shows at once, as today. The stack reads the same mark from the same store (Interfaces G), so one state decides both drawings and a message is in exactly one of them in every render: an unread message the queue lists as a pending steer is its "Next step" row; an unread message the queue does not list (one sent now from an after-the-turn row, until the engine starts it; or one whose row arrived before the queue's snapshot) is a "Next step" row with no controls (decision 15); a pending steer the transcript already shows as started is no row. Lost: a filter on `pending` (this decision's first version; the gap above lets a bubble through while the stack still lists the message); a filter by the queue's list of pending steers alone (the row and the queue's snapshot arrive on different channels, so a bubble would show for a frame at every Enter); removing the tail for every mount (the side card would show nothing for a reply that waits). Reported, 2. Where several messages go together, each is unread until its own `started` (`u4p2-two-1-1.jsonl` seq 54 and 55), and what is then drawn for them is decision 32's.
13. **Taking a message back never leaves it nowhere.** One module (`composer/queue/take-back.ts`) runs it for ↑ and for Edit, in five steps. At each a complete copy of the message is somewhere that survives the step failing: in the host's queue until the withdrawal is saved there; in the session's draft store from before the withdrawal is asked; in the box from the load. Nothing owns the operation: every step is idempotent, and whoever finds a stage carries it forward.

    *What the draft store learns* (`DraftStore.set` in `src/services/index/drafts.ts`, each decided in the transaction of the write; Interfaces D). All three are values of the `replaces` that `session.drafts.set` already takes, or an attribute of the document, so no schema changes; and a store of an earlier build refuses each new value as stale, since it compares `replaces` with its row's stamp, so nothing is ever staged where the rest would not hold. (a) *The claim.* A save whose `replaces` is `DRAFT_CLAIM` is written only where no draft with a document is kept for the reader: a slot never written, and a cleared one alike. The store's `null` cannot say this: a clear leaves a stamped tombstone, every send clears, and the window cannot name the tombstone because its read hides it. (b) *The stage.* A draft whose document carries `attrs.takeBack: { ref, kind, since }`: the message's name in the queue, which kind of name it is, and the time the queue gave its row. (c) *The fence.* While the kept draft is a stage, the store refuses every save but the one that settles it, whose `replaces` is `settles(<the stage's revision>)`. It asks for that form and not for the bare revision because a writer for a session on another machine reads what is kept after any refusal and names it at its next save (Reported, 21). The fence holds off another window's writer, a save of the emptied box that was still pending (the local writer names no `replaces`), another device's writer, and build F's wherever this build's store serves it.

    (1) *Stage.* The message's document (`entryDoc`) is given the attribute. That session's box is read-only from here to the end, with "Taking the message back…". (2) *Save under the claim, whole and acknowledged.* The take-back invokes `session.drafts.set` itself, with every attachment's bytes (the 4 MiB budget of `draftOf` belongs to the editor's writer and is not applied), and awaits the answer; for a session on another machine the save goes to that machine, which keeps the member's drafts. Refused because a document is kept: "A draft is kept for this session, so the message was left waiting". Refused by the fence: a stage is kept already, and it is carried forward first (step 5). Refused as stale: that store is of an earlier build (below). Failed, cut to fit the link, or no drafts kept there: "Could not keep the message as a draft, so it was left waiting". In each of these nothing has been withdrawn and the row stays. (3) *Withdraw.* `session.steer.cancel` for a message at the next step, `session.queue.remove` for the others; then the record decides, never the answer alone (the table below). (4) *Load and settle.* `EditorHandle.load(doc)`, without the attribute; then the session's draft writer saves the box as an ordinary draft with `replaces: settles(…)`, at once, and names it again at each save until one is acknowledged; the box stays read-only until then. From that save on the slot is the editor's, as today. A settling save answered as stale means that no stage is kept at that revision any more: another mount settled first, or this mount's own earlier save landed and its answer was lost. The mount then does not keep the copy it loaded. It reads what is kept and takes that as a mount does: it loads it in place of its copy, an empty draft included, or carries a stage forward. A refused load (a card holds the box, a submission is in flight) leaves the stage stored, says "Taken back; it will be in the box when the box is free", and is tried again at the editor's next change of state. (5) *Carry forward.* Whoever meets a stored draft that is a stage (a mount that restores it, a second window, the initiator after an error or a lost answer, a claim the fence refused) is read-only with "Taking the message back…" and does steps 3 and 4 itself. A stage that is found is never rolled back. "Still listed" is a reason to withdraw and never a reason to drop the stage: the person pressed Edit so that the message would not go out as it was, and no interleaving of forward steps leaves the message in neither place, where rolling back does (a second mount drops the stage while the first mount's withdrawal is on its way).

    *What the record says* (`session.promptHistory` from `since`, with the command's own answer), read in the order of its rows, under the message's current name:

    | the record, or the answer | then |
    |---|---|
    | this caller's own `cancelled`, or its own removal resolved; a `cancel` row for the uuid; a `remove` row for the queue id; a `drop` or an `abandon` row (the engine dropped it unstarted, or the session ended with it unread) | it will not be delivered: the stage is the draft (step 4) |
    | a `hold` row for the uuid (a Stop made it a held entry), or a `promote` row for the queue id (it was sent on under a uuid) | it has a new name: go on under that name |
    | a `revert` row after a `release` or a `promote` (the frame's write failed, and that uuid never reached the engine) | the release or the promotion is undone: the message is listed again under its queue id, so ask for the withdrawal |
    | a `started` lifecycle for the uuid, or a `release` row for the queue id with no `revert` after it | the agent has it: the stage is cleared by a save that settles it, and the line reads "The agent has already read this message" |
    | none of these, and the queue lists it | ask for the withdrawal |
    | none of these and not listed, or the record is not complete | the record cannot say: the stage is loaded as the draft, under "This message may already have been read; check the transcript before sending it again" |

    A second withdrawal's answer is not evidence. `session.steer.cancel` answers `already_started` for any uuid that is not a pending steer, one that another mount has just cancelled included (`cancelSteer`), and is rejected with "This message is already being withdrawn" while another caller's withdrawal is in hand. `session.queue.remove` of an entry already removed is rejected with "no queued entry", as it is for one that was sent and retired. After each of these the record is read. The take-back never sends anything by itself. `release` and `promote` are journaled before the frame is written, and a failed write is followed by `revert` (`write` and `revert` in `src/engine-host/queue.ts`). A read that lands between a `release` and the `revert` of a write that then fails clears the stage while the row stays listed, under a line that says the agent read it; nothing is built for that, since the message is neither lost nor sent twice. Two mounts that carry one stage forward both reach step 4; the second one's settling save is refused as stale, and by step 4's rule that mount takes what is kept: the draft the first settled, or an empty box where the first has already sent it. The message is in the draft once and is sent once.

    *Attachments.* While the take-back runs the stage holds every byte and the fence keeps it. After the load the message is a draft and a draft's budget applies as it does today: an attachment past 4 MiB stays in the box until it is sent, is not in the stored draft, and its tile says "not kept in draft" (`composer/frame/AttachmentStrip.tsx`).

    *After the settle* the message is an ordinary draft, and two mounts of one session are last-writer-wins on a stored draft, as they have always been (the local writer names no `replaces`; the writer for another machine goes again on the stale words). A save from another mount of the same member can so replace the settled draft, and the message is then only in the first mount's box. One mount is covered: a save or a send's clear that was pending lands during the stage and is fenced, and the load waits for a submission in flight. The rest is a tracker row (M8), not this landing's design.

    *Where the store is of an earlier build* (Reported, 20). A claim answered as stale can only come from a store that does not know the claim. The take-back then withdraws nothing, says "Taking a message back needs a newer MAWS on `<machine>`" (for this Mac: "Taking a message back needs this Mac's MAWS services updated"), and remembers it for that machine: until that machine's link opens again, or this Mac's services restart, Edit and ↑ are drawn refused with the sentence for its sessions. Delete is never refused on this ground. The predicate of decision 30 is not the test here: the draft store is the machine's services', not the engine host's.

    Lost: withdrawing first and filling after (the first version); `replaces: null` as the claim (the second version: refused after every send); dropping a found stage whose message is still listed (the second version: two mounts could lose the message between them); reading a stale settle as done (the third version: the other mount may have sent the message meanwhile); reading a `release` row as delivery whatever follows it (the third version: a failed write reverts it); an owner of the operation for the session (the fence and forward-only steps need none); refusing Edit for a message with an attachment past the draft's budget (it removes the control for an ordinary screenshot, while the limit after loading is today's limit, drawn today); keeping full bytes through the editor's later saves (a second rule of persistence for one path); a command of its own for the stage (it would need an offer and a capability of its own in U9's files before any session on another machine could take a message back, where the existing offer carries a value). What is stored: Interfaces J.
14. **A refused act says its sentence and does nothing else** (UC-7). On a session on another machine, where taking back or sending now a next-step message is not offered, ↑ (when that message is the newest) and Esc (when it is the first) show the helper's sentence in the stack's notice line; ↑ does not fall through to the prompt history and Esc does not fall back to a Stop. Falling through would end the act without its effect and without a reason; ⌘. and the square remain the Stop. Reported, 7. Only a fresh press of Esc acts. `keyDown` hands every Escape to the composer today, auto-repeats included, so one press held while the first row is sent would find the stack empty at a repeat and stop the message's new turn. A repeat never reaches `onEscape`: it is consumed when the press it belongs to was the composer's and left alone otherwise. The double Esc that opens the rewind picker therefore takes two presses, which is what "twice" says. The same sentence-and-nothing-else rule holds where the serving host does not take Send now (decision 30).
15. **The stack's details.** A row's mark is "Next step" for a pending steer, "Held" for a queued entry in a paused queue and "After the turn" for any other queued entry. "Send at the next step" is in a row's menu only while a turn runs. Send now's title is "Sends this now; what the agent is running keeps running" while a turn runs and today's "Sends this now" otherwise. The row that may not have been delivered keeps today's shape, and its Edit, like every Edit, puts the message back in the box: `QueueEdit.tsx` is deleted, and the host's `session.queue.edit` stays (another machine's build still calls it) with no caller in this window. The notice line ("The agent has already read this message", "The box holds a draft", a refused act's sentence) is one `role="status"` line where the stack stands, drawn even when no row is left, and cleared at the next change of the queue or the next key in the box. Whether a folded stack is open is kept per session in the composer's store for the window's life. On hover or keyboard focus a row's three controls stand just left of its mark, over the end of the text behind a short fade: the mark stays where it is and the text does not reflow. A "Sending…" row has no controls. Once this window's Send now on a next-step row has been answered `delivering` or `stopped`, every next-step row reads "Sending…" until the engine starts it, for about a second: the engine sends them all (decision 32). Where more than one message waits at the next step, Send now's title on each of them is "Sends the 2 waiting messages now; what the agent is running keeps running", with their number. Send now on a row that waits after the turn, or on a held one, is a `now` frame, and what that does to a running shell command depends on the moment (the third pass; Reported, 25). Where the command is already a task, from about 3 s into the call, the engine moves it within milliseconds and cuts the turn about a second later. Before that it moves nothing: the command runs to its own end and the message is delivered then (a tracker row, M8). So from the press until the engine starts the message, the row reads "Next step" and has no controls: those words promise the agent's next step, which is what the engine gives in both cases, and never a cut that has not happened. | the message, when the command arrives | answer | written to the engine |
|---|---|---|
| a hold, a Stop or a cancel is withdrawing it (`withdrawing`) | rejects, "This message is already being withdrawn" | nothing |
| not a pending steer: the engine started or dropped it, or it never was one | `nothing_waiting` | nothing |
| a pending steer, the process not ready | rejects, "the session is not ready" | nothing |

The engine's `send_now` is read so: `stopped` and `nothing_waiting` as themselves; an answer with no `send_now` key as `stopped` (decision 5); any other string as `delivering`. The command pauses nothing, holds nothing, removes nothing from the pending steers (the engine's `started` for the message does, as today), and does not mark an interrupt as owed (the mark of `Session.interrupt` exists so that a Stop survives a host crash; a send-now that is lost leaves the message waiting, which is the safe side). A timeout is the bounded command's (`timeout`, "interrupt unanswered after … s") and leaves everything as it was. The request names one message, and the engine delivers with it every message that waits at the next step (decision 32). The answer's `still_queued` lists what waits at the answer, the named message included (`u4p2-two-1-1.jsonl` seq 45), and is not read as what remains: the queue removes each pending steer at its own `started`, as today. After `delivering` the move comes 0.8 to 1.0 s later, and the cut turn's end right after it.

*Who may call it, and on what.* On this Mac the window calls it for its own session, and only while the engine host that serves it has this build's wire: the port answers a failure before it invokes anything otherwise (decision 30; D). The check that matters is where the act runs: the queue acts only on a uuid that is one of this session's own pending steers, by its own list, so a call can hasten nothing but a message that session already holds, and can name no other session's. What a caller on another machine can read is unchanged: the queue's state through `session.queue.list`, and the events.

Sending now a message that waits after the turn, or one a Stop held, is today's `session.queue.promote` with `priority: 'now'`, unchanged. ### C. The command's row (M1)

A call the engine moved to the background reads **"Moved `<name>` to the background after 13s"**, and its row stays running until its task's end is heard, whether or not its turn has closed, and only while the process that started it lives: at that process's exit, a moved call whose task's end was not heard reads stopped under the same words (decision 33).
