# U9 · Sessions on other machines

> **A child of the UX wave.** Root: `docs/doperpowers/specs/2026-11-12-ux-wave-design.md`. Read it first: it holds the wave's purpose, its laws (law 1 to law 8), the contracts UC-1 to UC-15, the ordering and the record. This file is this child's section of that document. Where this file and a contract's statement in the root differ, the root binds; report the difference. **Grain:** plan. Items are named by their ids in the register, `docs/doperpowers/evidence/ux-wave-2026-11/register.md`.

- **Purpose:** A session on another machine stops behaving worse than one on this Mac in the places where it does today. Its tab says "Connecting" rather than "offline" while the machine link comes up, keeps the transcript on screen and the reader's place through every reconnect, and says when it is loading. Its row reads Working when its turn starts, not ten seconds later. From this Mac the owner can fork it (from its menu, from an answer, from `/fork`), rename it, and archive and unarchive it, with no Touch ID on a machine they own; a Stop that cannot reach the machine says so; the Touch ID sheet names the machine by its name. One contract (UC-7) settles, for every act on a session, whether it works on another machine or says why not, so the next act somebody adds cannot fail silently there.

  - **Owns UC-7 · An act on a session works on another machine, or says why not.** The content follows.

  **UC-7.1 The rule.** Every act a person can take on a session is in exactly one of three states for a session on another machine: it works, carried by an offer; main answers it itself; or it is declared unavailable, and then its control is drawn disabled with one sentence. No control is drawn enabled where the act cannot work now, and no act ends without either its effect or one sentence that names the reason. Disabling a control is never the authorisation: the other machine decides every request again (remote layer design §5.5).

  **UC-7.2 The shape of one offer.** An offer is these facts, and a child that asks for one states them:

  | fact | where it lives | what it says |
  |---|---|---|
  | the command | `src/shared/ipc/schema/` | its name and its input as it crosses the link; a new field on an offered command counts as a new offer |
  | the grade | `src/shared/ipc/grades/` | what the caller must hold: a rank on the session (`view` < `converse` < `approve` < `manage`) or a right on the machine (`spawn`, `shell`, `machine-admin`) |
  | the row | `OFFERS` in `src/endpoint/offers.ts` | a `call` (a read) or an `act`; who runs it (`engine-host` or `machine`); how its session is found (`appSessionId`, `each-session`, …); optionally a narrower input (`accepts`) and `owners` (the machine's owners alone) |
  | the device's half | `src/main/lane-b/remote.ts` | for an act, one of two sets. `ENGINE_HOST_ACTS`: sealed for the session's live launch, so a tab must hold the session, and a released session is resumed first. `MACHINE_ACTS`: placed by its arguments, so it works with no tab open and resumes nothing |
  | the capability | `ENDPOINT_CAPABILITIES` in `src/endpoint/capabilities.ts`, and `REMOTE_NEEDS` beside the declared set | the name an endpoint lists in its handshake once it serves the offer; the device sends the offer only to a machine that lists it, and the renderer reads the machine's list from the roster before drawing the control. `RL2_PROTOCOL.minor` moves by one |
  | the words | `src/shared/ipc/remote-unavailable.ts` | what the control shows when the act cannot work, and what a refusal says (UC-7.5) |

  A request a machine makes of a device (show a pane) is not an offer. It travels the other way, on the client port (`src/endpoint/client-port.ts`, behind `rl2.client-port`), to the device that sent the turn's input, and the device answers once. U12 adds its kind there.

  Fork is one act. Its input is today's: `{appSessionId, target, message, rewindTo}`. `rewindTo` is the id of one of the person's prompts, and the fork holds the conversation before that prompt; `null` forks the whole conversation. `message`, when given, is sent as the fork's first message. The renderer's one entry is `forkSession` in `src/renderer/src/sessions/record-ops.ts`; it keeps today's signature, and U3 and U4 call it with their arguments and decide nothing else. The fork's tab opens on every call, on every route: no caller chooses, and the act takes no option about the tab. This was ruled on 2026-11-13 with U4a's execution document: U4's A20 stands, and `/fork` opens the fork in a new tab, as the menu's Fork does. Before that ruling this paragraph left the tab to the caller, with the engine's own `/fork [prompt]` in mind ("Copy this conversation into a new background session and keep working here", `~/claude-code-bundle/2.1.322/cli.pretty.js:430960`), and U9's execution document gave `forkSession` an option for it; the option was removed and not kept unused, since no caller needed it once `/fork` opens the tab. "Fork from here" on an answer passes the first prompt after that answer, or `null` when none follows, which is Claude Desktop's rule (`readable/app-5.1231.2/web/app/v1/c96d752d0-CjYhuD9j.js:39351`, called from `forkAfterAssistantTurn` at `:142000`). On one of the person's own prompts Claude Desktop forks before that prompt and puts its text in the fork's composer (`:128270`); MAWS's edit-and-fork is that case, and sends the edit.

  A fork is recorded with no starter of its own, as a fork made at the machine is, so it belongs to the machine's owners. That is why only they may fork over the link in this wave: a member who merely holds a `spawn` grant would get a fork they do not own, because the proof that makes a starter the owner is tied to the name `session.spawn` (`SPAWN_ACTIONS` in `apps/org-protocol/src/attest.ts`, read by other machines and by sminos).

  The fork's ceiling is checked where the fork is made. A comparison at the endpoint alone cannot be bound to the record that is forked: the endpoint's decision follows a record's owners, access and shares, not its permission mode, and the services' fork reads its source again inside its own serialized operation and copies the requested mode from that reading. So the check is made inside the services' fork, between that reading and the allocation of the fork, on the same record the fork copies its mode from. Over the ceiling nothing is allocated and nothing launches, and the act fails with `ceiling` and the mode's name. The ceiling is read in that step from the endpoint's policy as it stands then, and does not ride the command's input. A fork made in this Mac's own window is not held to the ceiling, as today. Two tests pin it: with the source's mode raised above the ceiling between the endpoint's decision and the allocation, the fork fails and nothing is written; with it raised right after the allocation, the fork launches with the mode that was judged.

  Unarchive and Rename answer a record, and a rule of P5's stood in their way. The services' handler of `session.update` refused every request made under a reader (P5 O8's ruling: its answer is the whole record, which must never go to a reader), and the endpoint runs every act from another device under its caller's reader, an owner's included. The command also had no rule at the endpoint, where it is graded by effect; the row comes with one that answers `manage` for every input, and the row's `accepts` narrows it to an unarchive. A row alone would have left Unarchive failing on the machine. The rule now, as ruled on 2026-11-12 by the session that keeps master, whose ruling it amends: an unarchive is the one `session.update` a reader may make, under four conditions. (1) The handler itself checks that the patch's own keys are exactly `archived` and that its value is `false`, whatever the endpoint's row admitted. (2) It keeps refusing every other patch from a reader, `{archived: true}` included: Archive crosses as `session.archive`, which also ends the process. (3) The answer, and the answer of `session.rename` under a reader, is the record as `session.get` answers that reader, read after the transaction that applied the change, and never the value the change itself returns, which is the owners' reading. `session.get` is already offered over the link, so the answer is no wider than a read that reader may make. (4) The amendment is written at two places of `docs/doperpowers/plans/2026-10-28-p5-o8-org-interface.md`: at the line that opens "What a remote reader update answers", and as a dated sub-item of its Decision Log entry "M6a Task 1's fork". The comment line in that plan's Interfaces §P that repeats the old rule gets a pointer to the amendment (Rulings reversed).

  The machine's operation ledger stores a claimed act's answer as the handler gave it, and returns it again for a duplicate of the act (the same operation id) and for a query. A query is answered only to the member who sent the operation, and only while that member still holds `view` on its target. With condition (3), the answer stored for a member who is not one of the machine's owners is that member's own reading, so a replay gives them nothing wider than they were first answered. The tests read three answers (the first, a duplicate and a query), as an owner of the machine and as a member who owns the session without owning the machine. One case remains: a member who was one of the machine's owners when the act ran, is one no longer, still holds `view`, and replays the answer given to them as an owner. The session that keeps master, whose rule it is, ruled on 2026-11-12 that this is residue and not a defect of this child. The bytes replayed are the ones that member already received while an owner, and the operation ledger has the same property for every answer it stores. The answer is not projected again at replay, and nothing in the code changes for the case. One row in `docs/tech-debt-tracker.md` that names the op ledger (`src/services/machine-ops.ts`, `src/shared/host/op-ledger.ts`) records it; U9's last milestone writes it.

  *Not on this route, with the reason.* A terminal session on another machine gets none of Fork, Archive and Rename: on this Mac a terminal session is its terminal's (its menu has only the opens, and an archive answers "held"), and a link act finds its session by a MAWS session id, which a terminal session on another machine does not have on this side.
