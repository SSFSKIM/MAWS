The desk / Request cards / 04 View in dark

A request is answered where it appears in the chat, or says why it can't be.

Recommendation: A, the card as the session's message. A prompt or a question is something a session says to you, so it is drawn with the same name, machine and time as everything else that session says, and its card is the body of that message. That keeps three pending requests from three sessions readable as three voices instead of three alarms. It also gives every later state a place to land: the same slot shrinks to one line once the request settles. B is louder and fits a channel where requests are rare. C is right for a direct channel, where only one session can be waiting and the card can take the composer's place as it does in MAWS. In the group chat, though, C stacks every session's request into one tray and moves each one away from the moment it was asked.

Every state below is drawn in A. Each one follows MAWS's request card: the kind line in its tone (◐ amber for approval, ? amber for a blocking question, ? blue for a question that does not block), a bounded preview, numbered option rows, and CardButtons (secondary has a strong border, primary is drawn in the text colour with the background colour for its label). A request the web cannot answer keeps its content and swaps its controls for one sentence saying where it can be answered.

Why A over C here, and C in a direct channel

The group chat is many sessions at once. Docking pulls every request out of the moment it was raised and queues them behind a pager in arrival order, which is not the order you want to answer them in. A leaves each request where it was asked and lets you take them in any order.

Where C belongs

A direct channel has one session and, at most, one request waiting from its main loop. There the docked card is the right call, as in MAWS: the composer is paused anyway while the session waits, and the card stands in its place. The card body is the same component in both places.

Keyboard, in A

The newest pending card in view takes Enter and Esc only after it is focused (a click, or ⌘⇧J to jump to the next request). A group chat must never answer a request because you pressed Enter while typing a message.

What decides which state a card is in

The hub, never the browser's own reckoning. Pending, terminal only, settled (with its winner), and an RPC's forwarded, done, failed or uncertain all arrive as the hub's frames. The card draws the latest one, so two browsers looking at the same request always agree.

The tone ladder

Amber means waiting on you or not yet known. Blue means a question that does not block. Red marks a refusal, yours or mini's: a Deny, or mini refusing what you sent. Green appears only on a settled Allow or answer. Each state also has words, so none rests on colour alone.
