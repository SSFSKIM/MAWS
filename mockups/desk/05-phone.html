The desk / The phone / 05 View in dark

On the phone, the desk is one loop: see what needs you, answer it.

Laptop closed, the phone buzzes: maws-45 has a question. The notification only says so. One tap opens the installed app at the question's card, the card draws once its code checks out, and the answer moves the session on mini. Everything on the phone serves that loop: the grid with its waiting counts, a project's channels, the group chat, a direct channel, and request cards answered in place. A session's depth (the live transcript, subagents, the diff) stays on a computer.

Recommendations. The phone is a stack, not a layout: Projects → a project → a channel, one full screen each, and every pushed screen carries its own back control, since an installed app has no browser back button. 3: A, a project opens on its channel list, so the first thing a project says on the phone is who is waiting on you. 5: A, a direct channel keeps Steer, Send and Interrupt in reach and puts Model and Compact behind More. The grid keeps card B in two columns. A notification opens the session's direct channel with the card lit, and back from there walks up the stack (channel → MAWS → Projects), never out of the app.

For you to decide (each one is drawn the way I recommend; the reasons are at the end): whether Keep unlocked on this device starts checked in the installed app (8), whether a notification opens the direct channel or the group chat (6), whether the app icon carries the org's waiting count (1), and whether starting a session stays off the phone (notes).

What the phone leaves out, and what is yours to decide

Session depth stays on a computer

The live transcript, the subagent tree, the diff and session info (cwd, worktree, model, context, cost) need width and long reading, and they are not part of the loop of seeing what needs you and answering it. The phone does not draw dead doors to them. The working record is a quiet line ending “on a computer”, and the ⋯ sheet says it once. If depth arrives on the phone later, that line is where it opens from.

Notifications carry no actions and no text

The service worker holds no org key, so it can neither check what the hub sent nor sign an answer. Allow, Deny or a reply on the notification would mean putting the key in the service worker, a §6 decision not taken. Quoting a message would show the hub’s unchecked words on a locked screen. A notification is a pointer; the card, checked, is where you decide.

Also left out

Starting a session (+ Session asks for a machine, a checkout, a worktree and a brief), enrolling a machine (which happens on the Mac anyway), managing the org key’s methods, the machines dialog, and keyboard shortcuts. This device lists them under “On a computer”, so their absence reads as a choice, not a gap.

For you to decide

  1. Keep unlocked on this device, checked by default in the installed app. Recommended. Without it, iOS closing the app means an unlock before most notifications. The cost is that the key stays on the phone until you lock it under This device. (8)
  2. Where a notification lands: the session’s direct channel. Recommended over the group chat: the card arrives with that session’s own lead-up and none of the other sessions’ traffic, and back goes to the project’s list. The group chat is the better landing if you would rather clear every waiting request in a project in one scroll. (6)
  3. The app icon’s badge shows the org’s waiting count. Recommended: the home screen then answers the grid’s question unopened. It needs the count in every push payload, which the hub already has. (1)
  4. Starting a session stays off the phone in phase 1. Recommended: it is a form, not an answer. If “start a session from the train” is a real scene for you, it is a small sheet away and I would draw it. (notes)

For the implementer

Manifest display: standalone; viewport-fit=cover; apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style: black-translucent, with the top bar padded by env(safe-area-inset-top) and the composer by env(safe-area-inset-bottom). Keep every input at 16 px or larger. Hold the composer above the keyboard with visualViewport. Deep links need a history built under them, or back closes the app. “Show safe areas” at the top of this page draws the 47 px and 34 px insets on every frame.
