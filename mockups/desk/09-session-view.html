The desk / The session view / 09 View in dark

The session view: what the transcript does not know yet, and what runs beside it.

On a desktop browser whose machine is online, a direct channel's center is MAWS's report over the session's own transcript (§9.1): <message> spans as Message cards, one “Working record” row per marked turn, a Report · Transcript toggle. Around it, four surfaces are the desk's own and are this document's subject: the tail row's delivery line for a send the transcript has not shown yet, the request band above the composer, the end marker of an ended or retired session, and the jobs view in the column. Where a surface has a real choice, two alternatives stand side by side, named, with the owner's choice of 2026-10-17 marked.

0 The session view, assembled
1 The tail row's delivery line · one send in each state

A send the transcript has not shown yet is a tail row in MAWS's held-prompt shape (heldRow). It stays, whatever its delivery says, until its own framed user row arrives, then gives way to it. The choice is where the delivery line sits. Both columns hold the same six sends, oldest first: a Send queued for idle, one delivered whose row has not arrived, one recorded for a session not running, one refused by a read-only machine, one that failed with the bridge's reason, and a Steer whose RPC never answered.

queuedA Send logged, no outcome yet: the daemon delivers it when the session is idle (§4's Send). An outbox item not yet in the log reads “Sending…”, as DC5's stream says. deliveredDX4 delivered: in the session's queue. Waiting for its framed row; if the session ends first it stays, “Delivered · not seen in the transcript”, ahead of the end marker (3A). recordedThe session was not running. A later delivered for the same key, after Resume, replaces it. refused-read-onlyThe machine's policy runs nothing from outside it. Final. failedWith the reason the machine gave; a failed after delivered (the engine's later drop) replaces it. uncertainA push or a Steer RPC whose frame was taken and never answered: neither delivered nor not.
2 The request band · a permission and a question

The hub's pending requests for the session, as DC5's request cards, answerable in place; a card leaves when its request settles. The band is not in the scroll, so a request the turn is blocked on can never sit folded inside a closed working record (§9.1's reason for rejecting request_slot rows). The choice is what happens when many are waiting: the band, which keeps the oldest open and folds the rest to one line each, or the cards inline at the transcript's tail.

3 The end marker · ended, and retired · Chosen (owner, 2026-10-17) as drawn

One row at the tail of an ended or retired session's view, in MAWS's boundary shape (a rule, its words, a rule), with what the channel's header offers: Resume, or who retired it. The history above stays pageable to the first line; whether the session runs is read from presence, never from the subscription. The header keeps its own Resume, so the marker's is a second door at the place the eye ends.

4 The jobs view · shells, a monitor, the MCP names

A tab beside Subagents, Diff and Info, drawn with MAWS's ShellRow and MonitorRow under MAWS's Background work headings, over the widened subagents body's jobs and mcp. A shell's Output asks the machine for the file's tail (output(taskId, tail)) and draws it as plain text. The choice is where the output opens in a 380 px column: under its row, or as the column's whole pane.

Stop is MAWS's row as it stands: ShellRow and MonitorRow draw it on every running task. Stopping from the web is optional in §9.4 (an RPC through the bridge's TaskStop); the owner chose (2026-10-17) to draw it disabled, its reason on hover, until that RPC exists.
