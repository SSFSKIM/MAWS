# Layers

Design record for the round 2 synthesis `layers` (order 0, first in the gallery). The contract is `mockups/lab/BRIEF.md` and
`mockups/lab/ROUND2.md`. Values live in `layers.css` (tokens at the top), `colour.ts` (everything a colouring
settles), `paint.ts` and `paper.ts` (the two field painters); this file says what each part was lifted from, what
happened where two parts met, what the idea breaks and what that costs.

The owner chose this synthesis as the style of the redesign and, in the same message, dropped its colour. Colour is
now a switch, and its record is section 12. Five calls about the result were still the owner's, and each of them is
a switch too: section 13. Four of them are answered and their answers are the design at rest, in the system face:
section 15. Paper has four warmer neighbours (section 14), of which the owner chose Warm paper, and the painted form
has a fourth position with no drawing at all, Open's plain ground (section 16). Then the smaller parts of the
session went to a board of alternatives (`../choices/`), the owner took its suggested set, and those forms are the
design now: section 17, which is read over sections 2, 3 and 5. Last, the product's other scene and the second
surface of the first were drawn in the same design, the Overview and a file's page: section 18, whose file page
the owner then asked to be a card of glass (18.10). Code is coloured (section 19), and the tab row has a rule for more
tabs than fit (section 20). The pane's last two destinations, Artifacts and Customize, are drawn in section 21, which
lists every word and row they no longer show at rest for the owner to confirm. Sections 0 to 11 are the experiment as it was built, under what is now
the switch's last position (Orange); where they name the orange, read "the colouring's accent".

## 0. Thesis

The owner's suggestion taken all the way: "use tool use parts, tool use blocks and more, to liquid glass too." One
colour field runs under the whole window. Prose is read directly on it. Every block of the transcript (an opened
tool run, a diff, a running command, the person's own message) is a piece of glass standing over the field, and so
is every control around it: the pane, the tab capsule, the composer, the waiting capsule, and four separate modules
in place of a side column.

It is not a sheet set into a field (that is `sheet`), not one ground with light gathered under the controls (that is
`open`), and not a card layout painted to look translucent: the blocks are drawn by the glass runtime, with a lens,
over a plane that stays still while they move.

If a decision is ever ambiguous, choose the option that keeps the words readable over the one that shows more glass.

## 3. Where parts met

1. **Field's sheet against glass blocks.** Field read everything on an opaque sheet; the assignment removes it. The
   field gave way: it is regraded so that the main space sits at about a third of the level under the pane. Prose
   holds 10:1 or better on it at every strength and ground (section 7).
2. **Quiet's ringed cards against glass.** A ring on glass is an authored border beside the material's own edge.
   The ring gave way.
3. **Instrument's "content slides under the composer" against "blocks are glass".** A glass block under a glass bar
   is glass on glass. Instrument's signature gave way: content thins out before it reaches a bar (section 5).
4. **Quiet's lighter hover fill against light glass.** Glass this size is nearly white in the light scheme
   (0.95 and up), so a lighter fill is light on light. Hover became the ink at 5 %; what is current stays Field's
   white chip with a contact shadow. Dark takes the lighter pair for both.
5. **The runtime's shadow against thirteen surfaces close together.** The runtime clips each surface's exterior
   shadow to its sampling padding. Under a 680 px card or four modules 12 px apart the shadow is wider than the
   padding and ended in a visible seam beside every surface. The runtime's shadow gave way on both roots
   (`materialProfile`, the six occlusion anchors at zero); shadows are drawn in CSS, on the block for content and on
   a pseudo-element behind a host's content for controls, and are removed in the receded pose.
6. **The library's host model against a transcript in flow.** A glass host is portalled into its root's plane and
   cannot follow a flow. So a block stays an ordinary element in the transcript, and `glass.tsx` draws one
   decorative surface beneath it from its measured box (the same for the modules). The block's words are real
   text in reading order, and the Veil and Opaque materials reuse the same markup. Because the block's own corner
   clips its fills and casts its shadow, these surfaces use the circular corner profile, which CSS matches
   exactly; the pane, the bars and the composer keep the continuous corner.
7. **Quiet's hover card against the library's popover.** Quiet's card re-anchors from row to row in 200 ms; a
   morph does not follow an anchor. The card, the row menu, the usage and model popovers and the waiting list are
   one CSS platter (a backdrop blur and a tint). Round 1 found the two hard to tell apart at this size.
8. **Two state vocabularies.** Instrument's dots and Quiet's glyphs. Quiet's won everywhere.
9. **Instrument's margin help against Quiet's.** The same idea in both; Quiet's geometry, Instrument's timing.

## 4. Material: the record

```
register: spatial, extended to content on the owner's word. Glass holds the transcript's blocks, which the
  guideline's first law forbids ("Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer"); section 8 lists what breaks.
plane: `xl-plane`, one viewport-fixed canvas painted once per size, scheme, ground and strength, supplied to both
  roots as a texture with `live: false` (read once per painting, never per frame). Broad frequency: eleven radial
  masses, seven at the edges and four across the main space. Fine frequency: 1 px contour lines, the level sets of
  the same masses, painted into the same canvas. Nothing in it moves or means anything.
roots: two. The window root is viewport-attached and holds everything that stays put. The transcript root is
  attached to the scrolling column (`attachment="container"`), so the compositor carries the blocks' glass with
  their content; in a viewport root it trails the scroll by a frame.
groups, all on the texture: xl-pane; xl-lead (overlay plane; the sidebar toggle, present only while the pane is
  away); xl-tabs; xl-composer; xl-side (the waiting capsule, the side toggle and the four modules: neighbours,
  one tint among plain members); xl-blocks (transcript root); one more per open select menu.
family: thickness 8 everywhere. Pane 272 wide, r 20. Modules 296 wide, r 20. Blocks r 18, the message r 22.
  Composer r 26. Bars are capsules at span 36. Anchor: the window corner, at an 8 px margin. Rows inside a pane
  or module are r 12 (20 minus 8); fills inside a capsule are r 15 (18 minus 3).
tint: one seed, the colouring's accent (#fd780f under Orange), on the waiting capsule. Glyphs on it are the
  colouring's authored ink (#2a1300 under Orange); receded, the tint loses its chroma and the glyphs return to
  the runtime's ink. The runtime draws the seed itself in both schemes (drawn L, C and hue within 0.01, 0.005 and
  1 degree of the seed for every colouring), so a seed is chosen as a fill is: clear of the levels where neither
  ink holds.
ink: on a host, the runtime's primary on children, with two authored levels under it (86 % and 76 % light, 92 %
  and 86 % dark). On a block or a module the words are not inside the host, so the page's own ink is used and
  every line is measured (section 7).
room: derived, never typed. `useGlassSamplingGap` answers 62 px for the pane beside a bar; the layout reads it as
  `--xl-room` and keeps the largest value seen. It is spent between the pane and the tab capsule and between
  the tab capsule and the side column. At 1280 wide the composer comes within 32 px of the modules; the runtime
  reported nothing there, and nothing was done about it.
motion: section 9.
scheme and pose: the roots' scheme follows the lab's; window activation is left on auto (`pose=` pins it).
```

What the runtime reported (1440 by 900, `dev=1`):

- Every group on every route, both schemes: renderer `webgpu`, refraction `true`, analysis `exact`, health `ok`.
- One diagnostic, on every session route with glass blocks, by design: `backdrop-root-at-container`. The scroll
  edge is a mask on the scroller, which re-roots the backdrop around the attached root, so its glass cannot
  sample the page behind the column. It samples the field texture instead, so nothing is lost.
- Nothing else at rest, with either pane away, at 1280 by 800, after a round trip of the side column, or with a
  tool run, the row card, the waiting list or a select open. Three findings were met and removed on the way: the
  tab capsule nearer the pane than the room (`group-proxy-overlap`), a module group with its merge distance forced
  to zero (`merge-distance-below-padding`), and dematerialised modules left registered where the composer came to
  stand (`same-plane-overlap`).
- CSS tier (`tier=css`), looked at on purpose in both schemes: the same layout with frost and no lens.
- Receded pose (`pose=inactive`), looked at and measured: bodies grey, the tint drains, CSS shadows go.
- Forced colours: Canvas panels with CanvasText borders, the field hidden, selection as a Highlight outline.

## 5. The scroll rule

- The field is fixed to the window. Blocks and prose travel over it.
- The transcript's scroller carries one mask. From each bar's measured far edge (`useGlassBarInset`) plus 4 px,
  content rises from nothing to full strength across 32 px. So nothing is under the tab capsule or the composer at
  any scroll position: glass never passes under glass, and the bars' own backdrop is always the field.
- The mask thins a block's glass with its words, because the transcript root's planes are inside the scroller.
- At rest (a live session is read from its end) the last block clears the composer by the mask's depth.
- The pane and the modules are outside the reading column, so no block can reach them. Between the column and
  either of them there is at least 32 px at 1280 wide and 88 px at 1440.
- A transient platter (the row card, the waiting list) may cover a block. It is a CSS platter at 74 % or more, so
  it occludes what is under it; it does not show glass through glass.

## 8. What the idea breaks, and what it costs

Laws of the glass guideline broken on purpose:

- **Content is not glass.** The transcript's blocks are. This is the experiment.
- **One to three windows.** At rest 13 surfaces are drawn: the pane, four modules, four blocks and four controls
  (14 are registered, counting the sidebar toggle that waits absent while the pane is there), and one more for
  each tool run that is open.
- **No shadow authored where the runtime draws one.** Every exterior shadow is CSS (section 3, item 5). It no
  longer follows the material's measured law: it is a fixed recipe per span, it is alpha black-brown and not
  multiplicative, and it leaves in the receded pose by a rule of the page.
- **Glass materialises, never fades.** The CSS platters scale and fade. The library surfaces do materialise.
- **Prose is not on glass, but lists are.** A diff and a list of tool calls are collections read in place.
- **No native comparison** was made. The nearest Apple surface is a visionOS window with ornaments; nothing Apple
  ships puts a scrolling column of glass cards over a still environment.

Costs:

- **Legibility.** Nothing measures under its floor, but the margin is thin exactly where content is densest: the
  12 and 13 px quiet lines on dark glass sit at 4.9 to 5.0 at Vivid on `#1f1f1f`, and they depend on the field
  staying graded. A field with a bright mass in the main space would put dark glass bodies toward the dead band. The
  opaque material holds 5.5 or better with no dependence on the field.
- **Calm.** A page of prose with five cards has five more edges, rims and shadows than Quiet's ringed cards had,
  and in the light scheme a block is nearly white (0.95 and up): it reads as a frosted white card with a soft
  shadow, and the glass shows only at its rim and while it moves. The dark scheme is where the blocks read as
  glass. Flip Blocks to Opaque to see how little is lost at rest.
- **Performance.** Two WebGPU roots, each with its own device and four window-sized canvases (eight canvases of
  2880 by 1800 at 2x, beside the field). The field is imported once per painting, not per frame. A layer
  re-reads every block's box on a resize and for up to 0.9 s of frames after anything that moves blocks. Scrolling
  the transcript end to end held the main thread's frame interval (240 frames in 4 s, median 16.7 ms, none over
  20 ms) with glass, veil and opaque alike, in headless Chrome on a loaded machine; GPU time was not measured and
  is where the cost would be. With Veil or Opaque the second root is not mounted at all.
- **Scale.** The lab's transcript is ten items. A real one is virtualised and hundreds of items long; glass
  surfaces would be registered only for rows in view, and a block entering during a fast scroll can be cut short
  for a frame (the library's stated limit for attached roots).

## 9. Motion

- Layout (the pane, the main space, the composer, the tabs): 460 ms on Field's curve, `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)`.
  The window root is asked to re-measure on its own frames while a transition runs.
- The side column: the modules leave one after another (30 ms apart), each dematerialising as its content slides
  36 px and fades; only then does the main space take the room (320 ms later). Arriving is the reverse: the room
  is made, then the modules materialise 55 ms apart.
- A tool run opens by height over 300 ms on Quiet's curve; its glass is absent until 10 px of card show, then
  materialises and grows with it.
- Quiet's: the slot exchange (130 ms fade, the entering mark scales from 0.8), popovers and the row card (210 ms
  in from the anchor's corner, 130 ms out; the card re-anchors in 200 ms), press (90 ms scale).
- Instrument's: the switch knob and the segmented thumb on a light spring (420 to 440 ms), pills (220 ms).
- The runtime's: press on the bars (light and compression), the select platter, presence.
- At rest only a running mark moves (a 1.8 s breath, one spinner). Under Reduce Motion every page transition is
  1 ms and the runtime's springs lose their bounce.

## 11. Rejected

- Settings sections as glass cards. The owner chose Quiet's unframed rows; a form is not a transcript block.

## 12. Colour

Added 2026-11-14, after the owner chose Layers as the style and dropped its colour ("for color I'll actually abandon
the strong orange #FD780F"). Colour is now the first knob. Sections 0 to 11 describe the experiment as it was built,
under what is now the last position, Orange; where they name a hue, read "the colouring's".

### 12.1 What a colouring is

In Layers colour does three jobs, and a position of the switch settles all three together, in both schemes:

1. **The field**: what is painted on the canvas and seen through every pane, block and module.
2. **The tint glass picks up from it**: the library carries the field's hue into each body by itself; the page's
   part is the ink's temperature and the tint under its CSS shadows (`--xl-shade-rgb`), because a brown shadow in a
   blue room reads as dirt.
3. **The mark that means "this session needs you"**: the waiting capsule's seed, the glyphs on waiting rows and
   tabs, Approve, the send button when there is something to send, and keyboard focus.

Each is one record in `colour.ts`: a field per scheme, six tokens per scheme (ink, shadow tint, accent, ink on the
accent, the accent as a stroke, focus), the mark's form, and optionally its own ground. Nothing else in the
experiment names a hue. The mark has two forms. `stroke` is Quiet's glyph drawn in the mark colour, as before.
`badge` is the same glyph reversed out of a 20 px disc of the accent (18 px in a tab), for an accent that cannot be
a 15 px stroke: the ink itself, which would be one more grey mark among the row's own. Inside the capsule a badge
goes back to a plain glyph, since the capsule is already the filled thing. In every form the state is a shape first
(a hand, a question mark, a list); no colouring says it by hue alone.

The owner's answers, in order, shaped the list: "Show me a range", "Softer warm", "A different hue"; then "also want
to try using full white and full black and other charecteristic color. if that could work"; then a picture, with
"How about using imagery a bit like this? To answer about colour". The picture is the answer, so it is the first
and default position and the others are the comparison set behind it.

### 12.2 Paper

**Source.** The owner's picture is outside the repository and nothing of it is in here; this is what it shows, in
words. A still, warm, almost colourless scene: a matte ivory ground; one sheet of paper that lifts in a slow curve
and, along the same curve, becomes clear glass; the paper's lit crest near the ground's own level and its shaded
underside a clear step below; one soft warm shadow on the floor; the glass nearly as light as the ground and
declared by a thin bright rim, a faint reflection inside it and that shadow, not by any tint; near-black ink; one
word in a light, high-contrast serif.

**What was taken from it.** Colour stops being a hue and becomes four materials: paper, glass, shadow, ink. And the
field stops being a wash and becomes a picture of that kind, painted by `paper.ts` on the same canvas the other
fields use, so both glass roots refract it like any field.

**The ground is the picture's ivory, and that is what makes the glass read.** `#e8e1db` (level 0.89), a shade
deeper and greyer than the app's `#faf9f5` (0.98). The library's light glass draws at 0.90 to 0.99 at these sizes.
On `#faf9f5` nothing separates the two, which is round 1's finding that glass over a pale flat ground is close to
invisible. On the ivory every pane, block and module is the lighter thing, with its rim and a warm shadow under it:

from the field under it in OKLab (1.13 to 1.28:1; blocks and modules 0.05 to 0.06), against 0.03 to 0.05 for the
blocks and modules under Orange and 0.002 to 0.006 on pure white. It is a modest gain and a real one, and it is
linear: every 0.01 the ground comes down is 0.01 more. Paper therefore brings its ground and the Ground knob is not
consulted (dark `#181614`).

### 12.4 Full white and full black: does it work?

The owner's question was honest ("if that could work"), so here is a plain answer for the best version found.

**What a field cannot do on a pure ground, the colour has to do.** On `#ffffff` light glass (0.92 to 1.00) has
nothing to differ from, so a room-wide wash would have to be strong to show a pane, and then it is no longer full
white. Cobalt therefore paints colour only under the glass (the pane, the modules, the composer, the tab capsule)
and leaves the reading column pure. The colour is blue for three reasons that are not taste: on white, blue is the
one hue that reads as shade and so as depth, where a pink or a yellow reads as a stain; saturated blue has the
lowest luminance of any hue, so dark glass over it stays far below the level where text fails (bodies 0.30 and
down); and it is the complement of the orange that was dropped. On `#000000` two broad floors of white at 5 % go
under the glass first (round 1's remedy for faint colour turning into a wash), and the blue is then let through
at full chroma on purpose: the pane is a slab of blue glass.

**Verdict.** In dark it works, and is the most striking position of the eight: prose on true black, glass drawn
by its rim and its lifted body, the pane lit blue. In light it half works: the pane, the modules and the composer
read as blue-lit glass, but a transcript block on pure white is a white card and its shadow, the experiment's own
idea (blocks of glass) is invisible exactly where it lives, and the distance from field to body there measured
0.002 to 0.006 in OKLab against 0.05 under Paper. What it costs besides: white ink on black for hours (the ink is
held at `rgb(243 244 247)`, not white, for that reason); a product that is no longer warm anywhere; and blue on a
control competing with every link and selection convention.

**The warm answer on pure grounds is not a field at all.** A yellow was built and dropped (12.5): as light under
glass on white it is sallow. The warm version of "full white and full black" is marks only, and the switch already
has it: Paper's picture is hueless, so the idea carries over; the Ground knob at `#ffffff · #000000` under any of
the four warm colourings shows the rest.

### 12.5 Rejected

- **Sun**: pure grounds with a yellow (`oklch(0.88 0.18 97)`). Built twice. As light under glass on white it
  yellows the panes without reading as sunlight; as marks only over neutral shade it is Paper with a louder

- **Mono**: the owner's grounds with light and shade and no hue, and an amber mark. Built; it was the strongest

- **A violet mark** for the hueless and many-hued rooms: it would have been chosen because it was the one hue not

### 12.7 What the colour work showed to be fragile

1. **In light, a colouring barely reaches the glass.** Light glass draws at 0.92 to 1.00 whatever is under it, so
   the field shows between surfaces and faintly through the pane, and a block is a white card. Changing the hue
   changes the room, not the blocks. The only thing that made light glass read better was Paper's lower ground.
2. **The mark is a fill, and fills have a forbidden band.** The runtime draws the capsule's seed as given, in both
   schemes. A seed between about 0.55 and 0.70 in lightness takes neither ink at 4.5. The orange sat just above
   it. Rose and Cobalt needed a different fill per scheme, which the single `tint="#fd780f"` could not express.
3. **A pale accent cannot be a stroke.** Any accent lighter than about 0.6 has to be deepened for a 15 px glyph
   on light glass, and a deepened yellow or apricot is bronze: the hue's identity is gone in exactly the scheme
   where it is needed. Hence the second form, the badge.
4. **The warm red is taken.** The removed-line colour sits where a coral, a vermilion or a seal would go, so the
   natural warm alternatives to orange are closed unless that red moves.
5. **Shadows, contour ink and three inks were literals.** The CSS shadows were brown, which under a blue or
   hueless field reads as dirt; they are a token now. The same was true of the contours' ink and of the tooltip
   and solid colours in dark.
6. **The dark pane's margin** (above): 4.53 at the strongest setting under Orange, 4.66 under Honey.
7. **A picture field has a composition, and the layout can move under it.** Paper's form is placed for the
   session: there the column is glass blocks and the form stays under the pane, the composer and the modules. The
   settings page is the weak scene. Its rows are read directly on the field and scroll over the whole of it, so
   the leaf's rim runs behind the segmented controls and the arch's edge can pass through a heading. Every line
   still measures above its floor, and it is still lines behind words. The remedy to try, if the owner keeps the
   picture, is a lower strength on that scene only, or a settings column that stands on a surface.
8. **Five knobs.** The switch added one to an experiment that already had four.

### 14.1 What mixing in orange can and cannot do here

Four facts from sections 7 and 12 decide the values, and one found here.

- **Light glass draws 0.90 to 0.99 whatever is under it** (12.7 item 1). By day the warmth cannot reach a pane or
  a block through the field; it shows in the room between surfaces and as a faint cast in the panes (over Warm
  paper's ground, chroma 0.025, the bodies were drawn at 0.013 to 0.018). A warmer Paper by day is a warmer room,
  not warmer glass.
- **The quietest text bounds the lightness of anything it can cross.** Captions and placeholders are the ink at
  70 % by day and 68 % at night. Composited over a flat colour they hold 4.5:1 only where that colour is 0.82 or
  lighter by day and 0.38 or darker at night, whatever its hue (computed for hues 40 to 55 at chroma 0 to 0.19; it
  moves by less than 0.01). `#fd780f` is 0.72. So the orange as given cannot be a broad area anywhere text can
  scroll, by day or by night; it can be a small mark, a light, or a tint of itself at 0.79 and up.
- **So every warm position keeps Paper's lightness, value for value, and changes only what has a hue.** The
  ground stays 0.914, the sheet's face 0.95, its underside 0.86 to 0.79, the floor shadow 0.53. The readings of
  every line are then Paper's own to within a tenth, and the room is the same room.
- **Dark glass turns the hue under it about ten degrees toward red** (12.6), and a saturated orange under it was
  drawn redder still and lifted the body toward the band where neither ink reads (the `orange` synthesis's
  finding). So the night oranges are kept deep (0.38 and under) and authored a few degrees yellow (hue 56 to 58
  for 49). The turn itself could not be observed here: at this depth no dark body drew more than 0.012 of chroma
  (over a field of 0.02 to 0.03 under its footprint), which is under what the script reads a hue from. At night,
  too, the warmth is in the room and at the foot of the glass, not in its body.
- **Found here: Paper's own night picture put a caption under its floor at one scroll position.** Section 12
  measured the session at rest, from the top, and with things open, never mid-scroll. At `x=mid` in dark the
  caption "Key migration check and Load profile started working" lay across the sheet's lit face (0.49) and its
  icon across the bright edge (0.79): 3.87:1 and 3.47:1 at default strength with the form everywhere. The four
  warm Papers were built with those two night values at 0.36 and 0.66, and Paper has since been given the same
  two (15.2), so at night all five share them and none leaves Paper's lightness anywhere.

## 16. The plain ground

Added 2026-11-14. Asked what Open has that they want to keep, the owner chose only "Plain ground": no drawing in

what is left between the two finalists is mostly the ground, and it is easiest to judge inside one design: the

drawing, as they answered.

### 16.1 What it is

No drawing on any page, New chat included. The ground is one plain colour, the colouring's own, with the paper's
grain (a surface, not a drawing). The only light and shade on it is what gathers where glass stands.

- **Under the glass that stays put**, the gathering is painted into the field, so the glass stands over it and
  takes it up: the pane, the side card or modules, the composer, and more faintly the tab capsule, the waiting
  capsule and the small round controls. The method is Open's (`open/field.ts`): each gathering is its surface's
  own shape, grown by three tenths of its reach and blurred by four tenths, so it ends a hand's width past the
  glass. So are Open's shares of the pane's strength: the side card 0.86, the composer 0.8, the tabs 0.4 (Open
  0.36, where text passes under them; here nothing does). A small control cannot spend a large reach, so reach is
  capped by the control's own span. Each pool deepens toward its foot, so a tall pane does not stand over one
  flat tone.
- **Under a block of the transcript**, which travels over a still field, the gathering travels with it: a second,
  wider shadow under the block in the gathering's own colour (CSS, as every exterior shadow in Layers is). The
  glass of a block does not take it up; it shows around the block's foot.
- **What gathers is the colouring's.** Under Paper by day it is shade, not light: Paper's own floor shadow
  (`rgb(126 104 84)`) at 26 % under the pane. Light glass on ivory is declared by the soft shadow it lays on the
  floor, as in the picture Paper was built from, and a pool lighter than the ground would take away the one thing
  the glass differs from. At night it is the lamp (`rgb(255 224 188)` at 15 %), which dark glass takes into its
  body. A warmer Paper names its own, and here that is where its warmth sits: Warm paper and Clay paper their
  warmer shadow and lamp; Orange light the orange (`rgb(253 136 44)` at 20 % by day, `rgb(255 150 56)` at 17 % at
  night); Orange underside, which has no sheet left to show a reverse, its tangerine (`rgb(253 161 102)` at 28 %)
  and its ember.
- **It follows the glass.** While a pane slides, the side card grows or a scene arrives, the field is repainted
  from where each piece of glass stands on that frame, for a second and a half, and both glass roots read it for
  that long. The pools are soft, so they are painted on a canvas a quarter of the window's size and scaled up. At
  rest nothing is repainted.
- **Strength is capped** at 30 % by day and 22 % at night, whatever Field strength says: under the cap the ground
  beside a control stays where the quietest text holds its floor.

### 16.2 What it costs Layers, and what it gains

  into focus on New chat, the one moment the picture had, is gone with it, and New chat is a composer on a plain

  lived in the composer and would otherwise be gone from the session.

  and one word at a caption's size, in the message's own wash, on the person's side of the column.

  approved plan's steps become the To do section of the side column, which they were not before (two lists of the
  same five lines, one of them not yet true; until then that section holds what the session has done so far). The session's mark in the pane, its tab and the capsule goes back to

## 18. The other scene: the Overview, and a file's page

Added 2026-11-14. The product has two scenes of equal weight, watching over many sessions and going deep in one,
and until now Layers was drawn on three pages that are all the second scene or beside it. The owner settled the
style on the session page (Warm paper, the blurred drawing, the suggested set, the orange disc); a style chosen on
one scene of two is not chosen yet. The pane has had an "Overview" row and the tab strip a "bucket.ts" tab since
round 1, and neither had ever opened. They open now. Both pages are Layers, not a neighbour of it: nothing in this
section adds a material, an ink, a radius or a motion curve, and each page had one question of its own to answer.

What the real app shows on these two surfaces was read from its renderer for the information each carries, not
for its look, and none of its words are here. Its Overview is a board of cards, one per live or unread session,
"needs you" first and then by project; a card carries a state glyph, the title, the project, the branch, an unread
dot, what the session is doing, its last answer, the pending request with its controls, and a footer of context,
cost and running agents. Its file tab is a viewer and an editor with a separate diff tab: unified or split, wrap,
hunk and file stepping, a viewed mark, comments anchored to a line.

### 18.1 What both pages keep

The session page's skeleton, piece for piece: the pane; the tab capsule on the reading column's left edge; one
column, read on the field; and one place for glass beside it, at the top right, where the side column grows from
its button. The same four inks, the same glyph for each state, the same two fill sets, and orange on the disc and
nowhere else. The test was that someone who knows the session page should need no telling.

The rule is the session page's too, read at two new scales. **What is read is on the field. Glass is what can be
opened or answered.** On a session that put prose on the field and the agent's blocks on glass. On the Overview it
puts twenty sessions on the field and one thing on glass: what is the person's to answer. On a file it puts the
whole file on the field and no new glass at all.

### 18.2 The Overview

**Its question: what do glass, the field and the "needs you" mark do when there are twenty sessions and not one?**

*A session is a line on the field.* Its state's glyph, its title, what it is doing or what it ended on, its
project, how long ago: 36 px, in the grammar of the waiting list's own item. Twenty cards of glass would have
made glass mean "a session", which is everything on the page and so nothing; and a board is where the count
grows. So a session that is the agents' business is words on the field, as the agent's prose is.

*The order is the heading.* Without the waiting ones, the lines stand in three runs: finished and not read yet
(the solid dot, the title strong), working (the breathing dot), at rest (no dot, the quiet ink). The glyph column
says which run is which, so the runs have no titles. A session at rest is its title and its age: what it ended on
was read once already and comes back under the pointer. Under the pointer a line's end gives way to where it runs
(branch, machine) and the arrow that opens it, as the age gives way in the waiting list.

*Glass is the one thing that is yours.* The sessions that wait are not lines. They stand at the top right, where
the side column stands on a session, on the page's one piece of glass: the waiting capsule's own list, standing
open. It is the same component (`WaitItems`, which the capsule's popover now also uses), so one item is open at a
time and holds what that session's composer holds: Approve and Deny with the command; the question's chips and a
field; the plan's steps with Approve and Send back. An answer given there is the answer everywhere. Answered, the
item folds away and the session joins the working lines with a line that says what it does now (the command it
was allowed, the plan's first step, the choices made, the person's own words); when nothing waits the glass is
gone and the lines take the room.

*The mark is all in one place.* The capsule is not drawn on this page, because the page is its list. So every
orange disc on the page is inside the one piece of glass (the pane keeps its own), and the lines carry only grey
and ink. "It is your move" reads as a place, not as a scatter of twenty rows to search.

*The tabs are still the window's.* The Overview is a place in the pane, not something that was opened, so it adds
no tab; the capsule shows what is open with none of them current.

Twenty sessions in six projects: three waiting, four unread, seven working, six at rest. At 1280 by 800 all of
them are in the window without scrolling. The column is what the pane and the glass leave (520 px at 1280, 680 at
1440, 744 from 1504), and under 600 px a line gives up its project first, which then waits with the branch under
the pointer.

### 18.3 The Overview's forks

**Lines, against cards** (the "Overview: sessions" switch; `sessions=cards`). Cards is the board the app has
today, drawn in this material: every session a card of glass in a grid as wide as the room, the waiting ones
first with their answers open inside. It is kept on the switch because it is the form a board is expected to
have. Against it: twenty glass surfaces where the chosen form has one, so glass no longer says "yours"; three
answers open at once put a command, two questions, five options, a field and five steps on screen before a
single session is read; and at 1280 by 800 the window holds the three answers, the four unread and the first
three of the working, where the lines show all twenty.

**By state, against by project** (the "Overview: grouped" switch; `by=project`). The pane already lists
sessions by project, open on the same screen. Grouped by project the Overview would be the pane again with one
more line of words; grouped by state it answers what the pane cannot: what finished, what is working, what is
quiet. The project is then a quiet word at the line's end.

Not switches, and why. *Where the glass stands.* On the left it would be the first thing read, but it would sit
against the pane, a second glass column beside the first, and the lines would lose the tabs' edge. Across the top
it would be three columns of unequal height over a list. At the right it is where this design already keeps what
is beside the reading (the side column), under the place the capsule has on every other page, and the orange
finds it without help. *Three answers open at once* was drawn (it is what Cards shows) and refused for the words.

### 18.4 A file's page

**Its question: how does code at length sit in a design whose rule is prose on the field and blocks on glass?**

*The answer below is the first one, and the owner overruled it the same day: the file is a card of glass now
(18.10). What follows is how the page was first drawn, and is still what the Code switch's second position shows
(`code=field`). The paragraphs on the change marks, on pointing at a line and on the session beside the file hold
for both.*

*It is read on the field, as prose is.* The sheet an edit stands on in the transcript is a passage lifted out of
its file, and it needs an edge because prose runs above and below it. Opened as a tab the passage is back in its
file, and a file has nothing to be told apart from: so the glass is gone and the code lies on the field at the
column's own edge, in the transcript's own setting for code (12 on 20). The glass on this page is what it is on a
session's: the tabs, the side column, the composer. A sheet as long as a file would have been a second ground,
and its top and bottom would be under the page's fades for almost all of the reading, which leaves a veil and not
a piece of glass.

*What the session did is marked as the sheet marks it.* One bar of the change's colour for each run of lines,
hung in the left margin so that the code keeps the column's edge, the tabs' edge and the caption's. A line that
was taken out stands where it stood, in the second ink on the red wash the sheet uses. The page's head is the
caption an edit has in the transcript: the name, where it is, the counts.

*A line's number is not on the page until the line is pointed at*, and then it is that line's alone, at the
line's end. There is no gutter, so there is no column of sixty numbers beside sixty lines. A press on a line
points at it: the line takes the selected fill, keeps its number, and the composer gains a chip naming it
(`keys.ts:35`), so the next message is about that line.

*Where the changes are in a file longer than the window* is drawn beside the column, in the room between it and
the side column: the same bars at the file's scale, and a soft shape for the part that is in the window. A bar is
pressed to go to its change. A file that is new from end to end, or all in the window, has none.

*Beside the session it came from* is meant literally. A file's page is its session's page with the file where the
transcript was. The side column is the session's and has not moved; in its Changes the file on screen is the
current line, and another line shows another file in the same tab. The pane keeps the session's row current. The
composer is the session's: at rest one line (48 px), so the file keeps the height, and whole again while the
caret is in it, while it holds words, or while its menu is open. If the session waits on the person, the composer
is the answer, whole, as on the session's own page: reading the file of a session that wants to push, Approve is
under the file.

### 18.5 The file page's forks

**A card of glass, against on the field** (the "File: code" switch; `code=field`). As first built this fork read

**Numbers in a gutter, against on the pointed line only** (the "File: line numbers" switch;

most lines are cut. In the file, a change costs one line of height where it happened and nothing in width. Side by side
would be the better form for a rewrite read on a wide window with the side column closed.

### 18.10 The owner's correction: the file is a card

Added 2026-11-14, after the owner saw 18.4. Their words: "good -- for file editor, let's just make it look more
like a file -- currently it's only floating texts. perhaps put the on the glass so that it's clear file card".
They were looking at the file read on the field: a caption, then code lying on the warm ground with a green bar
beside it and the drawing showing through under its last lines. 18.4 argued that a file needs no edge because it
has nothing to be told apart from. The owner's eye says otherwise, and it is right about what was lost: with no
edge, no numbers and no end, nothing on the page said "this is a file" except the tab. So the file is a card of
glass now, and that is the Code switch's first position; the file on the field is its second (`code=field`), as
it stood. The sheet of 18.5, which had the same glass and none of what follows, is gone from the switch.

**What makes it a file and not a tinted area behind text.** The earlier sheet failed at exactly this: its caption
stood outside it, it ran out under the composer with no bottom, and its lines had no numbers.

- *All four edges are in the window.* The card stands still between the tabs and the composer, 12 px from each,
  and the code moves inside it. The page itself does not scroll. A file shorter than the room is a card as tall as
  the file, with the field under it; a longer one fills the room.

## 19. Colour for code

Added 2026-11-14, asked for after the file became a card (18.10): the card and the transcript's sheet are one
family, so one palette serves both, and the file on the field too. The owner's wish for it: warm, refined, quiet.
The page already spends three colours on meanings (green for a line added, red for a line taken out, orange for
"waiting on you"), so a palette for code has to keep clear of reading as any of them.

### 19.1 The palette

Five kinds of token, three hues, and the ink.

| token | what it covers | by day | at night |
|---|---|---|---|
| the language's words | `import`, `export`, `const`, `function`, `return`, `await`, `if`... | a plum, `#6a4380` | `#dcc4ea` |
| values | strings, regular expressions, numbers, `true`, `null`, constants written in capitals | a bronze, `#6f5210` | a straw, `#e6d39c` |
| type names | names that begin with a capital, and `string`, `number`, `boolean`... | a slate, `#375680` | `#bed1f2` |
| comments | | the quiet ink | the quiet ink |
| everything else | names, calls, members, punctuation | the ink | the ink |

Why these. Each hue is taken from a part of the wheel the page does not use: the plum sits past the red towards
blue, the slate is blue, and the bronze is the yellow side of brown, well short of the mark's orange and deep
enough by day to read as a brown ink, not as a colour of state. None is green. All three are low in chroma and
close to the ink in lightness (by day they are dark inks with a temperature; at night they are pale tints of it),
so a screen of code reads as one text with three quiet voices in it and not as a palette laid over the page. The
warmest of the three goes to what there is most of in the fixtures' code after names: values. Names, calls and
punctuation are the ink, so more than half of every line is not coloured at all, and that is what keeps it quiet.

A line taken out keeps its colours (its plain words are the second ink, as before): what was removed is still
code to be compared with what replaced it. The change bars, the wash and the counts are the only green and red.

The switch "Code: colour" has Plain ink as its second position (`syntax=ink`): every token in its line's ink, as
18.10 was drawn.

### 19.2 Where it had to be tuned

- **On the field, by day.** The field is a shade darker than a glass body and darker again over the shaded side
  of the picture's paper: there a value read 4.11 and a keyword 4.54. On the field by day the three take a deeper
  step (`#54316a`, `#563f00`, `#2a456c`), as the counts do there (section 17). On glass and at night there is one
  set.
- **The pointed line, at night.** Its fill was white at 15 %, lighter than the card, and pale text loses to a
  lighter ground: a comment read 3.71 on it and a keyword 4.81. On the card at night the fill is white at 11 %
  now, and a comment on the pointed line steps up (the second ink by day, a step past it at night).

### 19.4 What the app would use, and what to measure again there

The lab's tokenizer (`syntax.tsx`) reads a line at a time and knows enough TypeScript for the fixtures: comments
on one line, quoted strings, a regular expression after an opening context, numbers, a list of keywords, and
names told apart by their case. It has no grammar: a template string with code in it, a comment that runs over
lines, JSX, a generic's angle brackets and every other language are beyond it.

The app has two real highlighters. The editor and the file viewer are CodeMirror with Lezer grammars, styled from
twelve colour tokens (keyword, string, comment, number, type, function, variable, property, operator,
punctuation, meta, invalid). The transcript's code blocks and the review's diffs are highlighted by a second
library with its own class names. Shipping this palette means:

- **Mapping twelve roles onto three hues and two inks.** Keyword to the plum; string and number to the bronze;
  type to the slate; comment to the quiet ink; function, variable, property, operator and punctuation to the ink;
  meta to the quiet ink; invalid to the red of a line taken out, which is the one place a state colour belongs in
  code. Both highlighters have to be given the same mapping, so a passage in the transcript and the same passage
  in its file are coloured alike.
- **Measuring again**, because a real grammar colours more text than this one: a decorator, a tag name, a long
  template string, a whole comment block. Each token colour against the card, against a line taken out and a line
  added if added lines ever take a wash, against the selection colour (text selection is not drawn in the lab at
  all), against the search-match colour, against the pointed line, in both schemes; on the field if the field
  form ships; and in every colouring that ships, since the glass draws its level from the colouring.
- **Languages whose weight sits elsewhere.** Markdown, JSON, shell and CSS put most of their text in what is a
  string or a property here. One hue carrying a whole file is not quiet; they need their own look at this
  palette before it is called done.

### 19.5 Not good about it

- **Three hues are few.** A call and a plain name are the same ink, and so are a property and a variable: a
  reader used to a fuller palette will miss them. It is what keeps the page quiet, and it is the first thing to
  give if the owner wants code to say more.
- **The bronze and the plum are close to the ink by day.** At 12 px on warm glass they separate from it by hue
  more than by lightness; on a poor display they will read as one dark ink with comments in grey.
- **At night all three are pale.** They have to be, to hold their floor on the pointed line, so they sit near the
  ink in lightness and differ by tint only.
- **The slate is the one cool colour on a warm page.** It was kept because types need to be told from values and
  a second warm hue would have had to be the orange or the red. Under Blue hour it is no longer an accent at all.
- **The lab's tokenizer is naive** (19.4); what it calls a type is anything that begins with a capital.
- **Hover is not measured.** A line under the pointer takes a fill of the ink at 5 % by day, a little darker than
  the card; the readings above have about one point of room for it and it was not read.

### 19.6 What changed in the code

`syntax.tsx` (new): the tokenizer and `Tokens`. `File.tsx` and `Transcript.tsx`: a line of code is its tokens (in
the sheet they are inside one span, so the spaces between them are kept). `layers.css`: section 19's block: the
three hues in both schemes, the deeper step on the field by day, the pointed line's fill on the card at night.
`Layers.tsx`, `meta.ts`: the "Code: colour" switch (`syntax=ink`).

## 20. More tabs than fit

Added 2026-11-14. Until now a fifth tab shortened every title to an ellipsis after two or three letters (17.7),
and tabs did not close. The row had one rule, the column rule of 13.4: it starts on the reading column's left
edge and ends a seam short of the waiting capsule. That rule stands untouched, so the row's room is fixed by the
column, and the question is what the tabs do inside it as they are added.

### 20.1 The rule

**The row's room decides. A tab gives up its title only when the row is full, and the row never gives up a tab
that waits for you.**

As first built, the tabs other than the current one gave their titles up all together, the moment one of them
would have been shorter than 44 px. That kept the row even, and left it standing a third empty with every name
hidden. Put to the owner, their answer was "Keep names longer": tabs keep short names for as long as the row has
room and become marks only when the row is full, and the cost is accepted that names are cut to a few letters
before they go. So evenness gave way to names, and the steps are these:

1. **The tab on screen keeps its whole title, always.**
2. **The others shorten together**, as far as a few letters (26 px of title, three or four letters and a fade;
   there is no ellipsis).
3. **When even that does not fit, tabs give up their names one at a time, from the end of the row**, each only
   when the row is full, and stand as their marks alone, 30 px round: the state's glyph for a session (the
   orange disc, the solid dot, the breathing dot), a ring for a session at rest, the file mark for a file. The
   names that are left take the room a mark frees, so no room stands unused while a name is hidden. A mark's name
   is under the pointer, sooner than other tooltips arrive.
4. **When every other tab is a mark and the row is still full, tabs leave from the end of the row** for a menu
   behind a chevron beside the plus, which lists them by mark and whole title. A tab that waits for the person
   never leaves: the row gives up its neighbours first. Choosing one in the menu makes it the tab on screen.

**Why from the end.** A row in which some tabs have names and some do not can read as damage; the question was
which mix reads least so. From the end, the row thins out the way one title does: whole at its start, cut short,
then gone. Names stand together on the left, marks together on the right, and the menu and the plus continue in
the same direction, so the whole row is one gradient and not a scatter. The order is also fixed: it depends on
where a tab stands, never on which tab is chosen. Choosing another tab moves the boundary by at most the room its
title needs; it does not pick different tabs to keep. Ordering by what a tab is (waiting first, or files first,
since every file has the same mark) was weighed and refused: the names kept would then be scattered along the
row, which is the damage the first build avoided.

**Closing.** Tabs close: the mark of a tab gives way to a cross under the pointer, as it always did, and the
cross works. Closing the tab on screen shows its neighbour (the next, or the one before the last); closing the
last one leaves New chat. A tab that is only its mark is pressed to open it and is closed once it is open; a
session that was closed opens again from its row in the pane.

### 20.2 What it comes to

The room is the reading column less the waiting capsule and its seam: 458 px at 1280 by 800 with both panes open,
and 630 px wherever the column reaches its 744 (1280 with the right column closed, and 1440 and 1728 either way,
since the column rule holds the row to the column and not to the window). With "Rate limiter rewrite" on screen:

| tabs open | 458 px: 1280, both panes | 630 px: 1280 with the right column closed; 1440; 1728 |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | every tab named, the three others shortened | every tab named, whole |
| 6 | three names, two marks | every tab named, shortened |
| 9 | the current tab and eight marks | five names, three marks |
| 14 | the current tab, seven marks, and six in the menu | the current tab and thirteen marks |

A shorter title on screen leaves a little more, a longer one a little less; the row decides from the widths it
draws (`fitTabs` in `Session.tsx`, fed by a hidden element that is laid from the row's left limit to its right,
so every rule that moves a limit is already in it).

Held, at every count and size looked at: the disc of a waiting tab is in the row; the tab on screen has its whole
title; the row starts on the column's edge and is as wide as its room allows and no wider; and between the row
and the waiting capsule there is the seam or more. The runtime's own check, with `dev=1`, raised nothing at 1280
and 1440 with 4, 6, 9 and 14 tabs, with the right column open and closed (the overlap that 15.1 tuned for cannot
return: the row cannot be wider than its room).

### 20.5 Not good about it

- **A name of three letters says little.** "buc", "Fix", "Off": at the floor a name tells its tab from its

- **The row is two things side by side when it is full**: names, then marks. It reads as thinning out, but a
  person's own order of tabs decides which keep their names, and the newest tabs, at the end, lose theirs first.
  Those are often the ones in use.
- **Marks do not say which.** Two files are the same mark; two working sessions are the same breathing dot. The
  name is a hover away and the pane lists every session by name. A file's mark could carry its first letter; not
  drawn.
- **Choosing another tab still reflows the row**: the tab on screen takes its whole title and the one that was
  gives its up, and the boundary between names and marks can move by a tab.
- **1728 gains nothing over 1440.** The row is held to the column by the column rule, so a wide window has the
  same room for tabs and more empty field beside them.
- **The steps cut, they do not move.** A title shortening, a tab becoming its mark, a tab leaving for the menu:
  none is animated, and none was watched.
- **Not drawn:** more waiting tabs than the row can hold as marks (the rule keeps them all, so the row would
  then pass its room); dragging a tab; a keyboard path along the row; the menu when it is longer than the
  window (it scrolls inside itself, not seen); reopening a closed file other than through its session.
- **On the Overview, Artifacts and Customize no tab is current**, so every tab shortens and the row has no whole
  title in it.

## 21. Artifacts and Customize

### 21.1 One grammar for both, and it is the transcript's

- **Built in.** The app's mods, the same line. Opened: the choice for the session that is running (as
  everywhere, on, off).
- **Skills.** Your own. A plugin's skills are inside the plugin, and the search finds a plugin by its skills.
- **Connectors.** One list, not two. A line is the name; its mark is its state only when the state is news:
  connecting (the breathing dot), failed (the warning mark, with Try again at the line's end), and sign-in
  needed, which is the orange disc with a key, with Sign in on the line itself: the person's move, answered where
  it is. Opened: its address, how it is reached, where it is configured, and its tools when a session has them.
- **Whose configuration** is a chip beside the search (yours, or a project's), as the folder chip is on New chat.
- **Discover** is the round button: the catalog's search and what its sources put first, each with a mark if it
  was reviewed and a mark if it runs code, and a plus that adds it. One more row leads to the whole catalog.

### 21.4 Forks

- **A sheet in place, against a page of its own.** The app opens a plugin's detail as a page with a way back. In
  place keeps the list in view and is the transcript's own move; a page of its own would be right only for
  something as large as the catalog. Not a switch.
- **Artifacts by date, against by Space** ("Artifacts: grouped", `shelf=space`; also in the page's own menu). By
  date is what a person comes for most: the thing just made. By Space is the app's other view, kept whole.
- **A plugin's skills inside it, against listed under Skills too** ("Customize: a plugin's skills",
  `skills=listed`). Listed, the page is ten lines longer and every plugin is said twice; inside, a skill is found
  by searching. The app lists them all.
- **The + card as a button until pressed, against open at rest** (`add=open`). Open, the Artifacts page has the
  app's "Make something new" always in view; as a button the page is its list. It is open on an empty page
  either way.
- **One column for Customize, against three sections.** Three tabs make the person choose a kind before seeing
  anything; six plugins, three mods, two skills and five connectors are sixteen lines and fit one screen. It
  stops being right at several dozen lines (21.7). Not a switch: the alternative is another page structure.
- **Artifacts as lines, against tiles.** Tiles are worth their room when they show the thing. The lab has no
  previews, and twenty tiles of glass holding a title would spend the page's material on nothing. Not built.

### 21.7 Not good about it

- **Opening an artifact is two presses** where the app's is one: the line opens the sheet, and Open opens the
  thing. The sheet is where everything about it is, so the line had to open the sheet; but the commonest act got
  longer. An arrow at the line's end under the pointer, as the waiting list has, would give the one press back.
  Not drawn.
- **Nothing shows what an artifact looks like.** No preview in the line, the sheet or anywhere. The fixtures have
  none; the sheet is where one belongs.
- **Seven kind glyphs have to be learned.** Their names are in the sheet, the menu and the + card, never beside
  the title.
- **A name alone can say too little.** "ptc" at rest says nothing to someone who did not install it; what it does
  is a pointer away, and on a touch screen or by keyboard it is only in the sheet.
- **No mark means fine.** A connected connector and a working plugin are told apart from a broken one by the
  absence of a mark. It has to be trusted, and a state that fails to load would look healthy.
- **A second disc.** The key in the orange disc is the person's move, as the orange means; but the waiting
  capsule counts sessions and does not count it, so this one is only seen by coming to the page.
- **One column at this size only.** Sixteen lines. A project with forty plugins and sixty skills would want the
  heads to fold, or to be jumped between; not drawn.
- **Sheets only.** The whole catalog, adding a connector, the sign-in's steps, a Space's menu and every dialog
  are named in 21.5 and not drawn.
- **With the + card open at 1280 by 800 the column is 572 px and the tabs fold** (section 20); the page is drawn
  for the card to be opened and closed, not left open there.
- **Motion was not watched**: a line opening, the card growing, a chip arriving.
- **Measured under Warm paper only.**

## To ship this in the app

What the real renderer would need, against "The app today" in the brief.

- **A plane under everything.** Today the window, toolbar and sidebar paint one flat ground and the window has an
  opaque background colour. This needs one canvas behind the shell painted from the theme (ground candidate,
  strength), and every region above it transparent. No window transparency or vibrancy is involved: the field is
  the app's own.
- **Two glass roots.** One for the shell (pane, bars, composer, modules), one attached to the transcript's
  scrolling content. The transcript is a virtualised list with its own hand-built stylesheet; glass can be
  registered only for rows in view, and each tool, edit and command row needs the "element in flow, surface
  beneath" arrangement of `glass.tsx`, fed by the virtualiser's measured rows instead of a `ResizeObserver` per
  block. This is the largest piece of work and the riskiest: the open questions are a transcript of hundreds of
  items under load, rows entering during a fast scroll, and streaming rows that grow every frame.
- **Shell geometry.** The side column is one raised rounded card today and the sidebar's width lives in
  TypeScript; the column becomes separate surfaces stacked with a gap, the 44 px toolbar goes, the tabs become
  fills in one capsule and the traffic lights sit on the pane at about 24, 22. Every resize and collapse has to
  tell the roots to re-measure.
- **Tokens.** New roles: the field's parameters, ink at four strengths, two fill sets (on the field, on glass),
  one status accent with a deeper mark step for light, shadow recipes per span, a radius ladder (12 / 18 / 20 /
  22 / 26 and capsules) in place of 6 px on nearly everything, and motion tokens, of which there are none today.
  The colour-literal guard would need to learn radii, shadows and durations.
- **A material setting.** Ship Blocks as a setting (Glass, Veil, Opaque), defaulting to Opaque wherever WebGPU is
  missing, Reduce Transparency is on, or a session is long. The same markup serves all three.
- **Things that draw their own colours** have to follow the ink pole and be measured on glass: syntax colours in
  code blocks and diffs (the transcript's highlighter brings its own themes), the terminal, charts. Here only two
  diff hues do.
- **Native views.** The browser tool's pages are native views composited above the page; they cannot sit under
  the pane, a module or a platter, so they stay inside the main space and the existing cover-with-a-picture path
  is used when a platter opens over them.
- **Menus and popovers** get one shared primitive with entry and exit motion (today each call site styles its own
  and none animates), drawn as the CSS platter or as library glass, chosen once.
- **Fonts.** Figtree and Geist Mono bundled as local variable files; remote fonts cannot load. Korean falls to the
  system face.
- **A colouring is one record, and Paper is the one to ship.** The theme carries what `colour.ts` carries: a
  field, the ink, the shadows' tint, the accent with the ink on it and its stroke, focus, the mark's form, and
  the ground. Under Paper the app's grounds become `#e8e1db` and `#181614` (today `#faf9f6` and `#131313`), the
  accent becomes the ink, and a waiting row carries a badge. The terracotta goes with the orange.
- **The picture is code, not an asset.** `paper.ts` paints it at the window's size and pixel ratio on every
  resize and scheme change (a few hundred canvas operations, several of them blurred; measure it on a resize drag,
  and paint to a smaller canvas and scale if it costs a frame). Its composition is tied to the shell's geometry
  (the fold under the composer, the leaf behind the side column), so the two change together.
- **The serif**, if the owner keeps it: Newsreader's latin optical-size file bundled locally, used for the
  new-chat line and nothing else, with the system serif behind it for Korean.
- **The clearer glass**, if it is wanted for Paper later, needs `@vitreajs/vitrea-web` as a direct dependency:
  the React package names the material documents and exports none of them.
- **The five calls, once answered** (section 13). An out-of-focus form needs the field kept as layers (the room, the
  form, the form out of focus), painted at half size and composited on a change of scene, never per frame at rest.
  The mark as a disc is a token pair (`pin`, `on-pin`) and a clear capsule; nothing of it is a glass tint. The
  column rule is three variables (the column, the capsule's right edge, the tabs' right limit) fed by the derived
  room and seam, with the capsule in a group of its own. The grown card is one element in flow whose box
  transitions, with its height fed by its content's measured height and capped at the window's.
- **The plain ground, if it is chosen** (section 16). The field becomes a quarter-size canvas of pools placed from
  the shell's own boxes (pane, side surface, composer, bars), repainted when one of them moves; the picture, its
  composition and its out-of-focus layers go. Each transcript row that stands on glass carries the travelling pool
  as a second shadow. At night the pools are kept out from under the glass.
- **A warmer Paper, if one is chosen** (section 14). It is a light record beside the colouring (the values the
  painter reads) and, for two of them, a pigment for the mark; the night values of the sheet's face and edge
  (0.36, 0.66) are Paper's as well.
- **The Overview** (section 18). The board is a list, not a grid of cards: one row component fed by the same
  derivation the board has today (waiting first, then unread, working, the rest), with the waiting group moved out
  of the list into one glass surface in the shell's root, in the side column's group. That surface holds the
  request card's own controls through the slot the cards use now, one open at a time. The board's keyboard model
  (one tab stop, arrows across cards) becomes arrows down a list. The toolbar's "needs you" count is not drawn
  while the Overview is open.
- **A file's page** (section 18, as corrected in 18.10). The viewer and the diff tab become one surface: a glass
  card registered in the shell's root (it stands still, so it needs no root inside a scroller), with the file's
  name as its top, a scroller inside it holding the file with its changes, a gutter of line numbers, change bars
  between the gutter and the code, and a rail at its right edge fed by the diff's hunks. The editor's own gutter, its themes and its merge view have to be brought
  to the same margin and the same two hues, and its syntax colours measured on the field. The session's composer
  is mounted on the file's page with a collapsed state, and an anchored line becomes a chip in it; the side
  column is kept mounted across a session and its files so that it does not leave and return.
- **Colour for code** (section 19). Three hues and two inks as theme tokens, with a deeper day step for code on the
  field if that form ships; both of the app's highlighters mapped onto them (19.4), and every token measured
  again against each ground code can stand on, in every colouring that ships.
- **The tab row** (section 20). The row's steps are decided from measured widths against a room that the layout
  already knows; in the app the room is the strip's own slot. Tabs keep a mark for every kind of thing that can
  be a tab (a session's state, a file, a tool), since the mark is what survives. Closing, the overflow menu and
  a keyboard path along the row belong to the same piece of work.
- **Artifacts and Customize** (section 21). Both become one list component: a line whose press opens a sheet in
  place, registered as a glass block as a tool run's is, one open at a time. The library's row, its menu and its
  header controls move into the line, the sheet and one menu; the "make" strip and each Discover view move behind
  the round button, which is the side column's own growing surface. Customize's three tabs become one page that
  reads the place's running session without being told which. Each cut in 21.5 is the owner's to confirm first.
- **Measurement in CI.** `measure.mjs` should run against real content in both schemes before a field or a ground
  is changed: on this design a palette change is a legibility change.
