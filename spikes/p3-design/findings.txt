(c) THE MODEL: GEOMETRY AS PLAIN KEYS ON C'S SLIDE NODE

Proved on C's model and A's real store (spikes/p3-design/model/, 32 tests; fixture boards b1 {0,0,390,844}, b2
{500,0,1440,1024}, b3 {2000,0,1080,8192}, keys written by Y.setSlideKey):
 1. Convergence (geometry.test.ts): A writes b1.x and B writes b1.w concurrently: both kept, states equal. Both write b1.x:
    one value, the higher clientID's, on every replica and in both delivery orders. Each key is its own attribute of the
    slide node (yjs/slides.ts:136-138).
 2. ⌘Z alone: a four-frame drag under DeckUndo's origin, cut before and after, is one undo step; a remote write landing
    mid-drag (b1.w, b2.x) is not undone; a board move and an element restyle in one stack undo separately, move first;
    redo restores the move (slides-undo.ts:37, :53). Observed, C's rule as for element styles: a remote x written after
    the owner's drag survives the owner's undo of the drag while y goes back.
 3. History: after a move, deckChanges lists exactly b2 `changed` with keys x and y, author and minute, count 1; a
    three-update drag reads as one change credited to its last frame; a resize lists w and h (history.ts:192, :203:
    every slide key but elements and notes is the slide's own).
 4. Restore (restore.test.ts, A's DocumentService and ArtifactStore with C's adapter): a version, then a drag, a resize,
    an agent restyle and a reply; store.restore brings b1's x, y, w, h and the element back, deckFiles equals the
    version's, and the thread's later message stays (adapter.ts:170-181, revertScope keeping conversations only). A
    board deleted since the version comes back with its geometry.
 5. C's paths leave the keys: a heavy agent publishDeck of the board (restyle, text, an element added and one removed),
    a forced publish over a conflicting restyle, a held board file then fixed, and repair: x, y, w, h untouched every time
    (publish.ts:96, :110, :668-681 compare and write only style:/attr: slide keys; :961 writes only order; repair writes
    order and parent only, model.ts:560-599). deckFiles carries no geometry (model.ts:234-248): the design's adapter
    writes it into canvas.json. Observers: a geometry write reaches observeSlide as {kind:'slide', keys:['x','y']} and
    touchedSlides answers the board, so the host's repair is scoped to it (adapter.ts:160-163).
 Two things E1 owns: C's duplicateSlide drops x, y, w, h (and a `title` key) and keeps the element ids (writers.ts:50-54,
 model.ts:115-118, 133-136, 195-201), while §1 wants a duplicated board to have new element ids and new geometry: E1 writes
 its own duplicateBoard. And a board created by its file alone (or held when new, publish.ts:637) has no geometry: the
 manifest gives it, else §1's default placement.
 The default stands; no reason for a canvas root was found.

The cross-board move (move.ts, a prototype of §1 "Identity" in one transaction: the subtree read with its text, deleted
from the source, created at the destination's top level, every thread anchored to a moved element rewritten):
 - No collision: the id kept, the thread follows ({b2, e1}, live, quote kept).
 - Collision: the moved element (and any colliding descendant) takes elementMinter's id; the threads are rewritten in the
   same update (one update); two ⌘Z restore both boards deep-equal and the anchors (the undo scope holds threads).
 - The race the acceptance names, under XE4's keep-id rule: A moves e1 from b1 to b2 (b2 lacks it at A's state) while B
   creates e1 in b2 (Y.createElement, and as an agent publishDeck of b2 adding <p id="e1">). Both replicas converge with
   one e1 in b2 and no orphan, but one element is silently lost: with A's clientID higher, the agent's published element
   is gone; with B's higher, the moved element's content is gone from every board and the thread {b2, e1}, live, now
   quotes the agent's element. A container moved this way loses itself and re-parents its children. Cause: both are
   sets of one key of b2's `elements` node (yjs/slides.ts:184-189; publish.ts:817, 826 create by key), last writer wins.
 - Always minting a new id (clientID-hashed, elementMinter): both elements survive in both orders, the thread follows the
   moved one. Its one cost: two replicas moving the same element into the same board at once leave two copies (the
   thread on one); keep-id leaves one. A derived id (a function of source board, destination board and id alone, no
   clientID) survived both races in the tests: one copy on a double move, both elements on an insertion race.
 Recommendation: XE4's "keeps its id unless the destination holds it" becomes "always takes a new id": the derived id
 (8+ base36 characters, falling back to elementMinter on a collision with the destination at the mover's state) is the
 best of the three; plain minting is acceptable if E1 prefers C's minter. Losing content silently is worse than keeping
 two copies of one element, and the kept id buys nothing once the threads are rewritten in the move's transaction (the
 agent's next read sees the move either way). Side note: elementMinter's suffix is hash4, four base36 characters
 (parse.ts:77-84, ~1.7 M values), so two replicas' minted ids differ only probabilistically; a longer suffix is safer if
 moves mint.


(d) AN AGENT PUBLISH WHILE THE OWNER DRAGS

Prototype publishCanvas(live, base, sent, force) (model/publish-canvas.ts): board files renamed onto C's paths and
published by C's publishDeck; canvas.json compared three-way per key (deck keys; each board's x, y, w, h, title; order as
one key) with the details `board <id>: <key> changed since your read`, `board <id>: removed since your read`, `order:
changed since your read`, `canvas.json key <k>: changed since your read`; any conflict refuses the whole publish unless
force; one transaction. On A's real store (publish-canvas.test.ts, service.writeAs, each drag frame the owner's own
write): the agent reads, the owner drags b3 for 10 frames, the agent publishes canvas.json (b1 moved, b2 resized, b3 as
read) and the files of b1 and b2, the owner drags 10 frames more: no conflict, changed [b1, b2, canvas]; host and owner
both end with b3 at the owner's last position {2200,100}, b1 {50,60}, b2 1200×800, the agent's restyle and text in
place; the owner's one ⌘Z returns b3 to {2000,0} and leaves the agent's b1. Variant: the agent also moves b3: refused
with exactly `board b3: x changed since your read` and `board b3: y changed since your read`, nothing written; forced,
the agent wins exactly x and y while a key it sent equal to its base (b1.w, changed by the owner) is left alone.

(g) THE SIZE CHROMIUM DRAWS AND EDITS: THE NUMBERS BEHIND THE CAP

Each board drawn alone in the world layer (pass 1, test "(g) …"; markers 1/8 of the shorter side in each corner and at
the centre): laid out at its CSS size (offsetWidth and offsetHeight exact); at 100 % with its far corner brought under a
point, the bottom-right marker's pixel right and hit-tested as that element, the top-left right; at the fit zoom, the
centre and bottom-right markers right; the renderer's working set after each:
  8,192² (67 Mpx), 12,288×1080, 16,384×4,096 (67 Mpx), 16,384² (268 Mpx), 1080×24,576, 32,768×1080, 1080×32,768,
  23,170² (537 Mpx), 32,768² (1,074 Mpx), 65,536×1080, 1080×65,536: all right at 100 % and at fit (the last's
  bottom-right marker 1.9 screen px at fit, blended at its edge). Laid out in 23 to 55 ms; renderer 204 to 207 MB and GPU
  87 MB whatever the size (Chromium tiles: only the visible tiles are rastered).
Editing: in C's editor, the far-corner markers of 8,192², 16,384², 1080×32,768 and 65,536×1080 boards selected and
dragged exactly as wanted ((a) above).

So neither candidate is an editor limit. What does bind, by output:
  - a native PDF page: 19,200 px a side (the readers' 14,400 pt), Chromium itself none to 65,536;
  - a picture: MAWS's 8,192 a side (policy); Chromium's capture window whole to 16,384 a side at any area tried (144 Mpx)
    and silently cut at 16,384 past it (the GPU's largest texture on Apple silicon); and a native bitmap's memory, 4
    bytes a pixel in main (67 Mpx = 256 MiB; 268 Mpx = 1 GiB);
  - the same 16,384 texture for any surface Chromium must hold whole (a filter or opacity group over a whole board, a
    cached picture of a board for a level-of-detail view): content layers tile past it, such surfaces do not.
Recommended cap for the owner's ruling (Decision Log 5): 16,384 px per side and 2^26 px (67,108,864, written "64 Mi px",
8,192²) of area. Every board inside it prints as one native PDF page in every reader, pictures natively when ≤ 8,192 a
side and at a stated scale otherwise (and could picture natively whole should the policy ever rise to 16,384), and keeps
a whole-board surface inside one texture; the area bound keeps a native bitmap at 256 MiB. E1's bench boards (4,096×8,192,
1080×8,192) and the posters of the parent's acceptance (taller than 8,192) are inside it with room (1080 × 16,384 is
17.7 Mpx). A board outside it is refused at creation or publish naming the board and the limit (§1 "Limits").
