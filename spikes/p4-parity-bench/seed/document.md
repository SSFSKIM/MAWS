# Riverside Community Garden: Spring Season Plan

Welcome to the spring season at Riverside Community Garden. This plan gathers what the steering group agreed over the winter so that every member, new or returning, can see how the season will run before the first seeds go in. It covers the opening schedule, the work the first weeks need, who looks after which part of the garden, and how decisions get made when something changes. Nothing here is fixed for good: the plan is a starting point that the members' meeting can amend at any time, and the steering group will keep this page current as the season moves along. If you have questions, the volunteer coordinator is the first person to ask, and every open question goes on the agenda of the next members' meeting.

## Schedule

The garden opens for the season on the first Saturday of April, weather permitting, with a short welcome at the tool shed at ten in the morning. Regular work sessions run on Thursday evenings from five thirty until dusk, when the steering group is on site to hand out tools, answer questions and sign off plots that are ready for planting. Weekend mornings are open to everyone with a plot, and the water stays on from sunrise to sunset once the last frost has passed. The compost bays are turned on the second Sunday of each month, and the shared beds are weeded by whoever is on the rota that week.

During the first month the gate code changes every two weeks, and the new code is posted on the noticeboard inside the shed rather than sent by message. Members who cannot make the regular sessions can arrange a time with the volunteer coordinator, who keeps a spare key for that purpose. School groups visit on weekday mornings by appointment only, and the steering group asks members to leave the front path clear on those days so that the children can reach the pond safely.

The season ends with the harvest supper on the last Saturday of October. The final work session of the year is the one before the supper, when the shared beds are cleared, the water is turned off, the hoses are drained and stored, and the tool inventory is checked against the list from the spring. Plots that will be renewed for next year should be mulched before the end of November.

## Tasks for the opening weeks

The steering group has listed the jobs that need doing before planting can start in earnest. Each job has a lead who will organise the work and call for help on the noticeboard; anyone with an hour to spare is welcome to join.

- [ ] Repair the north fence where the winter storm loosened three posts
- [ ] Rebuild the raised bed by the pond with untreated timber
- [ ] Replace the cracked water butt behind the tool shed
- [ ] Sort the seed library and throw out packets older than three years
- [ ] Paint the plot numbers on the new marker stakes

Larger jobs, such as relaying the gravel path or extending the polytunnel, will be discussed at the April members' meeting once the costs are known. The treasurer has set aside a small budget for materials, and receipts should be handed to her within a month of purchase so that the accounts stay tidy.

## New members

Twelve new households join the garden this spring, most of them from the waiting list that opened last summer. Each new member is paired with an experienced plot holder for the first two months, who shows them where the tools and the water points are, explains the rota and helps them plan a first planting that suits the soil and the light of their plot. The pairs are listed on the noticeboard, and the coordinator matches anyone who arrives later in the season.

New plots are smaller than the established ones, so that nobody takes on more than they can keep up with in a first year. A member who wants a larger plot can ask for one at the end of the season, when plots that are not renewed become free. Seeds from the library, compost from the bays and the use of every shared tool are included in the membership fee; the only things members buy for themselves are gloves, plants they want that the library does not hold, and anything that stays on their own plot.

The garden runs two short workshops for new members in April: one on preparing beds and sowing, and one on watering, mulching and keeping slugs at bay without chemicals. Both take place in the polytunnel and last about an hour. Returning members are welcome to sit in and share what has worked for them, and the notes from both workshops go into the seed library binder for anyone who missed them.

## Decisions and changes

Day-to-day decisions are made by the owner of the area concerned. Anything that affects more than one area, costs more than fifty pounds or changes the rules for plot holders goes to the members' meeting, which is held on the first Monday of each month in the community hall. The steering group prepares the agenda and circulates it a week ahead; any member may add an item by telling the coordinator before the agenda goes out.

Minutes are kept by the secretary and posted on the noticeboard within a week. The full rules of the garden, including the plot agreement and the code of conduct, are in the [member handbook](https://example.org/riverside-garden/handbook), which every new member signs when they take on a plot. If the plan and the handbook ever disagree, the handbook wins until the members' meeting resolves the difference.

Finally, a word on safety. Tools go back in the shed at the end of every session, children on site are always accompanied, and anyone who sees a hazard, from a broken step to a wasp nest, reports it to the coordinator the same day. The first aid kit hangs inside the shed door, and the nearest defibrillator is at the community hall across the road.
