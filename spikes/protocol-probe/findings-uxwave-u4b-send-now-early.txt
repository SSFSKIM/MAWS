UX wave U4b: Send now on an after-the-turn row early in a shell call, written as a promote at `next` followed at once by
`interrupt {send_now: true, message_uuid}` for that message, on engine 2.1.322, on MAWS's own wire and environment (U4b's
execution document, docs/doperpowers/plans/2026-11-15-ux-wave-U4b-composer-integrations.md on ux-wave/U4b, "Probes" and
"Reported to the architect" 8; the earlier passes findings-uxwave-u4-send-now.txt and -cases.txt; recorded 2026-11-15)

Question. A turn runs a foreground shell command; the app writes a user frame at priority `next` for a message M (as
queue.ts promote() writes it with priority next) and, at once after it, the request naming M. (i) Does the request wait
for the command to become a task and move it, and is M moved in, in every run of five, at 1.5 s into the call (not yet a
task) and at 10 s (long a task)? When is M read relative to the command's end? (ii) With another next-step message P
already waiting, are P and M joined into one delivery, and in which order? (iii) What is answered when the request comes
before the engine has queued M: the two writes with no gap (the pair as built), and the request written first? (iv) What
is answered when M has already been read?

Verdict.
(i) Yes, 10/10 (pair 5/5 at 1.5 s, pair-late 5/5 at 10 s): `{still_queued: [M], send_now: "delivering"}` 4 to 12 ms after
    the request, the command moved and kept running, M read about 26 s (early) or 19 s (late) before the command's own
    end. The engine queued M before it answered in every run (M's `queued` lifecycle precedes the answer). What follows
    the move depends on the timing, and the late timing differs from the recorded engine fact:
    - At 1.5 s (5/5), the answer comes before the call is a task. The engine waits: the call's task_started at 2.94 to
      2.97 s into the call, the move (task_updated is_backgrounded) 0.16 to 0.20 s after it, so 1.61 to 1.63 s after
      the answer. Then, 0.84 to 0.88 s after the move, the cut: the "moved ... so that a message that arrived ... can
      reach you" tool result (backgroundedToDeliverMessage true), result success / aborted_tools, the prompt
      `cancelled`, and M `started` 7 to 17 ms after that result, as the next turn (3.98 to 4.02 s into the call). As
      u4p2-a-* recorded for a message queued 0.5 s before the request.
    - At 10 s (5/5), the call long a task: moved 3 to 6 ms after the answer; 0.89 to 1.00 s later the "moved" tool
      result, and M is read IN THE SAME TURN: no `result` between, M's replay and `started` right after the tool
      result, the status `requesting` frame naming both the prompt and M, the turn's one result `completed` after M's
      lifecycle `completed`, the prompt `completed` (not `cancelled`). In the transcript file M is a queue `remove` and
      a `queued_command` attachment, with no user line under M's uuid.
    So the pair can be relied on to deliver M now at both timings, with the running command moved, never killed. What
    cannot be relied on is how the turn reads afterwards: a cut turn and M as a turn of its own early, M folded into
    the running turn late (see "Where the engine differs").
(ii) Joined only early. P (written at `next` 0.5 s before) and M go together in 6/6 (two 3/3, two-late 3/3), P first
    then M, both `started` at consecutive seqs, whichever was named (M here). At 1.5 s (3/3) they are one user message
    under M's uuid holding P's text, a newline, then M's text (M's replay frame; P's replay holds only its own text; one
    user line in the transcript under M's uuid, none under P's), the next turn after the cut, as u4p2-two-* and
    u4p3-end*. At 10 s (3/3) they are not one message: each has its own replay with its own text, both read in the
    running turn at the tool boundary (requesting names the prompt, P and M), two `queued_command` attachments in the
    transcript and no user line under either uuid. Order P, M in every run.
(iii) No gap (the pair as built): never `nothing_waiting` (19/19 frame-first pairs across every case of this probe): the
    engine queued M 2 to 22 ms after the frame and before answering, and answered `delivering`. Request first (reqfirst 3/3):
    `{still_queued: [], send_now: "nothing_waiting"}` 3 to 5 ms after the request, written before M's frame was even
    read; M's `queued` came 2 to 4 ms after the answer; nothing was moved or cut; the command ran to its own end in the
    foreground (30 s) and M was read at that tool boundary, in the same turn, after the command's output. So the order
    of the two writes is load-bearing: the frame must go first.
(iv) After M has been read (read 3/3, three presses each): `{still_queued: [], send_now: "nothing_waiting"}` at M's
    `started` (+0 to 2 ms after it), at the first assistant frame of M's turn, and 0.5 s after M's turn's result (9/9).
    No press did anything: no move, no cut, no state change; M's turn ran to its own result.

Plainly: the pair (frame at `next`, then the request, in that order) delivers the message now in every run recorded
here (19/19 pairs whose request followed the frame, read included), and joins it with what already waits at the next
step, the waiting one first. No run said otherwise. Two things the design must take as they are: the request must not
precede the frame (reqfirst: `nothing_waiting`, the message then waits for the command's end), and the turn's shape
after the move depends on whether the call is already a task when the request arrives (cut and next turn before; same
turn, and not joined into one message, after). The design's condition "(i) holds, (ii) is yes and (iii) is never
`nothing_waiting`" holds for the order frame-then-request; (ii) is yes in delivery (both go at once, in order) but the
"as one user message" reading holds only for the early timing.

Method. Scenario scenarios/pass3/uxwave-u4b-send-now-early.mjs, on the second and third passes' launch exactly (launch

never on the MacBook; vm-check.mjs's claims held before the first probe (2.1.322, routed, built-in plugins only, no MCP

feature cache.
- Each run: the prompt "Run this exact shell command with the Bash tool, in the foreground ...: node wait.cjs <tag> 30"
  as a `now` frame; time zero is the waiter's pid file (the command running), polled every 20 ms.
- pair / pair-late: at 1.5 s / 10 s, M ("Reply with the single word PINEAPPLE, then carry on with what you were
  doing.") as `{type: "user", uuid, message, parent_tool_use_id: null, origin: {kind: "human"}, priority: "next"}`
  and `{subtype: "interrupt", send_now: true, message_uuid: M}` written in the same tick, the frame first (no gap: the
  host's promote awaits only the frame's flush before its sendNow; one tick is the tighter case).
- two / two-late: P ("... MANGO ...") at `next` 0.5 s earlier, its `queued` awaited; then the pair for M.
- reqfirst: at 1.5 s the request naming M, then M's frame, same tick.
- read: the pair at 1.5 s; then the request naming M again at M's `started`, at the first main-lane assistant frame
  after it, and 0.5 s after the following main-lane result.
- Each run ends when every message has started, the command has ended and the wire has been quiet 12 s (at most 90 s).
  The uxw_final row lists answers (with request and answer seqs), results, lifecycles, task frames, requesting frames
  and string replays; uxw_transcript reduces the engine's transcript file as the earlier passes do.

Where the engine differs from the document.
1. "Engine facts", row 1 (`interrupt { send_now: true, message_uuid }` behind a running shell command "... ends the turn
   `aborted_tools`; the message is the next turn"): only when the request arrives before the call is a task (the first
   ~3 s; u4p2-a-* and u4p1-a-* were all written then). Once the call is a task (pair-late 5/5, two-late 3/3), the command
   is moved within milliseconds and the message is read in the same turn: no `aborted_tools` result, the prompt
   `completed`, one result for the whole turn. This is a property of the request, not of the pair: nothing in the late
   runs depends on M having been written a tick before. Inference, not checked in the source: the deferred move that
   waits for the task ends the turn to deliver, the immediate one lets the message fold in at the tool boundary, as the
   first pass's background_tasks retry did (findings-uxwave-u4-send-now.txt (e)).
2. "Engine facts", row 4 ("every message waiting at the next step goes with the one named, as one user message"): all
   go, in order, at both timings; "as one user message" holds only early (two-*). Late (two-late-*) each is its own
   replay and its own queued_command attachment.
3. The document's (iii) asks whether the request can meet a message not yet queued: with the frame first, never
   (19/19); with the request first, always `nothing_waiting` (3/3), and the message then waits for the command's own
   end rather than going now.

Bearing on other parts of the spec.
- Whatever reads a Send now's end as "Ended for your message" from a cut turn (the first pass's verdict (a), Interfaces
  B of U4a) meets no cut turn when the call is already a task: the end is an ordinary `completed`, and the message's
  turn is the same turn. The rule "a row leaves at its own `started`" draws the right stack at both timings.
- An after-the-turn row sent by this pair goes together with every next-step row already waiting, P first: the child's
  cell "that message alone goes now" would become "it goes now, with what waits at the next step, after them".
- The second and third presses (read) are harmless: a double click on Send now after delivery answers
  `nothing_waiting`, which the stack shows today as "The agent has already read this message" (Reported 11).
- The moved command's completion starts an engine-own turn in every run (as the earlier passes found).

Not run. A gap between the frame and the request longer than one tick (the product's await of the frame's flush is
shorter than any timing seen here; with the frame first, the engine had already queued M by its answer in 19/19). The
request first at the late timing. A call other than a shell command. The direct route (it does not authenticate on
this VM, VM.md). Model spend: under US$0.07 over the 22 runs (haiku; the largest total_cost_usd of any run 0.0031).
